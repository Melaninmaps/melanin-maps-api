## The Hierarchy

```
Life Chapters
  ↓
Journeys
  ↓
Moments
  ↓
People · Businesses · Videos · History · Community · Kinfolk
```

"Journey" was too small a word. People don't have one journey — they have Life Chapters, each containing one or more Journeys.

---

## Life Chapters (the top-level containers)

- Moving
- Marriage
- Education
- Career
- Business
- Parenthood
- Travel
- Wellness
- Retirement
- Heritage
- Community Leadership

Kinfolk occasionally asks: **"What's happening in your life right now?"**

Not during onboarding. Not every week. Only when it makes sense, or when the user chooses to tell it.

---

## The Journey Filter (locked decision)

**"Will this matter to someone five years from now?"**

If yes → it is probably a Journey.
- Choosing a college? Yes.
- Starting a business? Yes.
- Reconnecting with heritage? Yes.
- Planning a wedding? Yes.
- Becoming a parent? Yes.
- Moving to a new city? Yes.
- Mentoring the next generation? Absolutely.

If it is a single transaction — finding a tutor for one afternoon, buying a wrench, booking a haircut — that is a service, not a Journey. It belongs as a **milestone inside a Chapter**, not a Chapter of its own.

**Why:** Single transactions don't shape a person's life. Chapters do. The distinction keeps the platform from becoming a task manager.

---

## What Is NOT a Journey

Single transactions:
- Finding a tutor
- Booking a haircut
- Finding a plumber

These are milestones. They belong inside a Chapter.

Example: the College Chapter contains finding tutors, finding housing, finding internships, finding alumni, finding scholarships. These are milestones — not separate Journeys.

---

## My Story (replaces "My Journey")

The personal record is called **My Story**, not "My Journey" and not "Saved."

When opened, it does not say "Achievements." It says:

> **"Here's the story we've been building together."**

Because it's about the user's life, not completing tasks.

---

## Journeys Don't End — They Evolve

College → Career → Mentor → Scholarship Donor → Alumni → Board Member → Speaker

One chapter becomes another. The platform remembers the full arc.

---

## The Pay It Forward Moment (the most distinctive feature in these documents)

When someone completes a chapter, Kinfolk asks:

> "Five years ago someone shared a two-minute alumni video that helped you choose Cheyney. Would you like to record a message for someone beginning the same journey?"

Or at graduation:
> "Congratulations. Four years ago you saved your first HBCU. You watched alumni stories. Visited campuses. Asked hundreds of questions. Today you graduated from Cheyney. Would you like to leave a message for the next student beginning this journey?"

This is not AI. This is legacy.

**Why this matters:** The end of every meaningful journey isn't just personal achievement — it's leaving something behind for the next person who begins. This is what completes the circle.

**How it applies across chapters:**
- Graduating → guide for future students
- Wedding complete → "Would you like to share what surprised you most?"
- Business launched → "Would you like to mentor first-time entrepreneurs?"
- Trip documented → "Would you like to recommend your favorite neighborhood?"

---

## Kinfolk Remembers Moments, Not Clicks

Not: "You viewed 27 bakeries."

Instead:
> "The first bakery you saved was Sweet Magnolia. Remember? You said you loved the smell of fresh bread. Today you're planning your wedding cake."

Memories become relationships. Businesses that were part of a chapter are remembered when they become relevant again:
> "The bakery that made your wedding cake is celebrating its tenth anniversary."

---

## Platform Success Metric (locked — use verbatim in founder documents)

> "Mapping With Melanin™ does not measure success by how often someone opens the app. It measures success by whether it helped someone grow, connect, remember, contribute, and eventually help someone else along the same path."

---

## The Circle

Most platforms celebrate **activity**.

MWM celebrates **growth**.

The full circle: learner → contributor → guide.

- Learner: someone beginning a journey
- Contributor: someone in the middle sharing what they know
- Guide: someone who has completed a chapter and chooses to walk alongside the next person

That last step — from learner to guide — is what completes the circle. It's why the alumni video matters. It's why the bakery owner tells their story. It's why the Cultural Ambassador exists.
