> Everything should ultimately trace back to one of these four pillars.

---

## The Four Foundations

### 1. Legacy
**"Why will this still matter in 25 years?"**

This governs:
- Foundations Book
- Legacy Collection
- Welcome Home Tour
- Documentary
- Coffee table books
- Living Archive
- Letters to the Future
- To the Next Steward

Nothing in Legacy is measured by today's metrics.

---

### 2. Community
**"Does this help someone feel they belong?"**

This governs:
- Businesses
- Community members
- Cultural Ambassadors
- Kinfolk Circles
- Events
- Conversations
- Mentorship
- Music
- City stories

Every community feature should answer this question.

---

### 3. Trust
**"Does this increase confidence?"**

This governs:
- Verification
- Safety
- Trust scores
- Community reputation
- Review weighting
- Moderation
- Transparency
- Business authenticity

Trust should always outweigh engagement.

---

### 4. Discovery
**"Does this help someone find something meaningful?"**

This governs:
- Search
- Recommendations
- Businesses
- Neighborhoods
- Culture
- People
- AI
- Travel
- Relocation

Discovery isn't about showing more.
It's about showing what matters.

---

## The Pillar Test

Instead of asking:

> "Should this feature exist?"

Ask:

> "Which pillar does it strengthen?"

If the answer is none — it probably shouldn't exist.

---

## On Trade Secrets

> The biggest trade secret isn't the algorithm. It's the philosophy behind the algorithm.

Google can copy features.
Facebook can copy features.
TikTok can copy features.

They cannot copy twenty years of intentional thinking.

The Community Trust Engine will matter.
The weighting will matter.
The AI will matter.

But what makes them valuable is that they're all serving the same philosophy.

That is incredibly difficult to reverse engineer.

## A Future Document — The Decision Book

Not now. Later.

**The Mapping With Melanin™ Decision Book**

Not the Decision Log. Something different.

Every major decision gets one page. It would include:

- The problem we were solving
- Options we considered
- Why we chose this path
- Alternatives we rejected
- Risks
- Lessons learned later
- Whether we'd make the same decision again

Imagine someone joining the company in 2040. Instead of asking, "Why did they build it this way?" — they open one page. And the founders are still explaining it to them.

That's institutional memory.
