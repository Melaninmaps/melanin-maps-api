Mapping With Melanin™
Community Governance & Moderation Specification
Status: Proposed — Founder Review
Purpose: Product, policy, moderation, group governance, reporting, business safety/discrimination reporting, and KinfolkAI behavior.
Founder-review note: The structure and policy recommendations below are my recommendations based on the platform principles we have established. They are not legal advice and should receive legal review before final public policy language is published.
1. Core Community Principle
Mapping With Melanin™ is an inclusive platform built to allow people across cultures, communities, identities, and the diaspora to discover places, people, resources, businesses, and experiences that matter to them.
Inclusion does not mean every member must share the same preferences, beliefs, culture, religion, politics, lifestyle, or community affiliations.
Members may intentionally seek communities and businesses that reflect their own experiences.
Examples of acceptable affirmative discovery include:
“Show me Ethiopian-owned businesses.”
“Find Black-owned salons.”
“I want a Spanish-speaking attorney.”
“Show me LGBTQIA+-friendly destinations.”
“Find Caribbean restaurants.”
“I want a culturally responsive therapist.”
Mapping With Melanin should help people find what they are seeking, rather than require them to justify why that community or cultural connection matters to them.
However:
Members may center their own communities without attacking another community's humanity.
2. Platform Standard
The platform should adopt this foundational statement:
Center your community. Celebrate your culture. Choose the spaces that serve you. Disagree when you disagree. But do not use Mapping With Melanin™ to deny another person's dignity or right to belong.
And:
Belonging somewhere does not require everyone else to belong there. Belonging here requires respecting everyone else's right to be here.
3. Preference vs. Discrimination
The system must distinguish affirmative preference from targeted discrimination or hostility.
Acceptable:
“I'm looking for a Black OB-GYN.”
“Show me Cuban-owned restaurants.”
“I want an LGBTQIA+-friendly hotel.”
“This group is for Haitian entrepreneurs.”
“This is a Christian mothers group.”
“This group centers queer Black travelers.”
“This group is for HBCU alumni.”
Not acceptable:
“Don't support those people.”
“People from that group aren't welcome on this platform.”
Targeted slurs, dehumanization, threats, harassment, or organized campaigns against another community are prohibited.
Preference filters should primarily be affirmative:
Show me X
rather than:
Hide everyone who is X.
4. Three Governance Environments
A. Public Mapping With Melanin Spaces
Includes:
Community Feed, public posts, public comments, reviews, business profiles, public events and other publicly visible member contributions.
These spaces follow the full Mapping With Melanin Community Guidelines.
Platform administrators retain final moderation authority.
B. Groups / Kinfolk Circles / Community Spaces
Groups receive greater autonomy.
Group administrators can establish the culture of their spaces and moderate conduct that might be completely acceptable elsewhere on Mapping With Melanin.
Recommended configurable settings include:
Setting	Options
Conversation	Open Debate / Respectful Discussion / Supportive Space
Membership	Open / Approval Required / Invite Only
Language	Family Friendly / Casual / Explicit Allowed
Politics	Allowed / Limited / Not Allowed
Religion	Allowed / Group Focus / Limited / Not Allowed
Self-Promotion	Allowed / Approval Required / Not Allowed
Off-Topic Posts	Allowed / Limited / Not Allowed
Anonymous Posts	Allowed / Not Allowed
Media	Allowed / Approval Required / Not Allowed
Admins can also establish custom written group rules.
Recommended policy: Joining a group means agreeing to that group's rules.
A member therefore cannot necessarily demand that content be removed merely because it violates their personal preference if it is permitted under the group's disclosed rules.
They can leave the group.
They can also always report content to Mapping With Melanin if they believe it violates platform-wide safety standards.
C. Private Communications
Private conversations receive greater conversational latitude than public posts.
However, users retain Report and Block controls.
Threats, stalking, targeted harassment, scams, hate-based targeting and other serious platform violations remain subject to platform intervention.
5. The Non-Negotiable Platform Floor
Group administrators cannot override:
credible threats or encouragement of violence
targeted harassment
stalking
dehumanizing attacks against protected communities
serious hate-based targeting
non-consensual sexual content
exploitation
scams/fraud
dangerous impersonation
doxxing or prohibited exposure of private personal information
coordinated harassment
other serious safety violations
There is therefore no administrator setting for “hate allowed.”
Group rules can be stricter than Mapping With Melanin rules.
They cannot eliminate Mapping With Melanin's baseline protections.
6. Reporting UX
Every report should first ask:
What happened?
Possible selections:
Discrimination / Unequal Treatment
Harassment or Bullying
Hate or Identity-Based Attack
Threat or Safety Concern
Sexual Harassment / Inappropriate Conduct
Scam / Fraud
Privacy Concern
False Information About Me/My Business
Group Rule Violation
Spam
Something Else
Then:
How do you know about this?
It happened to me
I witnessed it
I saw it posted publicly
I'm reporting on behalf of my business/organization
Optional:
Tell us what happened.
Where appropriate:
Add supporting information.
7. Business Discrimination Reporting
Business reports require additional structure.
Possible experience types:
Denied Service
Treated Differently
Racial/Ethnic Discrimination
National-Origin Discrimination
Religious Discrimination
LGBTQIA+ Discrimination
Gender-Related Discrimination
Disability/Accessibility Discrimination
Language/Accent Discrimination
Immigration-Related Concern
Harassment
Threat/Safety Concern
Other
Reports should record, where voluntarily provided:
date/time, location, reporter relationship to incident, description, supporting information, and whether the person personally experienced or witnessed the incident.
8. Reports Are Signals — Not Automatic Verdicts
Recommended rule: Never use a simple “X reports = guilty” algorithm.
Moderation should evaluate:
Severity + Pattern + Credibility + Independence
For example:
One severe credible threat may require immediate escalation.
Five independent reports describing substantially similar discriminatory treatment over time may justify formal review.
Fifty reports from newly created accounts within fifteen minutes may indicate coordinated reporting rather than fifty independent experiences.
9. Recommended Escalation Framework
Level 1 — Group/Community Issue
Examples: off-topic posts, group-specific language rules, self-promotion.
Primary moderator: Group Admin.
Level 2 — Conduct Issue
Examples: personal insults, repeated disruptive behavior.
Possible response: warning, removal, temporary restriction.
Level 3 — Targeted Harassment
Repeated harassment or targeting.
Possible response: removal, posting restriction, temporary suspension.
Level 4 — Serious Identity-Based Abuse / Discrimination
Escalates to Mapping With Melanin moderation.
Possible response: content removal, account restrictions, suspension or removal depending upon severity/history.
Level 5 — Credible Immediate Safety Concern
Priority moderation/safety review.
Serious cases should skip lower levels rather than requiring accumulated strikes.
10. Pattern Detection
The system should distinguish:
Report count from independent corroboration.
Potential factors:
number of unique reporters, account age, prior legitimate participation, similarity of incidents, incident dates, supporting evidence, geographic consistency, previous reports against the subject, reporter abuse history, and indicators of coordinated reporting.
Automation can prioritize cases.
Human review should make consequential determinations whenever reasonably possible.
11. Business Public Warning Threshold
My recommendation: Do not automatically publish “This business discriminates” based solely on member reports.
There are substantial fairness, reputational and legal considerations.
Instead, maintain reports privately while they are evaluated.
Where an approved threshold/process has been met, potential language might eventually be:
Community Treatment Concerns
Multiple community members have reported concerns about treatment at this location. View community experiences.
This feature should not launch until Mapping With Melanin has established:
verification standards, response rights, correction procedures, appeal procedures, expiration/re-review rules, moderation documentation and attorney-reviewed public language.
12. Business Right to Respond
Where appropriate, a business should be able to respond to a report or public community concern.
Responses cannot expose the reporter's protected/private information.
Business responses do not automatically invalidate reports.
Likewise, reports do not automatically establish wrongdoing.
The historical record should preserve meaningful moderation actions and corrections.
13. False and Weaponized Reporting
This needs its own violation category.
Abuse of Reporting Tools
Examples include coordinated mass reporting, knowingly fabricated allegations, repeated reports solely because someone disagrees with a speaker, targeting culturally specific groups through reporting, and attempting to suppress legitimate businesses through false reports.
Possible consequences:
warning → reporting restrictions → account restrictions → suspension.
This prevents the platform's safety system from becoming a tool for discrimination itself.
14. Appeals
Members, businesses, group admins and creators affected by significant moderation decisions should have an appeal mechanism.
Possible statuses:
Submitted
Under Review
Action Taken
No Violation Found
More Information Needed
Appealed
Appeal Under Review
Final Decision
The person who reported something should receive an appropriate outcome notification without receiving private disciplinary information they are not entitled to.
15. Group Administrator Powers
Group admins may:
set group rules, approve members, remove members, approve/remove posts, restrict posting, establish discussion boundaries, approve media/self-promotion and report serious conduct to Mapping With Melanin.
Group admins may not:
override platform safety standards, view confidential reporter information without authorization, prevent users from reporting to Mapping With Melanin, authorize threats/targeted hate, or retaliate against a member merely for using the platform reporting mechanism in good faith.
16. Group Entry Disclosure
Before joining a moderated group, users should see:
About This Group
Who This Space Is For
Community Rules
Conversation Style
Admin-Defined Restrictions
Then:
Join & Agree to Group Rules
This makes expectations transparent before conflict happens.
17. LGBTQIA+, Religious and Cultural Differences
Mapping With Melanin should not require members to abandon religious or cultural beliefs to participate.
Nor should religious or cultural beliefs be used as permission to target another person.
A faith-centered group can establish faith-centered discussion standards.
An LGBTQIA+-centered group can establish itself as an affirming/supportive space.
Members who do not want those environments do not have to join them.
Neither group may organize harassment against the other.
Disagreement is not automatically hate.
Identity is not automatically hostility.
Targeted degradation, harassment and threats cross the platform boundary.
18. KinfolkAI Governance
Kinfolk should support affirmative discovery.
Examples:
“Find Ethiopian-owned businesses.”
“Find Black-owned salons.”
“Show me LGBTQIA+-friendly destinations.”
“Find Spanish-speaking attorneys.”
Kinfolk should not convert hateful language into discriminatory exclusion logic.
If someone requests hostile exclusion, Kinfolk can redirect toward affirmative discovery:
“I can help you find businesses based on the communities, ownership types, cultural experiences, services, or values you'd like to support.”
Kinfolk should not infer protected identities that businesses or users have not explicitly supplied through approved data sources.
19. THE REAL and Discrimination Reports Must Remain Separate
This is important to the business system we're building.
THE REAL = community trust/praise.
It should not become:
“They weren't racist.”
“They didn't discriminate.”
“They didn't harass me.”
Those are inappropriate praise badges because basic nondiscrimination should not become an achievement.
Negative experiences belong in:
Something Wasn't Right
and serious issues can proceed into the formal reporting/safety system.
20. Community Intelligence Without Public Shaming
Mapping With Melanin should prioritize helping people make informed decisions rather than building a public punishment system.
That means the platform can learn from:
THE REAL, What Stands Out?, Vibes, saves, return intent, safety surveys, structured experiences, discrimination reports and verified patterns.
But each data type retains its meaning.
A Vibe is not a safety determination.
A report is not a proven fact.
A pattern is not automatically a legal finding.
A business response does not erase someone's experience.
That distinction should exist in both the database and the UI.
21. Data Architecture Recommendation
I recommend Replit keep these conceptually separate:
community_reports
business_discrimination_reports
group_rule_reports
moderation_actions
appeals
group_rules
group_moderation_settings
reporter_abuse_signals
Do not simply place everything into one generic reports table without first determining whether the current architecture can preserve these distinctions.
This is a design recommendation, not authorization to modify the production database.

Before implementing this specification, Replit should perform a read-only audit of the existing:
Community Feed moderation, reports, business safety reports, discrimination reporting, group administration, blocking, appeals, admin moderation queues, user sanctions, THE REAL/endorsement architecture and Kinfolk moderation behavior.
Then compare:
