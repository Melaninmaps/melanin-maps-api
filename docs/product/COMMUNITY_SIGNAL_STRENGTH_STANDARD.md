## Core Founder Principle

> **KinfolkAI must understand scale.**

A raw count is not automatically a meaningful community signal.

Example:
  100 relevant searches among 500 people = 20%
  100 relevant searches among 50,000 people = 0.2%

Although both contain 100 searches, the first may represent a substantial
local pattern while the second may represent ordinary background activity.

KinfolkAI must not treat these signals as equivalent.

Information must be collected and interpreted for a defined purpose.
Mapping With Melanin™ must not collect information merely because it may
be useful someday.

## Section 1 — Community Signal Strength Model

Five required dimensions. KinfolkAI should only act proactively when the
combined signal across all five is meaningful.

### A — Prevalence
The proportion of the relevant population represented by the signal.
  "20% of active members in this area searched for brunch."
  NOT: "100 people searched for brunch."

### B — Volume
The number of independent members, reports, searches, saves, events,
businesses, or verified records supporting the signal.
  Rate + sample size together — never one without the other.
  4 of 10 = 40% but weak evidence.
  100 of 500 = 20%, stronger evidence.
  5,000 of 50,000 = 10%, very strong evidence.

### C — Momentum
Whether the signal is increasing, decreasing, stable, seasonal, or a
one-time anomaly.
  "Brunch interest increased from 8% to 18% of relevant local searches
   over the past four weeks." → Stronger signal than 100 searches alone.
  Normal seasonal pattern ≠ meaningful new momentum.

### D — Relevance
Whether the signal meaningfully applies to the specific member, business,
professional, organization, Cultural Ambassador, neighborhood, or
community receiving the insight.

### E — Confidence
Whether the signal has enough recent, independent, representative, and
privacy-safe evidence to support a conclusion.

Mathematical weights are NOT finalized during this documentation phase.
Architecture must be configurable. Founder review required before any
weights are set.

## Section 2 — Define the Correct Denominator

For each insight, identify the appropriate relevant population.

Potential denominators (match to the question):
  Active members in a ZIP code
  Active members within a defined radius
  Members who searched the relevant category
  Members planning travel to the location
  Members who selected a relevant interest
  Families who voluntarily selected family-related preferences
  Businesses in a specific category
  Verified local contributors
  Members active during the measured time period

DO NOT compare a local signal against the complete national membership
unless the question genuinely concerns the national population.

Document how KinfolkAI chooses and explains the denominator.
The denominator must be surfaced to the member in a "Why this result?"
response.

## Section 3 — Minimum Sample and Privacy Rules

A high percentage from a very small group must never automatically
trigger an insight.

| Sample | Rate | Evidence Level |
|---|---|---|
| 4 of 10 | 40% | Weak — do not surface |
| 100 of 500 | 20% | Stronger evidence |
| 5,000 of 50,000 | 10% | Highly supported |

Minimum sample requirements must be defined for each category:
  General consumer interests
  Business opportunities
  Community needs
  Safety and discrimination reports  ← higher threshold required
  Health-related searches            ← higher threshold required
  Legal-service searches             ← higher threshold required
  Family and child-related information ← higher threshold required
  Employment and workplace reports   ← higher threshold required
  Financial concerns                 ← higher threshold required
  Protected or sensitive characteristics ← highest threshold required

Privacy rule: KinfolkAI must not expose insights where individuals
could reasonably be identified.

DO NOT expose exact counts for small or sensitive groups.

Example of what must never happen:
  "Three members in your building searched for divorce attorneys."
  Even if mathematically accurate — this is invasive.

## Section 4 — Unique People vs. Repeated Actions

Distinguish clearly:
  100 searches by 100 different people (independent community signal)
  100 searches by 10 people (partial community signal)
  100 repeated searches by one person (individual pattern, not community signal)

Document and implement:
  Unique-member counts
  Total actions
  Repeat behavior patterns
  Bot and abuse filtering
  Anonymous-session handling
  Cross-device deduplication
  Time-window deduplication

Do not treat repeated actions by one person as equivalent to independent
community demand.

## Section 5 — Time, Baseline, and Seasonality

Every signal must include an appropriate time period.

Compare signals against:
  Previous period (week-over-week or month-over-month)
  Same period last year (when data is available)
  Normal seasonal pattern
  Community-specific baseline
  Category-specific baseline
  Relevant comparison communities (only when responsible and
    sample sizes are sufficient)

Seasonal examples that require baseline context:
  Back-to-school interest in August
  Brunch searches around holidays
  Moving searches near school-year changes
  Tax assistance in tax season
  Family activities during summer
  Heating repair during winter

KinfolkAI should distinguish normal seasonality from meaningful new
momentum. A normal annual pattern is still potentially useful context —
but must not be described as an unexplained surge.

## Section 6 — Data Quality and Representation

KinfolkAI must account for:
  Uneven platform adoption across geographies
  Communities with very few MWM members
  Visitor activity vs. resident activity
  Free vs. paid member participation patterns
  Highly active members skewing aggregate signals
  Missing information and coverage gaps
  Reporting campaigns that increase awareness (not necessarily conditions)
  Duplicate reports
  Moderation removals
  Delayed or stale data
  Geographic boundary differences
  Population changes over time

Required honest language:
  "Among active MWM members in this area..."
  "Based on recent searches within Mapping With Melanin™..."
  "This is an emerging signal with limited information."
  "The available information may not represent the full community."

KinfolkAI must not imply that MWM member behavior represents an entire
city when the sample does not support that conclusion.

## Section 7 — Business Insight Standard

Business insights must use proportional, contextual language.

UNACCEPTABLE:
  "100 people searched for brunch. Start brunch and increase sales."

ACCEPTABLE:
  "Brunch represented approximately 20% of restaurant-related searches
   among active MWM members in your area over the past four weeks,
   compared with 11% during the prior period. This may be worth exploring
   based on your menu, staffing, costs, licensing, and business goals."

Full required example:
  "Brunch represented about 20% of restaurant-related searches from
   active members in your area during the past four weeks, up from 11%
   in the prior period. This may be worth exploring based on your current
   menu and business goals. Would you like to review likely costs,
   staffing, hours, and licensing considerations?"

Why this example is correct:
  Proportional (rate not raw count)
  Evidence-based
  Non-promissory (never guarantees revenue)
  Specific to the business's context
  An invitation, not an instruction

Business guidance must always:
  Never guarantee revenue
  Never exaggerate outcomes
  Explain the relevant population
  Explain the time period
  Distinguish searches from actual purchases
  Consider the business's objectives and capabilities
  Invite evaluation rather than command action
  Offer realistic planning support
  Recommend community businesses or professionals where relevant
  Clearly distinguish organic insights from sponsored opportunities

## Section 8 — Community Need and Opportunity Standard

KinfolkAI may identify potential community needs only when signals are
sufficiently supported.

Applicable categories:
  Skilled-trade demand
  Medical-professional shortages
  Legal-service gaps
  Childcare needs
  Accessibility services
  Mentorship needs
  Cultural event demand
  Business-category gaps
  Volunteer demand
  Education and apprenticeship opportunities

Required language:
  "May indicate an unmet need"
  "Suggests sustained local interest"
  "Relatively few verified providers are currently listed"
  "More information is needed before treating this as a confirmed shortage"

Example:
  UNACCEPTABLE: "People need welders here."
  ACCEPTABLE:   "Local searches and verified job postings suggest
                 sustained demand for welding services, while relatively
                 few local providers are listed. This may indicate an
                 opportunity, but more information is needed before
                 treating it as a confirmed shortage."

DO NOT declare a shortage solely because few businesses are listed on MWM.
The platform's own coverage gaps must be considered and stated.

## Section 9 — Safety and Discrimination Signals

Safety-related interpretation requires additional controls beyond
standard community signals.

Required considerations before surfacing any safety signal:
  Unique reporters (not total reports)
  Relevant visitor or resident volume (the denominator)
  Reporting period (recent vs. historical)
  Corroboration (independent confirmation)
  Severity of reported incidents
  Recency weighting
  Duplicate or related incident detection
  Public findings (official civil-rights findings, court decisions)
  Historical context (sundown town data — kept separate)
  Community support or intervention
  Increased awareness of the reporting feature
  Underreporting from low platform adoption
  Limited platform adoption in the area

An increase in reports may mean ANY of the following:
  Conditions worsened
  Reporting access improved
  Awareness increased
  One event generated multiple related reports
  More MWM members visited the area

KinfolkAI must communicate this uncertainty explicitly. It must never
assign community-wide meaning from raw report totals.

Historical sundown-town data must remain separate from current report
rates and must never be combined into a unified score.

## Section 10 — Purposeful Data Collection Standard

Before collecting any new information, document all 12 of the following:

  1. The specific community or member benefit
  2. The decision or experience it may responsibly improve
  3. Whether less personal information could accomplish the same result
  4. Whether the field is required or optional
  5. How consent is obtained
  6. Who can access it
  7. Whether it is used individually or only in aggregate
  8. How long it remains useful
  9. When it expires or stops affecting results
  10. How the member can view, correct, disable, or delete it
  11. What harm could result from misuse
  12. Whether the Community Intelligence Constitution permits its use

Shorter version (four required questions — minimum bar):
  What member or community benefit does this create?
  What decision could responsibly change because of it?
  Can the same result be achieved with less personal information?
  When should the information expire or stop affecting results?

If the team cannot answer those four questions, do not collect
the information.

## Section 11 — Member-Facing Explanation (Progressive Disclosure)

Every community insight should support three levels of explanation.

### Level 1 — Plain Result (immediately visible)
  "Brunch interest is growing nearby."
  No jargon. No numbers. No methodology.

### Level 2 — Why This Result? (one tap)
  "Brunch represented about 20% of restaurant-related searches from
   active members in your area over the past four weeks, up from 11%
   the month before. This is based on searches, not purchases."

### Level 3 — Learn More (full methodology — available on request)
  Sample size
  Relevant population definition
  Time period
  Comparison baseline
  Evidence category (unique members vs. total actions)
  Data limitations
  Coverage gaps
  Correction process

## Two New Constitutional Rules — Add to Community Intelligence Constitution

### Constitutional Rule 8 — Proportional Interpretation
  "Kinfolk must interpret community information in proportion to the
   relevant population, time period, context, and strength of evidence.
   Raw totals alone must never be treated as community meaning."

### Constitutional Rule 9 — Purposeful Collection
  "Kinfolk collects information only when there is a defined,
   privacy-respecting purpose that can produce a meaningful benefit
   for members, businesses, organizations, or communities."
