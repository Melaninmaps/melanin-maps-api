## Question 1: Search Discovery & Layout

A user opens the app and searches for "Jewelry." The system finds 12 Verified Businesses and 5 Community Entrepreneurs.

**Answer:**
The results are presented in two clearly separated sections — they are never mixed. The 12 Verified Businesses appear first, at the top of the results list. Below them, visually separated with a distinct section header, are the 5 Community Entrepreneurs. Each Community Entrepreneur card displays:
- Their name and offerings
- A snippet of their story
- Their community endorsement count
- A distinct **"Community Entrepreneur"** label

The separation is deliberate — it celebrates all levels of entrepreneurship while maintaining clear expectations for users about what they are looking at.

## Question 2: Promotional Capabilities

A Community Entrepreneur wants to spend $50 on a promoted listing for the holiday weekend.

**Answer:**
**No, they cannot.** Community Entrepreneurs do not have access to advertising or promoted listings. These features are reserved exclusively for Verified Businesses. The Community Entrepreneur tier is **always free** — the platform will never charge individuals pursuing their entrepreneurial dreams. Discovery for this tier is entirely organic, driven by community reviews, endorsements, and feed posts that link back to their Marketplace profile.

## Question 3: The Upgrade Journey

A home chef has been listed for 6 months with 65 sales, 115 reviews, and 300 followers.

**Answer:**
Kinfolk AI detects these organic growth signals and triggers a **celebratory prompt — never a warning**. The tone is congratulatory:

> *"🎉 Your community has supported you through more than 100 sales! You're now eligible to become a Verified Business. Verification unlocks additional tools, greater visibility, and helps customers recognize your growing brand."*

The upgrade is **never forced.** It is always encouraged, and the prompt is designed to feel organic — not like unlocking a game achievement. All history (reviews, endorsements, sales records) carries forward if they choose to upgrade; they never start fresh.

## Question 4: Content Placement (Feed vs. Marketplace)

A Community Entrepreneur posts: *"I'll be at the Juneteenth Festival this Saturday from 12-4 PM!"*

**Answer:**
The post lives in the **Community Feed**, which is reserved for conversation and community connection. However, the post **must link back to their Marketplace profile**. The Marketplace is a distinct section of the app for commerce — Community Entrepreneurs are not buried in the feed. Feed posts function as funnels that drive traffic to their Marketplace listing, where their products, reviews, endorsements, and commerce activity live.

## Question 5: Abuse Prevention Guardrails

A user attempts to add 3 locations, upload 5,000 wholesale items, and add 15 employee profiles.

**Answer:**
The system's guardrails intercept this behavior. Operating at this scale — multiple storefront locations, wholesale inventory, and an employee roster — indicates they are functioning as a full business, not an informal community entrepreneur. **Kinfolk AI prompts them to upgrade to a Verified Business.** If they refuse to upgrade while clearly continuing to operate at business scale, an **administrative review is triggered** to protect the integrity of the tier system. The guardrails are designed to protect legitimate Community Entrepreneurs and the community's trust in the tier, not to punish growth.

## Question 6: Data Continuity on Upgrade

A Community Entrepreneur with 85 five-star reviews and 200 endorsements accepts the upgrade to Verified Business.

**Answer:**
**All history carries forward.** Their 85 five-star reviews and 200 "put your people on" endorsements are fully preserved when their profile transitions to Verified Business. The platform ensures they never have to "start fresh" — their community trust, credibility, and sales records move with them. This principle is non-negotiable: the upgrade is a milestone, not a reset.

## Question 7: The Talent Pipeline

A restaurant owner is looking for a pastry chef. A Community Entrepreneur selling vegan desserts just received their 50th glowing review.

**Answer:**
The platform uses the **Kinfolk AI matching engine**. Kinfolk monitors **Marketplace activity** — reviews, endorsements, and sales patterns — as its primary data source. Upon detecting the 50th glowing review on the CE's vegan dessert profile, Kinfolk:

1. **Proactively prompts the restaurant owner:** Surfaces the highly-rated local talent and asks if they'd like to be connected.
2. **Simultaneously prompts the entrepreneur:** Asks if they'd like to be connected to restaurants seeking their specific skills.

The match is bidirectional, opt-in, and driven entirely by demonstrated community trust — not by self-promotion or a job posting. This is the talent pipeline in action: the Marketplace is the data source, Kinfolk is the connector, and community trust is the qualification.
