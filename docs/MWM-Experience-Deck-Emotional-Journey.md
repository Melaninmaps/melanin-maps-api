## The Core Question

> "How does this slide make someone feel **before they read a single word?**"

This is the lens through which every slide in the Experience Deck is evaluated.

Not: Does it look polished?
Not: Is the typography correct?
Not: Is the hierarchy strong?

Those are craft. They matter. But they serve the journey — they do not define it.

---

## The One Sentence Test

After every presentation, ask:

> "What is the ONE sentence I hope they remember tomorrow?"

For the Experience Deck, that sentence is:

> **Belonging isn't a feature. It's the reason the features exist.**

If every slide serves that sentence, the deck succeeds.
If a slide does not serve that sentence, its purpose needs to be reconsidered.

## Slide-by-Slide Emotional Intent — Key Moments

### Slide 01 — Opening
**Before:** Uncertainty — a new audience arrives not knowing what this is or whether it speaks to them.
**After:** Hope — they feel something quiet and warm. A breath. A recognition that this is for them.
**Design must ask:** Does the visual create stillness before words?

### Slide 05 — Trust Score
**Before:** Skepticism — the audience has heard claims before. They are wondering: "Can I actually trust this?"
**After:** Trust — the data and system make the promise credible, not the words alone.
**Design must ask:** Does the visual feel like evidence, not marketing?

### Slide 11 — KinfolkAI™
**Before:** Curiosity — something is different here. They lean in slightly.
**After:** Anticipation — they want to know more. They feel the possibility before they understand the product.
**Design must ask:** Does the visual carry a sense of intelligence without coldness? Wonder without confusion?

### Slide 21 — Closing / Welcome Home
**Before:** Affirmation — they have traveled through the full arc. They believe.
**After:** Peace — not excitement. Not a call to action. Quiet belonging. The feeling of arriving somewhere true.
**Design must ask:** Does the visual feel like rest, not resolution? An open door, not a closing chapter?

If a slide scores under 9: do not redesign it. Identify **why** first.

## The Remove Half Test

If the deck were limited to 10 slides, which 11 would survive the cut?

*To be completed during Founder's Review. A slide that cannot be removed without breaking the story earns its place. A slide that can be removed without the audience noticing should be questioned.*

This test is not about eliminating slides. It is about understanding which slides are essential and which are supporting. Essential slides carry the emotional journey. Supporting slides amplify it. Neither is wrong — but the distinction matters.

---

## The Five-Audience Test

*To be conducted eventually — not today.*

Ask five different people to review the deck, each approaching it as:

1. An investor
2. A community member
3. A business owner
4. A Cultural Ambassador
5. Someone who has never heard of Mapping With Melanin™

If all five leave understanding the same mission, the deck has succeeded.

## The Real Standard

When the first Zoom presentation ends, the question will not be:

> "Did they like the slides?"

It will be:

> **"Did they feel what we hoped they'd feel?"**

Every design decision — color, type, icon, spacing, gradient, pacing — exists to serve the answer to that question.

---

## Relationship to Implementation

This map precedes implementation. It is not a design specification — it is the emotional specification that design must fulfill.

When implementation of the Experience Deck Enhanced v2 is authorized:

- Every visual choice is evaluated against the emotional intent for that slide
- If a design choice does not serve the intended emotional journey, it should be reconsidered regardless of its aesthetic quality
- The deck is complete when the emotional journey from Uncertainty → Peace has been successfully guided
