Communities are not just locations. They are living participants in the platform.

---

## Terminology — Permanent Decision

NEVER use:
  "Neighborhood Safety Score"
  — Too narrow, easily misunderstood, reduces communities to a
    single dimension, risks stigmatizing minority neighborhoods

USE INSTEAD:
  "Community Health Profile"
  — Multidimensional, asset-framed, measures capacity not crime

The framing distinction matters fundamentally. The platform should say:
  "This community is changing."
  NOT: "This neighborhood is dangerous."

---

## The Official Disclaimer — Preserved Verbatim

This language must appear wherever Community Health data is surfaced:

> Community Health reflects information voluntarily contributed by members
> and publicly available community indicators where appropriate. It is
> designed to help members understand resources, belonging, accessibility,
> community activity, and reported experiences. It is not a measure of the
> people who live in a community.

---

## The Foundational Safeguard — Permanent

The platform MUST NEVER imply that minority communities are unsafe because
they are minority communities.

That is the exact opposite of what Mapping With Melanin™ exists to do.

## Community Health Profile — Five Dimensions

### Dimension 1 — Safety
NOT crime. Community-contributed, asset-framed.
  Community-reported discrimination trends
  Safety survey trends (from existing neighborhood_surveys table)
  Walking comfort (day/night — only where members chose to share)
  Community support presence
  Emergency resources
  Community engagement levels

### Dimension 2 — Opportunity
  Minority-owned business growth
  Jobs available (existing jobs table)
  Apprenticeships
  Trade shortages (by type)
  Medical shortages (by specialty)
  Childcare availability
  Volunteer opportunities
  Entrepreneurship activity
  Professional specialty shortages (lawyers, doctors, counselors)

### Dimension 3 — Belonging
  Community organizations
  Active events
  Mentorship availability
  HBCU alumni groups
  Cultural organizations
  Faith communities (only if a user has indicated interest)
  Recreation
  Arts

### Dimension 4 — Accessibility
  Wheelchair accessibility
  Transit options
  Family friendliness
  Parks
  Senior resources

### Dimension 5 — Growth
  New businesses
  Community improvements
  Restoration projects
  Public investment
  Small business openings

## Community Progress — How Kinfolk Surfaces It

Communities that do the work should see the results.

When a community shows positive trend signals, Kinfolk does NOT say:
  "You moved up three points."

Kinfolk says:
  "Community engagement has increased over the past year."
  "More residents are contributing local recommendations."
  "Three new community organizations joined."
  "Businesses are receiving more positive accessibility feedback."

The community can see its own progress. That is hopeful — not ranked.

## Community Needs Intelligence

Kinfolk's question:
  "What does this community need?"
  NOT: "What can we sell?"

Examples of how gap detection creates ecosystem connections:

TRADE SHORTAGE (e.g., welders):
  Trade schools nearby
  → High school seniors open to trades
  → People interested in skilled trades
  → Businesses hiring
  → Community organizations offering scholarships
  → Employers connected

MEDICAL SHORTAGE:
  Nursing students
  → Medical residents
  → Volunteer clinics
  → Hospital openings
  → Health fairs
  → Community organizations

LEGAL SPECIALTY GAP:
  Kinfolk identifies:
    "Only two Spanish-speaking estate attorneys in this area."
    "No disability-rights attorney nearby."
    "No minority-owned IP attorney within fifty miles."
  That is valuable community intelligence, not just individual search.

FOOD INSECURITY:
  Community organizations serving food
  → Businesses willing to donate
  → Volunteers
  → Food-insecurity trend signals
  → Schools requesting support
  Kinfolk connects — it does not solve.

## Community Aspirations

Not just needs. Shared goals that a neighborhood articulates.

Example: A neighborhood wants to become more walkable.

Kinfolk notices → connects:
  Businesses that can participate
  Residents who want to contribute
  Volunteers
  City meetings and hearings
  Grant opportunities
  Community organizations
  Landscape architects and urban planners

Communities become participants in their own improvement,
not passive locations on a map.

## Life Season Integration — Trade and Career Example

A seventeen-year-old not interested in college.

Kinfolk presents, not prescribes:
  "There are several paths."
  → Local apprenticeships
  → Welding programs
  → Electrical apprenticeships
  → Union opportunities
  → Community businesses looking for skilled trades

It is not pushing college. It is not pushing trades.
It is presenting opportunities aligned with the person's goals.
The member chooses.

Kinfolk should not only surface "Attorney."

  "No disability-rights attorney nearby."

## The Seventh Constitutional Principle

> Kinfolk should measure the health of a community by its capacity to
> support the people within it — not by stereotypes, sensational events,
> or isolated incidents.

This principle:
  Protects against reducing a place to a crime statistic
  Protects against single-narrative framing
  Recognizes a healthy community by what it provides, not what
  sensational coverage says about it
  Belongs in the Community Intelligence Constitution

A healthy community is one where people increasingly have access to
opportunity, support, belonging, resources, and trusted information.

## Community Opportunity Intelligence — FSR Entry

A future-state capability that should be documented and preserved.

Rather than only helping individuals find opportunities, Kinfolk should
be able to identify GAPS and CONNECTIONS across an entire community.

Examples:
  Shortage of pediatricians or mental health professionals
  Growing demand for skilled trades (welders, electricians)
  Neighborhood with increasing cultural interest but few organizers
  Small businesses looking for bilingual employees
  Community organizations seeking STEM mentors
  Parents searching for SAT prep, apprenticeship programs, HBCU info
  Entrepreneurs looking for commercial kitchen or shared workspace

CRITICAL RULE: These insights must be based on:
  Aggregated trends (not individual profiling)
  Voluntary community contributions
  Verified information where available

NOT based on: profiling individuals, making unsupported assumptions,
or inferring demographics from content.

This is the next evolution of Kinfolk: helping entire communities
see opportunities they might otherwise miss.
