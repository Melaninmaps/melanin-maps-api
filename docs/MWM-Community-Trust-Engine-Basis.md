## Naming Philosophy

Stop calling it a "score."

The internal system is called the:

### Community Trust Engine™

It calculates **several** things. Not one score. Several. That makes manipulation much harder.

## The Broader Framework

The Community Trust Engine is one part of something larger:

### Mapping With Melanin™ Trust & Belonging Framework

This framework contains multiple confidential models. Google has PageRank. Uber has driver/rider ratings. Airbnb has reviews. This is an opportunity to create something much richer — an ecosystem, not an algorithm.

## The Five Scores

### 1. Community Trust Score

**Question it answers:** Can this member be trusted?

Not popularity. Trust.

Possible contributors:
- Verified identity
- Account longevity
- Constructive participation
- Helpful reviews
- Community feedback
- Event participation
- Reporting accuracy
- Consistency

---

### 2. Community Contribution Score

**Question it answers:** How much has this person contributed?

Not: Are they famous?
Instead: Have they helped?

Possible signals:
- Reviews
- Events attended
- Events hosted
- Businesses supported
- Comments
- Helpful replies
- Community challenges
- Referrals
- Ambassador activities

---

### 3. Business Community Score

**Question it answers:** How engaged is this business?

Not: How many stars?
Instead: How engaged is this business?

Signals:
- Responds to reviews
- Hosts events
- Supports other businesses
- Supports nonprofits
- Replies to questions
- Community partnerships
- Longevity
- Verified ownership

---

### 4. Safety Confidence Score

**Note on naming:** Not "Safety Score." Confidence. That distinction matters.

Internally the platform knows:
- Three reports → Confidence is low
- Hundreds of reports → Confidence is high

That's different. Both can produce the same score on the surface, but the confidence level determines how that score is surfaced and acted upon.

---

### 5. Belonging Score (Future)

Do people return?
Do they recommend it?
Do newcomers feel welcome?
Community participation.
Diversity of interactions.
Positive experiences.

This feels uniquely Mapping With Melanin™.

## The Five Pillars

Instead of "everything earns points," think in pillars. Each pillar has its own internal score. The Community Trust Engine synthesizes those.

| Pillar | Question |
|--------|----------|
| 1. Identity | Verified? |
| 2. Participation | Do they show up? |
| 3. Contribution | Do they help? |
| 4. Trustworthiness | Can others rely on them? |
| 5. Community Impact | Did they improve the community? |

## The Five Layers (Trust & Belonging Framework)

### Layer 1 — Identity Confidence
"Do we know who this is?"

Inputs:
- Verification level
- Account maturity
- Security checks
- Business documentation
- Consistency

### Layer 2 — Contribution Quality
"Do they add value?"

Not quantity. Quality.

- Helpful reviews
- Thoughtful comments
- Community participation
- Educational posts
- Supporting businesses
- Attending events
- Hosting events

### Layer 3 — Community Relationships
"How connected are they?"

Not: How active are they?
Instead: How connected are they?

- Do businesses interact with them?
- Do they support multiple businesses?
- Do they participate across different communities?
- Are they introducing people to one another?
- Are they strengthening the network?

This is a network effect, not an activity count.

### Layer 4 — Reliability
"Do they consistently behave in a trustworthy way over time?"

Not one good month. Not one viral post. Consistency.

### Layer 5 — Positive Impact
"How much positive impact did they create?"

Not: How many followers?
Not: How many likes?
Instead:
- Introduced a new business to the community
- Helped answer questions
- Organized events
- Welcomed newcomers
- Shared useful safety information
- Connected people with opportunities

## Anti-Gaming Principles

### No single category should dominate

No single category should account for more than approximately 25–30% of a composite score.

**Why:** Then the system becomes difficult to game. Someone can't simply attend 1,000 events, leave 5,000 comments, or invite 500 friends and dominate. They must demonstrate value across multiple dimensions.

### Negative weights (not punishments — corrections)

Example: Someone leaves 200 reviews, but they're consistently flagged as inaccurate. That should reduce the influence of future reviews — not necessarily their standing in the community, but the weight assigned to their future contributions until they rebuild trust. That's a healthier model than simply banning or ignoring.

### One thing that should never dominate: activity alone

Don't reward people simply for being *active*. Activity can be gamed.

Reward people for being **valuable to the community**. That's a much harder thing to fake.

---

## Time Decay Philosophy

Someone was amazing five years ago. Never came back. Should they still dominate rankings?

Probably not.

- Recent contributions should matter.
- Historical contributions should still count — just less.

---

## Reputation Philosophy

Many platforms use: Hard to gain. Easy to lose.

**Recommended philosophy:** Hard to gain. Hard to lose.

Unless there is serious misconduct. One mistake shouldn't erase years of trust.

---

## Community Momentum

An additional model worth building separately.

If a business suddenly becomes much more active — hosting events, responding to customers, supporting nonprofits, collaborating with ambassadors — the platform can recognize that positive momentum.

Not because it's permanent. Because it's happening now.

Likewise, if a community becomes less active over time, that can be reflected appropriately.

Momentum rewards positive change without erasing long-term trust.

---

## Confidence Alongside Every Score

Every calculation should produce two outputs.

Example:
```
Business Community Score: 87
Confidence: 94%
(Hundreds of observations)

Business Community Score: 87
Confidence: 42%
(Only three observations)
```

Internally that distinction is incredibly useful. Externally it helps prevent premature signal amplification.

## What Should Be Hidden

### Fraud Risk Score
Nobody sees it. It helps moderators only.

### What Should Not Be Displayed Publicly

Users don't need to see:
```
Trust: 87.4
Contribution: 91.8
Reliability: 84.2
```

That encourages people to optimize for numbers.

Instead, the internal models drive what the user *experiences*:
- Better recommendations
- More trusted reviews
- Stronger community signals
- Higher-quality discovery

The framework works behind the scenes without turning the app into a game.

---

## Business Community Health (not just reviews)

Businesses shouldn't just have reviews. Imagine if they had a **Community Health** metric based on:

- Community engagement
- Event participation
- Responsiveness
- Supporting others
- Longevity
- Verification
- Educational content
- Ambassador collaborations

That's much richer than a star rating.

Business influence should never come from paying for membership. It should be earned through behavior — consistent responsiveness, community engagement, hosting or supporting events, collaborating with other businesses, positive experiences over time, verified ownership, maintaining accurate information.

No single factor should dominate.

## Cultural Ambassadors

Weight:

That keeps the system fair.
