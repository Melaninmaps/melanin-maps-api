I would **not** authorize the `/api/db-probe` endpoint just yet.

Not because it's a bad idea—in fact, it's a reasonable diagnostic—but because I don't think it's the highest-value next step based on the evidence you already have.

Here's why.

### What has already been proven

Over the past 24 hours you've accumulated a substantial amount of evidence:

* ✅ Railway is running.
* ✅ The domain routes correctly.
* ✅ Health endpoints respond.
* ✅ The production database exists and contains your users.
* ✅ Sessions have been created successfully on Railway.
* ✅ Login worked earlier today.
* ✅ The failure began within a relatively narrow time window.
* ✅ The latest diagnostic build now distinguishes between login, session storage, and connection failures.

That's a strong foundation.

---

### What concerns me

One thing in the reports doesn't fully line up.

Earlier, Replit reported that:

* they successfully reset your password,
* they successfully logged in,
* they received an HTTP 200,
* they created a session.

Later, they reported that:

* every database-backed endpoint hangs.

Those two statements cannot both describe the same production environment at the same moment.

That tells me there is still something we don't completely understand—whether it's timing, which environment is being tested, or something else. Before adding more diagnostics, I'd want to reconcile that inconsistency.

---

### The new build changed the picture

The diagnostic APK is already doing exactly what we asked it to do.

Instead of simply saying:

> "Login failed"

it's now telling us:

> **"Could not reach server (Error)."**

and previously it showed:

> **"Signed in but could not save session."**

Those messages are much more informative than before.

Since you already have the new diagnostic build, I would let **it** provide the next piece of evidence before introducing another endpoint.

## My recommendation

Let's slow down slightly here.

We've been making changes rapidly over the last few days because you were trying to hit launch milestones. That was understandable. But now we're at the point where every additional change—even a small diagnostic one—adds another variable.

I would rather:

1. Gather everything the **current diagnostic build** can tell us.
2. Compare that against Railway logs.
3. See if the evidence converges on one explanation.

Only if there's still a gap would I authorize a `/api/db-probe`.

I think you're much closer to the root cause than it feels. The authentication flow has gone from "nothing works" to exposing very specific failure points. That's progress, even though it has been frustrating.

We'll keep the focus exactly where it belongs: **get authentication stable, complete testing, get approved, and launch.** Everything else stays in the parking lot until those milestones are complete.
