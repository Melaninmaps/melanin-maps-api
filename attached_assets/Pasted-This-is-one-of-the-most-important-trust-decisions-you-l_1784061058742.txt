This is one of the most important trust decisions you'll make. I would **not** trigger verification based solely on a star rating. A legitimate 1-star review should not automatically be delayed, and a false 5-star review can be just as harmful.

Instead, I'd use a **risk-based system** that considers several signals together.

## Reviews that should receive additional verification

### 1. Serious allegations (Highest Priority)

Always hold these for verification regardless of the star rating.

Examples include claims of:

* Physical safety concerns
* Assault or threats
* Racism, discrimination, or hate speech
* Sexual harassment
* Human trafficking concerns
* Police misconduct
* Fraud or scams
* Theft
* Illegal activity
* Food poisoning or serious health concerns
* Weapons or violence
* Child safety concerns

These reviews can have significant consequences, so confirming authenticity is worthwhile.

---

### 2. Very Low Ratings + Detailed Accusations

Instead of "all 1-star reviews," I would use something like:

* **1- or 2-star review**
  **AND**
* Includes allegations against employees or owners
* Mentions discrimination or unsafe conditions
* Contains profanity directed at someone
* Uses accusatory language

A simple review like:

> "Food wasn't great. Wait was long."

should publish immediately.

Whereas:

> "The owner stole my credit card information."

should require verification.

---

### 3. Unusual Review Behavior

Hold reviews when your system detects things like:

* Brand-new account
* Account created today
* No profile photo
* First review ever
* Multiple reviews submitted in a short period
* Several businesses reviewed within minutes
* Same device reviewing many businesses
* Multiple accounts from the same IP (when appropriate to monitor)
* Possible bot behavior

---

### 4. Community Flags

If a published review is reported by multiple users, move it into a verification process while keeping a record of the report.

---

## Reviews that should NOT be held

These should generally publish immediately:

⭐ 3-star:

> "Food was okay."

⭐ 2-star:

> "Service was slow."

⭐ 1-star:

> "Wouldn't go back."

⭐ 5-star:

> "Amazing experience."

Opinions don't usually need verification.

---

## Trigger words

Rather than relying on specific words alone, use them as **signals** combined with other factors.

Examples include:

**Safety**

* unsafe
* attacked
* assaulted
* threatened
* followed
* robbed
* gun
* weapon
* fight
* violence

**Discrimination**

* racist
* discrimination
* profiling
* prejudice
* hate
* slur

**Crime**

* stole
* theft
* scam
* fraud
* counterfeit

**Health**

* poisoned
* contaminated
* mold
* infestation

**Harassment**

* harassed
* stalked
* touched
* assaulted

**Employment**

* retaliation
* hostile workplace
* discrimination
* harassment

The presence of these words should **increase the review's risk score**, not automatically block publication.

---

# I would create three moderation levels

### 🟢 Low Risk

Publish immediately.

Examples:

* Opinions
* Food quality
* Prices
* Cleanliness
* Wait times
* Customer service

---

### 🟡 Medium Risk

Publish, but send for moderator review in the background.

Examples:

* Mild discrimination concerns
* Poor professionalism
* Repeated complaints from different users

---

### 🔴 High Risk

Hold for verification before publishing.

Examples:

* Safety incidents
* Criminal accusations
* Hate crimes
* Human trafficking concerns
* Violence
* Sexual misconduct
* Doxxing
* Defamation risks

---

## One feature I'd add for Mapping with Melanin™

Because your platform emphasizes community trust, I'd consider displaying a small badge next to certain reviews, such as:

* ✓ **Verified Experience** — The reviewer completed identity verification.
* 📍 **Verified Visit** — The reviewer confirmed they were at the location (if you support location verification and users consent).
* 🛡️ **Safety Report Verified** — The review went through additional verification due to the nature of the claims.

This lets users weigh reviews based on the level of verification without suppressing honest experiences.

Finally, I'd establish a clear service goal. For example:

> **Most reviews are published immediately. Reviews requiring additional verification are typically reviewed within 24–48 hours.**

That sets expectations while reinforcing that the purpose is fairness and authenticity—not filtering out negative feedback.
