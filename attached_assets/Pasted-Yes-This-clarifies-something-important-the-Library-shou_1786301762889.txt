Yes. This clarifies something important: **the Library should not merely contain topics. It should be a continuously growing knowledge system, with KinfolkAI acting as the research and relevance engine behind it.**

The distinction you just made between **manual discovery** and **Kinfolk-assisted discovery** is also essential to your membership model.

### The intended model

A user should always be able to manually search MWM without spending Kinfolk usage. They can search “soul food,” “Fruity Pebble waffles,” “IVF,” “Kenya,” “Delta Sigma Theta,” “Black Catholic churches,” etc., and explore whatever MWM already contains.

**Kinfolk usage is different.** Kinfolk does the work *for* the person: researching, synthesizing, personalizing, connecting information across MWM, and explaining why something matters. That usage remains governed by the user's tier.

And the Library grows from both.

Your infertility → IVF example is a very good acceptance test because it forces Replit to prove that this isn't simply another search box.

There is one additional piece I would add architecturally: **a Book should not equal a webpage.** It should be a knowledge object.

That solves your D9 idea beautifully. “Divine Nine” can be a Book; each organization can be its own Book; an HBCU can be an entity connected to several Books; Philadelphia can be a geographic Book; IVF can simultaneously belong to infertility, reproductive health, insurance, and family-building collections. Nothing needs to be duplicated.

And there is an even bigger flywheel emerging:

**Search → demand signal → research → Library knowledge → entity connections → personalized Kinfolk answer → community contribution → better knowledge → better discovery → business/creator opportunity.**

That is much closer to what you've been describing than the current “Library topic + Follow button” implementation.
