04.16 Evidence Eligibility
Community evidence is eligible only when:
it relates to the evaluated object;
it falls within the relevant timeframe;
its source relationship is known or appropriately classified;
conflicts of interest are disclosed where applicable;
duplicate activity is removed;
coordinated manipulation is screened;
private evidence is used only as permitted;
the evidence is specific enough to interpret;
the evidence is not based solely on popularity;
the evidence does not rely on prohibited identity inference.
04.17 Evidence Relationship Types
Every evidence item must identify the contributor’s relationship to the evaluated object.
Relationship	Example
Direct participant	Attended event, used resource
Customer	Purchased from business
Resident	Lives in neighborhood
Visitor	Visited location
Employee	Works or worked for entity
Business owner	Owns evaluated or related business
Organizer	Organized event or initiative
Partner	Formal collaborator
Volunteer	Contributed time
Recipient	Received support or opportunity
Observer	Witnessed but did not directly participate
Institution representative	Represents organization
Cultural Ambassador	Promoted or interpreted experience
Platform moderator	Reviewed evidence
Unknown	Relationship cannot be established
Unknown relationships reduce confidence.
Interested-party relationships do not automatically disqualify evidence but require conflict-of-interest adjustment.
04.18 Community Signal Dictionary
Each community signal must be defined in the Belonging Signals™ Dictionary.
Launch-state signal groups include:
welcoming signals;
connection signals;
reciprocity signals;
support signals;
voice signals;
continuity signals;
stewardship signals;
exclusion signals;
extraction signals;
manipulation signals;
crisis-response signals;
growth signals;
institutional signals;
intergenerational signals.
Every signal definition must include:
signal ID;
name;
description;
eligible sources;
polarity;
maximum contribution;
decay schedule;
verification requirement;
conflict-of-interest rule;
applicable entity types;
context restrictions;
anti-gaming treatment;
public explanation eligibility.
04.19 Positive Community Indicators
Positive signals may increase one or more dimension scores.
Examples include:
newcomers welcomed;
questions answered;
referrals completed;
people return voluntarily;
businesses support one another;
local hiring occurs;
Cultural Ambassadors create measurable opportunity;
mentorship relationships continue;
resource needs are met;
community concerns lead to change;
historical knowledge is preserved;
community members become leaders;
events recur;
institutions collaborate;
support reaches people outside dominant social circles;
conflicts are repaired;
businesses respond constructively;
community members recommend one another;
economic value circulates locally;
people report feeling known rather than merely marketed to.
04.20 Community Risk Indicators
Risk indicators may reduce dimension scores or trigger review.
Examples include:
repeated gatekeeping;
exclusion of newcomers;
insider-only access;
harassment or retaliation;
extraction without return;
misleading community claims;
performative partnerships;
abandoned commitments;
suppression of criticism;
coordination to manipulate scores;
concentrated benefits;
cultural exploitation;
community displacement;
inaccessible resources;
public-facing diversity without internal influence;
unpaid labor expectations;
inconsistent treatment;
false urgency;
resource hoarding;
reputation management replacing accountability.
