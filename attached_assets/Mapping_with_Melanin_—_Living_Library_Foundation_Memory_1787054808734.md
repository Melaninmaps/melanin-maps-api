# Mapping with Melanin — Living Library Foundation Memory

The Living Library is a living, relational cultural knowledge system. It must never render a grid of blank cards, repeated generic feather icons, or a rigid static folder tree.

## Foundational domains

Seed and preserve these durable topics: Housing & Home; Education & Learning; Trades, Skills & Certifications; Health & Wellness; Money & Economic Mobility; Careers & Professional Life; Business & Entrepreneurship; Community Resources & Help; Culture & Heritage; Places & Our History; Travel & Exploration; Living & Relocation; Family & Relationships; Beauty, Hair & Personal Care; Food & Culinary Culture; Faith, Spirituality & Community Institutions; Arts, Music & Creative Culture; Entertainment & Social Life; Community & Connection; Safety & Navigating the World; Rights, Advocacy & Civic Life; Legal Information & Resources; Parenting, Youth & Future Generations; LGBTQIA+ Life & Community; Disability & Accessibility; Immigration, Migration & Diaspora Life; People, Stories & Living Memory; and Current Issues & Community Conversations.

The Start Here set must visibly include at minimum Housing, Education, Trades, Health, Money, Careers, Business, and Community Resources. Each card must have a readable title, short summary, matching MWM gold-outline topic icon, and an honest entry state such as “Explore this foundation” when no research has been stored yet.

## Knowledge model

Topics are durable domains. They are not a single-parent folder tree. A Library entry may link to multiple topics and carries reusable facets for who, goal, where, life stage, cultural context, need, experience, resource, and content type. Culture and identity modify subjects across the Library; they are not isolated into a single resource silo. Guides, businesses, professionals, events, organizations, historic places, personal stories, and research sources are content types, not top-level topic replacements.

Kinfolk must store source-cited research once and link it to every relevant topic and facet. It may add new evidence and context, but it must not fabricate a research entry merely to fill a card.

## Visual rule

The Living Library hero sits on a dark MWM surface and must have high-contrast ivory title text and readable warm-light supporting copy. Do not use low opacity or inherited dark text for the Living Library heading or introduction. All MWM topic icons use the subject-specific polished gold-outline registry; the feather remains a brand-level mark only.
