# My review of the P0 findings

## 1. Alert confirmation deduplication

Completely confirmed.

One person must never be able to confirm the same alert repeatedly until it becomes “community confirmed.” The audit found that a single user can increment the count three times and trigger the confirmed state and another push notification. 

### Decision

Every confirmation must create a unique record tied to:

* alert ID;
* member ID;
* timestamp;
* trust level at confirmation;
* approximate verification context, where appropriate.

There should be a unique constraint on:

```text
alertId + userId
```

One person. One confirmation. One clearing vote.

A higher-trust member may eventually contribute stronger evidence weight, but should not receive multiple votes.

---

## 2. Global push notifications from safety tips

This is both an abuse risk and an experience risk.

A safety tip should not automatically reach every member in the platform. The report identifies a global notification mechanism combined with no dedicated rate limiter. 

### Decision

Safety notifications require:

1. geographic relevance;
2. category relevance;
3. member opt-in;
4. severity threshold;
5. evidence or confidence threshold;
6. duplicate suppression;
7. notification cooldown;
8. emergency override controlled by authorized staff.

No unreviewed community tip should generate a global blast.

---

## 3. No Confidence Engine

This is the most important architectural finding in Audit 6.

The platform currently treats a score based on one observation as though it carries the same certainty as a score based on hundreds. 

That contradicts Section 06 of the Invisible Architecture™, where we explicitly designed separate Risk and Confidence scores.

### Decision

Every public safety or trust conclusion must contain at least:

```text
Signal or Risk Level
Confidence Level
Evidence Count
Evidence Recency
Evidence Type
Geographic Scope
Last Updated
```

Example:

> **Community concern: Moderate**
> **Confidence: Limited**
> Based on 4 recent community observations from 3 contributors.
> Conditions may vary by time, location, and personal circumstances.

A platform score without confidence is not complete.

---

## 4. No score explanation

Confirmed as P0.

The system reportedly presents safety ratings, trust levels, weighted ratings, and risk scores without explaining their evidence basis or meaning. 

### Decision

Every visible score must have a **“How was this determined?”** explanation.

That explanation should not reveal confidential anti-gaming thresholds, but it should communicate:

* what sources were considered;
* the general time period;
* whether evidence is community-reported, official, verified, or inferred;
* the amount and confidence of evidence;
* limitations;
* how to report an error or add context.

The platform does not need to expose the full algorithm to be meaningfully explainable.

---

## 5. Pending reports and reporter identity exposed publicly

This is an immediate privacy and retaliation concern.

The report says unauthenticated callers may retrieve pending reports and names of reporters who did not select anonymity. 

### Decision

Public endpoints may return only:

* approved or otherwise publishable reports;
* redacted information;
* generalized location where appropriate;
* no internal review status;
* no reporter identity.

Even when a member chooses a named report, that should not automatically mean their legal name is exposed publicly. We need separate choices:

```text
Anonymous to the public
Named by chosen display name
Identity available only to authorized moderators
```

Reporter identity remains protected from the accused party unless disclosure is legally required or expressly authorized.

---

## 6. Officer Watch submission integrity

I agree this is P0 because of the combination of:

* no dedicated rate limiter;
* no duplicate protection;
* named individuals;
* public records;
* unclear evidence standard. 

### Decision

Officer Watch should not operate as ordinary community posting.

Minimum submission requirements:

* authenticated account;
* verified email;
* account beyond the 48-hour trust period;
* dedicated rate limit;
* duplicate matching by name, badge, department, and jurisdiction;
* required source category;
* required explanation of firsthand versus public-record evidence;
* moderation before publication;
* legal review standard;
* correction and dispute process;
* version history;
* source links where legally and practically available.

I would not require every member to reach a paid trust level before reporting serious civic information. Payment should never determine whose safety concern may be heard. But higher-risk publication requires stronger verification before the platform labels it verified.

---

## 7. No accuracy disclaimer or evidence standard for Officer Watch

Confirmed.

“Verified” cannot simply mean “an administrator approved it.” The public needs to understand what was verified. 

### Decision

Use precise labels:

* **Submission Received**
* **Source Reviewed**
* **Official Record Located**
* **Community-Corroborated**
* **Unable to Independently Confirm**
* **Disputed**
* **Corrected**
* **Archived**

Avoid one generic **Verified** label.

A required public notice should explain:

> Information is presented for civic awareness and may include official records, documented reports, or community submissions. Each entry identifies its evidence status. Inclusion is not a judicial finding, and records may be corrected or disputed through the platform’s review process.

This wording will still need attorney review before launch.

# Founder Decisions I recommend resolving now

## FD-1: Minimum trust level for safety reports

My recommendation:

* Anyone may access emergency resources.
* Authenticated, email-verified members may submit ordinary safety observations after the 48-hour trust period.
* Immediate serious incidents may be submitted before the trust period, but they enter a high-scrutiny queue and do not automatically affect public scores.
* Anonymous public reporting can exist through a protected guest-report mechanism with CAPTCHA, strict rate limits, and no automatic public scoring.
* Reports that name a person, business, employer, or institution require greater evidence and human review.

This preserves access without allowing unverified reports to directly control public conclusions.

## FD-2: Minimum survey count

My recommendation:

* **1–4:** Insufficient evidence; no public score.
* **5–19:** Emerging community signal; low confidence.
* **20–49:** Moderate confidence.
* **50+:** Higher confidence, still qualified by recency, diversity, and geography.

A simple count is only the starting point. Five reports from one person are not five independent observations.

## FD-3: Anti-coordination standard

For launch:

* unique actor counting;
* unique confirmation per alert;
* IP/device velocity signals used internally;
* account-age and verification context;
* duplicate narrative detection;
* notification thresholds require distinct actors;
* suspicious clusters pause automatic escalation;
* admin review before citywide or business-owner notification when manipulation risk is elevated.

## FD-4: Officer Watch evidence requirement

My recommendation:

A source URL should be required **unless** the submission is categorized as a firsthand confidential report.

For firsthand reports:

* source URL may be unavailable;
* supporting documents or details may be submitted privately;
* publication waits for corroboration or sufficient review;
* no public “verified misconduct” statement without an identifiable evidence basis.

## FD-5: KinfolkAI safety authority

KinfolkAI may explain platform safety data but should not independently declare a place safe or unsafe.

Approved response structure:

> “Mapping With Melanin™ currently shows an emerging community concern in this area, based on six recent reports. Confidence is limited, and conditions may vary by time and exact location. Here are the reported concerns and official resources available.”

Prohibited:

> “This neighborhood is safe.”

> “Avoid this business.”

> “You will be fine walking there.”

## FD-6: Sundown designation

A “sundown town” classification should never arise from a single community selection.

I recommend a specialized evidence pathway:

* historical evidence;
* recognized research or archival source;
* recent documented experiences;
* legal review;
* careful distinction between historical classification and current reports;
* contextual explanation;
* correction and dispute process.

The platform may discuss documented history and current community concerns, but should not casually assign this term as a standard report category.

# Two P1 findings I would elevate

## Decision Ledger™

I understand why Replit classified the missing Decision Ledger™ as P1, but for any safety moderation that goes live, a minimum audit ledger is effectively P0.

The report correctly notes that trust changes, safety-rating changes, and moderation decisions are not stored in a durable, queryable decision record. 

For Day 1, we do not need the entire future Decision Ledger architecture. We do need a **Minimum Decision Record** containing:

* decision ID;
* subject type and ID;
* action;
* prior state;
* new state;
* moderator;
* timestamp;
* reason category;
* evidence references;
* notification status;
* appeal eligibility;
* later correction or reversal.

Without this, appeals and accountability cannot be responsibly implemented.

## Moderation SLA

Critical safety reports cannot sit indefinitely.

I recommend:

* immediate emergency disclaimer directing users to 911 or relevant emergency services;
* critical reports highlighted instantly;
* defined review target;
* escalation when overdue;
* weekend and overnight operating policy;
* no promise of real-time review unless staff coverage exists.

The platform should state clearly that it is not an emergency-response service.
