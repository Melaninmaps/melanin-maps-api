I would approve this **as the smallest launch gate**, with two cautions.

First, requiring DOB and rejecting registrations under 13 is technically straightforward and consistent with your current launch position that the app is **not intended for children under 13**. COPPA generally requires verifiable parental consent when a service directed to children—or one with actual knowledge—collects personal information from a child under 13. ([Federal Trade Commission][1])

Second, this does **not** complete your broader child/teen compliance work. Apple expects apps capable of collecting or sharing minors’ personal information—such as names, email, location, photos, videos, or chat—to have an appropriate privacy policy and comply with applicable children’s privacy laws. ([Apple Developer][2]) Google also requires accurate target-audience declarations and imposes additional Families requirements when children are included in the target audience. ([Google Help][3])

## One technical improvement before approval

The current age formula:

```ts
(Date.now() - dob.getTime()) / (1000 * 60 * 60 * 24 * 365.25)
```

is usually close, but birthdays should be calculated by calendar date—not an approximate year length. Near a user’s 13th birthday, timezone and leap-year effects could produce an incorrect result.

Ask Replit to use a calendar comparison instead:

* reject future dates;
* reject implausible ages over 120;
* compare year, month, and day to determine whether the person has reached age 13;
* normalize the date consistently to avoid timezone shifts.
