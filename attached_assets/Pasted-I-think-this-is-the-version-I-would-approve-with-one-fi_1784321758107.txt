# Presentation Style Guide

## 1. Overall Feeling

Every slide should feel:

* Human before technical
* Confident before promotional
* Warm before corporate
* Premium before trendy
* Inspirational before informational

The audience should feel like they're hearing from a trusted friend—not watching a software demo.

---

## 2. Typography

Typography should create emotion.

Suggested hierarchy:

### Headlines

Large.

Minimal.

No more than one primary thought per slide.

### Supporting text

Conversational.

Easy to read aloud.

Never written like marketing copy.

### Body copy

Keep paragraphs short.

Use white space generously.

Avoid walls of text.

---

## 3. Color Philosophy

Use color intentionally—not decoratively.

Primary palette:

* Warm Brown
* Cream
* Gold

Use black intentionally for emotional pauses (such as the KinfolkAI™ reveal).

White space should communicate calm and confidence.

---

## 4. Screenshots

Screenshots should support the story—not become the story.

Never allow a screenshot to dominate the emotional message.

Every screenshot should answer:

> "Why does this matter?"

rather than

> "What feature is this?"

---

## 5. Icons & Custom Emoji

Whenever possible, prioritize our own visual language over generic icons.

If custom Mapping With Melanin™ icons or emoji exist for a concept, use those instead of standard emoji.

Generic emoji should only be used when they reinforce—not distract from—the presentation.

Consistency is more important than decoration.

---

## 6. Motion

Animations should feel cinematic.

Not flashy.

Preferred motion:

* Fade
* Slow reveal
* Gentle movement
* Intentional pauses

Avoid:

* Bouncing
* Spinning
* Overly playful transitions
* Anything that feels like a PowerPoint effect

---

## 7. Photography

Photography should feel authentic.

Real people.

Real emotion.

Real community.

Avoid stock-photo energy whenever possible.

---

## 8. Copywriting

Every slide should answer one question:

> Why does this matter?

Never explain a feature before explaining why someone should care.

---

## 9. Slide Rhythm

Alternate emotional intensity.

For example:

Emotion

↓

Information

↓

Emotion

↓

Proof

↓

Pause

↓

Inspiration

The deck should breathe.

---

## 10. Voice

Everything should sound like a conversation.

Not a pitch.

Not a sales presentation.

Not corporate.

If a sentence doesn't sound natural when spoken aloud, rewrite it.

The audience should feel like they're hearing from someone who genuinely wants to help—not someone trying to convince them.
