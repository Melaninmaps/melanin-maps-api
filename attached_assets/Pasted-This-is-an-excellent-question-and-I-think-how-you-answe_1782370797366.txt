This is an excellent question, and I think **how you answer it will shape the credibility of Mapping with Melanin™.**

My recommendation is:

> **Businesses should have influence over videos, but they should not have unilateral control over them.**

If businesses could simply delete any negative video, users would quickly lose trust in the platform. On the other hand, businesses should have a fair opportunity to respond to inaccurate, misleading, or policy-violating content.

## Here's the system I would build.

### Videos are community content

If I visit a restaurant and upload a video of my experience, **I own that content** (subject to your platform license).

The business cannot simply remove it because they don't like it.

---

## Businesses can respond publicly

Every video should have a business response option.

For example:

**Video**

> "The wait was 45 minutes."

**Business Response**

> "Thank you for your feedback. We were unexpectedly short-staffed that evening. We've since added additional staff and hope you'll consider visiting us again."

This demonstrates accountability and lets future customers see both perspectives.

---

## Businesses can report content

Businesses should be able to report a video for reasons such as:

* False information
* Harassment
* Hate speech
* Threats
* Nudity or explicit content
* Copyright infringement
* Spam
* The creator was never a customer (where that can be reasonably demonstrated)

Those reports would go through moderation—not automatic removal.

---

## Businesses can request a review

If a business believes a video violates your policies, they can request that Mapping with Melanin™ review it.

Possible outcomes:

* No violation → Video remains.
* Minor issue → Creator is asked to edit the video or caption.
* Clear violation → Video is removed.
* Repeated violations → Creator may receive warnings or lose posting privileges.

---

## A feature I think would make Mapping with Melanin™ stand out

You've already talked about giving businesses the opportunity to correct issues.

Imagine this flow:

1. Customer uploads a negative video.
2. Business responds.
3. Business resolves the issue.
4. Customer returns.
5. Customer updates the video or posts a follow-up.

You could then display a badge like:

> **Issue Resolved**

That tells users the business took action rather than ignoring the feedback.

---

## One thing I would avoid

I would **not** allow businesses to pay to remove reviews or videos.

That can undermine trust very quickly.

---

## My recommendation for control

| Action            | Business | User |        Platform       |
| ----------------- | :------: | :--: | :-------------------: |
| Upload video      |     ❌    |   ✅  |           ❌           |
| Delete own video  |     ❌    |   ✅  | ✅ (policy violations) |
| Respond publicly  |     ✅    |   ❌  |           ❌           |
| Report a video    |     ✅    |   ✅  |           ❌           |

# One feature I'd strongly recommend

Create a **Business Resolution Center**.

When a business addresses a complaint, they can mark it as **Resolved** and send the reviewer a notification inviting them to revisit or update their review. The reviewer remains in control—they can:

* Leave the original review unchanged.
* Edit it.
* Add a follow-up.
* Mark the issue as resolved.

That keeps the platform balanced. Businesses have a chance to make things right, reviewers keep ownership of their experience, and future customers get a fuller picture of how the business responds to feedback.

In my opinion, **how a business responds to criticism is often as valuable as the criticism itself**. A thoughtful, timely response can increase trust, while silence or hostility can be just as informative to potential customers. That philosophy fits well with the community-first approach you've been building for Mapping with Melanin™.
