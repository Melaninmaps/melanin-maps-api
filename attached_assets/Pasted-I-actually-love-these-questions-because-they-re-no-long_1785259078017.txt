I actually love these questions because they're no longer engineering questions—they're **founder philosophy** questions. These decisions become part of Mapping With Melanin™'s product DNA. Here's how I would answer them if we were writing the "Kinfolk Constitution."

---

# 1. Community Voice Names

**Current Proposal**

* Professional
* Warm
* Local
* Home

### My recommendation

I think these are close, but I'd refine them slightly because they describe *how Kinfolk feels*, not *how it speaks*.

* **Professional** — polished, business, interviews, formal planning.
* **Friendly** (instead of Warm) — approachable, encouraging, conversational.
* **Local** — sounds like someone who knows the city well.
* **Home** — sounds like someone who grew up there and wants you to feel welcome.

The progression is easy to understand.

Professional → Friendly → Local → Home

Importantly, **Home is not "more slang."** It is more familiarity, warmth, and cultural depth.

---

# 2. Cultural Language Names

Current:

* Standard
* Community-informed
* Full local authenticity

I think these are very strong.

I'd make only one small adjustment:

* **Standard**
* **Community-Informed**
* **Community Native**

Why?

Because "authenticity" can imply there is only one authentic way to speak.

"Community Native" says:

> This reflects how people commonly speak in this community.

without implying everyone speaks identically.

---

# 3. Profanity

This is where I have a stronger opinion.

**Membership should NEVER determine profanity.**

A Premium user should not automatically receive stronger language than a Free user.

Instead:

Profanity is a **personal preference**.

Default:

**Off**

Choices:

* None
* Mild
* Explicit

Who defines Mild?

Not AI.

A curated language list maintained by your moderation team.

For example:

Mild:

* damn
* hell
* crap

Explicit:

Everything stronger.

No dynamic guessing.

# 5. Cultural Journey

Should Kinfolk remember?

YES.

But...

It should be:

**Opt-in.**

Why?

Some people won't want a record of every community they've explored.

I'd make it like Spotify Wrapped.

"Your Cultural Journey"

only if enabled.

# 6. Layer Two Review Queue

This one is huge.

I think Cultural Ambassadors should **not** be celebrities.

They should be trusted community members.

Examples:

* historians
* educators
* artists
* neighborhood leaders
* librarians
* business owners
* long-term residents
* nonprofit leaders

I'd actually require multiple perspectives for significant changes so no one person becomes "the voice" of a community.

---

# 7. "Things You'll Hear"

Should this replace Local Terms?

No.

Absolutely not.

Think of it like this:

Layer One

**Local Terms**

Definitions.

History.

Context.

Example:

"The AUC"

means

Atlanta University Center.

---

Layer Two

**Things You'll Hear**

Natural examples.

"You'll probably hear people mention 'the AUC' when talking about Spelman, Morehouse, Clark Atlanta, and the surrounding area."

One is reference.

One is experience.

Both should exist.

---

# 8. Code-switching

This one deserves careful handling.

I would not make it completely automatic.

Instead:

Ask during onboarding:

> "Would you like Kinfolk to naturally adjust its communication style based on your conversation and context?"

Choices:

* Always
* Ask me
* Never

People should remain in control.

# One Question I'd Add

I think there is one founder decision missing, and it may be the most important of all.

## Should Kinfolk ever claim to speak *as* a community?

My answer is:

**No.**

Kinfolk should always speak **with respect for a community**, never **on behalf of it**.

That distinction protects the product from becoming performative.

Instead of saying:

> "This is how Black Atlanta speaks."

Kinfolk should communicate more like:

> "Here's a phrase you might hear in Atlanta, especially in certain neighborhoods or among some communities. If you're curious, I'd be happy to tell you where it comes from."

That keeps Kinfolk in the role you've always envisioned: **a trusted guide introducing people to communities, not a character impersonating them.**

---

## The principle I'd put at the top of the Kinfolk governance document

> **Kinfolk's purpose is to help people feel welcomed, understood, and connected. It celebrates communities by preserving their history, honoring their voices, and inviting respectful exploration. It never reduces a community to stereotypes, never assumes identity based on appearance or background, and never claims to speak for a culture. Instead, it learns alongside communities through consent, historical accuracy, and ongoing stewardship.**

Reading through everything we've worked on, I think this captures what you've been trying to build from the beginning. It's not just a smarter travel assistant—it's a cultural companion whose job is to help people discover places and communities with curiosity, context, and respect.
