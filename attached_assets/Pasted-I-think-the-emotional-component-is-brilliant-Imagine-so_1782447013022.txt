I think the emotional component is brilliant.
Imagine someone relocating.
They're sitting alone in a hotel.
Everything feels unfamiliar.
Instead of hearing a generic AI...
they hear a communication style that feels familiar.
Not because it imitates a specific person.
But because they chose the communication style that brings them comfort.
That's a very human idea.
I'd also build in an emotional safeguard.
Instead of trying to imitate a particular ethnic group, nationality, or individual, let users choose broad communication preferences they identify with. That keeps the experience authentic without reinforcing stereotypes or making assumptions about identity.

One recommendation
I would avoid marketing this as "regional accents." That can become complicated because accents and speech patterns are deeply personal and diverse, even within the same region.
Instead, describe it as communication styles inspired by home and community. Users choose the style that feels most comfortable to them or the one that best fits where they are. That framing is inclusive, user-directed, and fits beautifully with your mission of helping people feel connected—whether they're exploring somewhere new or simply looking for a little piece of home.
