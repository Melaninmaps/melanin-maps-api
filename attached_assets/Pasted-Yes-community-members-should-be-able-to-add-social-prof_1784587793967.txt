Yes—community members should be able to add social profiles, but it should be **optional, privacy-controlled, and different from the business experience**.

A person may want to share their Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, LinkedIn, portfolio, blog, or business page so others can understand who they are or follow their work. But the platform should never pressure members to connect outside accounts, especially minors or people participating in sensitive groups.

## Recommended media structure

### Every member, including free users

Every user should be able to:

* upload one profile photo;
* replace or remove it;
* control who can see it;
* report impersonation or misuse;
* keep the default avatar if they prefer not to upload one.

A profile photo should **never require a paid tier**.

### Community-member profiles

Optional social links:

* Instagram
* TikTok
* YouTube
* LinkedIn
* personal website or portfolio
* approved additional platform

Recommended controls:

* Show on my public profile
* Show only to connections
* Keep private
* Open links externally with a warning
* Allow messages from people who find me through my profile

For adults, a limited number of social links can be included by tier. For minors, I would default social links to hidden and require the approved parental-control rules before allowing them publicly.

Community members should also be able to link a social video to a post, but:

* it should not autoplay;
* it should show the original platform and creator;
* the platform should validate the URL;
* age/viewer-rating rules must apply;
* users must be able to report it;
* the app should not download or rehost copyrighted media without permission.

### Businesses

Businesses need a richer media system:

* logo;
* cover image;
* profile gallery;
* featured introduction video;
* additional external social videos;
* Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Facebook, LinkedIn and website links;
* optional booking, menu, storefront or ordering links.

Owner-provided videos should be labeled:

> **From the Business**

Community-contributed videos should be labeled:

> **Shared by the Community**

Those should never be mixed together without attribution.

### Suggested tier logic

The exact limits can be adjusted after Replit audits existing membership tiers, but the structure could be:

| Account                   |                               Images |              External videos |                Social links |
| ------------------------- | -----------------------------------: | ---------------------------: | --------------------------: |
| Community Free            |  Profile photo + limited post images |       Limited links in posts |                         1–2 |
| Paid Community            |             More post/media capacity | More saved or featured media |                  More links |
| Community-Listed Business |  Community images only until claimed |       Community-linked media | Official links not editable |
| Claimed Basic Business    |           Logo, cover, small gallery |             1 featured video |               Core profiles |
| Premium Business          |                       Larger gallery |     Multiple featured videos |             Full social set |
| Highest Business Tier     | Expanded media, campaigns and events |         Larger video library |    Full links and analytics |

The limits should apply to **storage and promotion benefits**, not basic dignity or identity. Profile photos should remain available to everyone.

## Navigation recommendation

The screenshot you shared is a classic slide-out drawer. It is cleaner than the crowded bottom bar, but I would use a **hybrid approach** rather than hiding everything.

Keep only the most frequently needed destinations at the bottom:

* Discovery
* Map
* Safety
* Community

Use a menu icon in the upper-left for:

* Events
* My Knowledge
* Messages
* Saved places
* Membership
* Business Dashboard
* Profile
* Notifications
* Settings
* Help
* Log Out

That gives iPhone users more screen space without making essential safety and discovery tools harder to reach. The drawer should match your own visual system; the reference image should guide the interaction, not be copied.

We cannot honestly **guarantee** them before testing, but we can require proof through a build gate.

## My recommendation on timing

This is now larger than a single quick patch. The safest route is:

1. Repair P0 stability first.
2. Implement media in contained batches.
3. Implement the navigation drawer.
4. Run one full regression.
5. Cut one new final candidate for both stores.
6. Test it physically.
7. Submit immediately after it passes.

That is faster than building repeatedly because it avoids discovering a new conflict after every store upload. The next build should be treated as the **corrected launch candidate**, not merely another temporary patch.
