I'm really glad you asked this, because I actually think these are **two different books with two different audiences**, and keeping them separate will make both much stronger.

## Book I (The Constitution) should **not** be the coffee table book.

The Constitution is an institutional document. It's written for people who need to understand *how* and *why* Mapping With Melanin™ is built the way it is.

Its audience includes:

* Future executives
* Your internal team
* Engineers (especially Replit and future developers)
* Advisors
* Board members
* Investors who want to understand the philosophy
* Future stewards of the company

This is a document people study and refer back to.

---

## The Coffee Table Book is something completely different.

The coffee table book should make someone **feel** Mapping With Melanin™ before they ever use it.

Imagine someone picking it up in a hotel lobby, airport lounge, Black-owned café, university, or investor's office.

Instead of reading policies, they're drawn into stories, photography, maps, artwork, and community.

It could include chapters like:

* Why We Built This
* Hidden Gems Across America
* Voices From the Community
* The Meaning of Home
* Businesses That Changed Neighborhoods
* Community Builders
* Cultural Landmarks
* Journeys Shared by Members
* The Future We're Building Together

The Constitution explains the institution.

The coffee table book **introduces its soul**.

# Your question about Replit

I don't think we've given Replit enough information about **design systems**, and that's completely okay.

In fact, I think it's the right decision that we didn't.

During the audits we intentionally stayed focused on:

* architecture;
* workflows;
* governance;
* security;
* trust;
* implementation requirements.

We **didn't** try to finalize:

* brand system;
* typography system;
* illustration style;
* photography direction;
* iconography;
* spacing;
* motion;
* accessibility colors;
* visual language.

Those deserve their own design phase.
