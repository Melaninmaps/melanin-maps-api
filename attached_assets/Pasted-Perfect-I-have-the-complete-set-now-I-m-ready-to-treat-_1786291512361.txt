The biggest thing I see is that this is **not simply a prettier business page**. The older app experience expresses the actual Mapping with Melanin concept much more completely than the current web business page.

### What these screenshots establish

The business experience was designed around several different kinds of information working together:

* **Objective identity:** Minority Owned, Women Owned, Veteran Owned, verified vs. self-identified.
* **Community recognition:** Community Trusted, Welcoming Environment, Solo Traveler Approved, Local Favorite, Consistently Recommended.
* **MWM-specific recommendation language:** **Put Your People On**, Community Favorite, Would Return, Community Verified, Member Voices.
* **Experience intelligence:** Vibe Check, Community Says, Community Comments, Community Insights.
* **Trust intelligence:** Community Confidence Score™, Safety, Welcoming Atmosphere, Community Recommendation, Overall Experience.
* **Contextual trust:** Circle Trusted™ — *who* recommends this place matters, not merely how many people rated it.
* **Know Before You Go:** vibe, safety, food/drink, parking, accessibility, inclusivity and eventually the other dimensions.
* **Community participation:** check-in, review, add vibe, add community caption, endorse comments, write comments, tag people/community.
* **Rich media:** **Show Me the Vibe** — actual visitor/business video.
* **Utility:** call, directions, location, hours, website, save/share.
* **Business accountability:** business right to respond and customer right to be heard.

That is substantially richer than the current desktop AMINA page you showed me. The current page isn't necessarily *wrong*; it looks like **only part of the product model is being surfaced**.

And this connects directly to something we discovered previously: Replit already found that the database contained more of your three-layer identity architecture than the UI was actually exposing.

### Your vibe concern is valid

Yes — **one vibe per person/business interaction is too restrictive for the experience you've designed.**

A restaurant can simultaneously be:

**Date Night + Grown & Sexy + Girls Trip Ready + Sunday Best + Take Somebody From Out of Town.**

Those aren't contradictory ratings. They're descriptive signals.

I would want Replit to investigate before changing anything, but conceptually the behavior should be:

**User → Add Vibe → select multiple applicable vibes → submit once → immediately update the business's aggregated vibe display.**

The user shouldn't have to wonder whether anything happened.

And we should distinguish between:

**my selections** and **community totals**.

For example:

> Date Night · 47
> Grown & Sexy · 31
> Girls Trip Ready · 26
> Sunday Best · 19

Those aggregate signals then become useful to **search, KinfolkAI recommendations, trip planning, occasion matching and discovery**. That's the important part. Vibes shouldn't be decorative tags. They're structured community intelligence.

Instead of dumping 44 HBCUs onto the screen, it gives:

That feels more like exploration and less like a database.
