Yes. We should give Replit a **sequenced implementation roadmap with gates**, so these features do not sit indefinitely as “future ideas,” but also do not destabilize launch.

I would not tie every feature to a user count alone. Some should be **before public launch**, some after stability is proven, and some only after enough real community data exists to make them useful.

Use this structure:

## Before public launch

These are foundational and should be in place before broad release:

* Standard greeting: **“Kinfolk’s here. Let’s map it out.”**
* Universal question support
* Compassionate follow-up behavior
* Existing platform enrichment
* Honest limitations and safety boundaries
* Tester prompts
* Clear privacy controls for memory and personalization
* Cross-platform consistency on iOS and Android
* Basic logging for errors, latency, and failed responses

These are not the full Opportunity Engine. They are the minimum needed for testers to experience the concept honestly.

## After launch stability, not user-count dependent

Build these after the first stable release has been live long enough to prove:

* login is stable;
* sessions persist;
* crash rates are acceptable;
* KinfolkAI responses are reliable;
* support issues are manageable;
* users understand the current experience.

I would use a **2–4 week stable-production gate**, rather than waiting for a particular number of users.

At that stage:

* Weather connected to saved outdoor events
* Better source labeling
* Improved contextual suggestions
* Feedback controls such as “helpful,” “not relevant,” and “don’t use this context”
* Permission-aware memory controls
* Better application, résumé, writing, and budgeting workflows

## After enough real platform data exists

These features require actual community density. Building them too early would produce weak or misleading results.

Trigger them when a city or category meets minimum data thresholds, for example:

* at least 50–100 active local members;
* at least 20–30 meaningful community contributions;
* at least 10 verified businesses in a category or city;
* enough reports to anonymize and aggregate without exposing individuals.

Then build:

* Community Memory
* Community trend summaries
* Neighborhood and business experience synthesis
* Tutor, mentor, scholarship, and resource recommendations grounded in verified listings
* City-readiness intelligence

The exact thresholds should be tested rather than treated as permanent numbers.

## After trust and safety validation

These should wait until privacy, consent, moderation, and audit controls are proven:

* Progressive Assistance across months
* Proactive financial or life-planning suggestions
* Sensitive barrier detection
* Cross-domain recommendations involving health, legal, financial, family, or safety circumstances
* Autonomous notifications based on personal history

These should require explicit user permission, visible controls, and auditability.
