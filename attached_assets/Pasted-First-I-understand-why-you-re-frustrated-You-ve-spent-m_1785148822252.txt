First, I understand why you're frustrated. You've spent months getting to this point, you're five days from leaving, and you've already solved what felt like the Apple Sign-In problem once. Seeing another 2.1 rejection is exhausting.

That said, **this rejection is actually much more actionable than some of the earlier ones.** Apple has told us exactly what failed.

## Apple is reporting two separate issues

### 1. Apple Sign-In registration failed

This is the important one.

Apple says:

> **"Error message appeared when tapped on Apple login and tried to register a new account."**

Notice what they did **not** say.

They did **not** say:

* Apple Sign-In is improperly configured.
* Sign in with Apple entitlement is missing.
* Apple authentication is rejected.
* Invalid client ID.
* Invalid redirect URI.

Instead they said:

> They tapped **Sign in with Apple** → attempted to register → **your app displayed an error message.**

That means one of these is most likely happening:

* Apple authentication succeeded, but your backend failed creating the account.
* Account creation failed after authentication.
* Registration validation failed.
* The app couldn't reach the API.
* A required field is missing.
* iPad-specific UI flow failed.
* Existing-account/new-account logic broke.
* Session creation failed.
* An exception is being caught and displayed as a generic error.

Those are very different from an Apple configuration problem.

---

### 2. Apple cannot review the app

This one is actually expected.

They wrote:

> "Provide a username and password."

Because your app requires authentication, Apple needs a way into the app.

If your app currently only exposes:

* Waitlist
* Registration
* Login

they still need an account they can use.

They are essentially saying:

> "We cannot verify everything because we cannot access every account type."

That doesn't necessarily mean every feature must be enabled. It means Apple needs whatever credentials are appropriate for the functionality that exists today.

# The iPad matters

This is new.

Apple tested on:

> **iPad Air 11-inch (M3)**

You and your testers have mostly been using phones.

That immediately raises questions:

* Is Apple Sign-In different on iPad?
* Is layout hiding something?
* Is Safe Area behaving differently?
* Is a required button off-screen?
* Is registration behaving differently on iPad?
* Is there an orientation issue?

This is why I wanted Build 97 to include multi-device testing.

## My recommendation tonight

Don't let Replit start making broad changes.

Have them perform a **forensic investigation**.

I would ask for:

* the exact error message Apple would have seen,
* the API response,
* server logs,
* client logs,
* whether the Apple token reached the backend,
* whether the user record was created,
* whether the session was created,
* whether the failure is iPad-specific,
* and exactly why the bug escaped previous testing.

Once we know that, the fix is usually much smaller than trying to "rewrite authentication."

We've been deliberately building an audit-first process over the past week. This is exactly the moment where that discipline pays off: identify the precise failure, fix only that failure, verify it on both iPhone and iPad, and then resubmit with confidence.
