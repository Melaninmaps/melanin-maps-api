## And yes — I strongly like your Philly “jawn” idea

But I would make this **another localization layer**, not allow Kinfolk to freely rewrite your master tags.

I would call the user-facing section something like **“What Locals Are Saying”**.

That gives Kinfolk freedom to sound local without corrupting the structured data underneath.

For example, the underlying structured endorsement could remain:

**Ask For The Special**

But in Philadelphia, an approved local expression might surface alongside it:

> **Philly:** “Order the special — you won't regret that jawn.”

The database still understands **Ask For The Special**. The local phrase is presentation/community flavor.

That distinction is crucial.

Kinfolk should **learn local language from community contributions and approved language patterns**, not simply decide that everyone in Philadelphia says *jawn*. There is a difference between sounding culturally aware and turning a community into a caricature.

So I would establish a model like:

**Core tag:** `worth_the_drive`
**Standard label:** Worth The Drive
**Philadelphia local expression:** “Yeah, make that drive.”
**Community/local attribution:** Philly locals
**Status:** community-approved
**Minimum usage/approval threshold:** whatever we establish
**Meaning stays identical:** worth making the trip

And another:

**Core tag:** `ask_for_the_special`
**Standard:** Ask For The Special
**Philadelphia:** “Get the special. Trust me, that jawn hits.”

Miami might develop completely different phrasing. New Orleans would have its own language. Houston could have its own. New York absolutely would. The Caribbean diaspora within New York could develop variations that differ from Black American New York language.

That becomes incredibly interesting because Kinfolk could eventually say:

> **What locals are saying:** “Don't leave without the oxtail. That jawn sells out.”

rather than presenting that as an official business claim.

### I would put four guardrails around Kinfolk's local language

**First, locality is not identity.** Kinfolk can know a business is in Philadelphia. It cannot conclude that a particular user is Black, Puerto Rican, Dominican, etc. because they're standing in Philadelphia.

**Second, community language should be learned, not fabricated.** Let members submit phrases through something like **“How would you say it?”** Kinfolk can cluster similar submissions and suggest a local rendering after sufficient community use.

**Third, the structured meaning never changes.** `worth_the_drive` stays `worth_the_drive`, whether the UI says *Worth The Drive*, *Make that drive*, or eventually a community-approved local expression.

**Fourth, Kinfolk can suggest; the community validates.** For a new city with limited data, Kinfolk could generate a **candidate** local phrase, but it should not become an official “locals say” phrase until people from that market approve/use it.

That could become another real moat for the platform: **Google can tell you a restaurant has 4.7 stars. Mapping With Melanin could eventually tell you what people praise, why they trust it, what kind of night it is, and how the neighborhood itself talks about it.**

And I especially like the label **“What Locals Are Saying”** because it makes clear that this isn't Kinfolk pretending to speak for Philadelphia. **Kinfolk is organizing and surfacing the language Philadelphia actually gave us.**
