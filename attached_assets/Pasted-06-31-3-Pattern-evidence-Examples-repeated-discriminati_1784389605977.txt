06.31.3 Pattern evidence
Examples:
repeated discrimination;
recurring event failures;
sustained harassment pattern;
chronic accessibility failure.
Pattern active:       100%
6 months inactive:     85%
1 year inactive:       65%
2 years inactive:      45%
3–5 years inactive:    25%
Over 5 years:          Historical relevance only
06.31.4 Severe verified harm
Verified severe incidents may remain persistent historical evidence.
Their current recommendation influence may decay, but they remain available to:
Governance Engine™;
Legacy & Learning Engine™;
Decision Ledger™;
appeals and accountability review;
pattern detection.
06.32 Corrective Action and Recovery
An entity may reduce current concern through verified corrective action.
Corrective evidence may include:
policy change;
staff removal or discipline;
accessibility repair;
improved emergency procedures;
independent review;
community accountability;
restitution;
documented training;
leadership change;
sustained incident-free operation;
public correction;
verified complaint resolution.
Corrective action does not delete the original event.
It may reduce current concern when:
the response directly addresses the issue;
implementation is verified;
sufficient time has passed to observe results;
no retaliation occurred;
affected community experience supports improvement.
06.33 Pattern Detection
A pattern may be established when:
multiple independent reports describe materially similar conduct;
reports occur across separate time periods;
reports involve more than one affected person;
contextual or institutional evidence supports the pattern;
anti-manipulation checks pass;
similarity is not solely copied language.
06.33.1 Baseline pattern thresholds
Pattern status	Minimum evidence
Emerging pattern	2 qualified independent reports
Meaningful pattern	3 qualified independent reports
Established pattern	5 qualified reports across at least 2 time periods
Severe verified pattern	One authoritative finding or multiple strongly verified reports
These thresholds guide review.
They do not replace judgment for severe immediate harm.
06.34 Single-Report Rule
A single report may:
enter the monitoring system;
affect confidence;
trigger private review;
initiate follow-up;
activate emergency routing;
temporarily hold promotion;
trigger a narrowly tailored safety precaution.
A single unverified report ordinarily may not:
publicly label an entity unsafe;
permanently suppress search visibility;
establish a broad pattern;
produce a permanent recognition penalty;
be disclosed as verified fact.
Exceptions apply for official emergency notices, verified severe incidents, or immediate threats.
06.35 Safety Override Levels
The Safety Intelligence Engine™ supports three BIF override levels.
Level 1 — Critical Override
Activated when:
immediate danger exists;
official emergency alert applies;
verified severe threat is active;
critical infrastructure failure creates immediate risk;
a credible emergency request is received.
Effects may include:
ordinary ranking suspended;
recommendation blocked;
emergency routing activated;
alert issued;
human review initiated;
audit priority increased.
Level 2 — High-Risk Override
Activated when:
strong evidence supports significant concern;
multiple serious reports corroborate;
a meaningful active pattern exists;
protective capacity is inadequate;
recommendation could expose users to material harm.
Effects may include:
recommendation blocked or restricted;
search result labeled cautiously;
promotion suspended;
human review required;
targeted alert considered.
Level 3 — Non-Override Concern
Activated when:
concern is meaningful but not sufficiently established for a full override;
evidence is emerging;
issue is context-specific;
protective capacity materially reduces risk.
Effects may include:
ranking adjustment;
explanation qualifier;
additional information request;
monitoring;
context-specific caution.
