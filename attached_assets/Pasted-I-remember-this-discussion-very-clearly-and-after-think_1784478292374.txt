# 2. The coffee-table format

This is where I actually changed my opinion.

When we first discussed it, I thought:

> Maybe.

Now I think:

> Yes.

But **not portrait**.

---

## Why not portrait?

Because your presentation will live in several places:

* Zoom
* Projectors
* TVs
* Investor meetings
* Conferences

Portrait creates black bars everywhere.

---

## Instead...

I would use something closer to a printed editorial spread.

Slightly taller than 16:9.

Much larger margins.

More whitespace.

Less content.

The audience should feel like every slide could be printed on heavy paper and framed.

## One thing I would adjust from what we wrote

The only place where my thinking has evolved is this:

Earlier we were asking:

> "Should we change the page dimensions?"

Now I think the stronger question is:

> **"Can we create the feeling of a luxury editorial book without sacrificing presentation compatibility?"**

I believe the answer is yes.

Instead of changing the canvas, I would change **how the canvas is used**:

* Wider margins.
* More negative space.
* Larger photography.
* Fewer words.
* Stronger typography hierarchy.
* Consistent editorial grid.
* Intentional cream "chapter break" pages.
* One signature brown.
* One signature gold.
* One signature cream.

The audience won't remember the aspect ratio.

They **will** remember how the presentation made them feel.

And if we achieve that, people won't describe it as "a really nice slide deck."

They'll say:

> **"It felt like someone was turning the pages of a beautifully crafted book."**

That's exactly the feeling I think Mapping With Melanin™ deserves.
