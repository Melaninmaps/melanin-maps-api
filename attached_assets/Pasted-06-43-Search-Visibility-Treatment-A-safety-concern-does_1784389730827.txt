06.43 Search Visibility Treatment
A safety concern does not automatically remove an entity from search.
An entity may remain searchable when:
public identification is legitimate;
the user explicitly searches for it;
the information is historically relevant;
removal would conceal useful accountability information;
the concern does not meet block criteria.
Search results may include approved safety context.
Critical legal, privacy, impersonation, or governance issues may require removal.
06.44 Promotion Treatment
Promotion requires a higher safety standard than search or ordinary recommendation.
Promotion must be held when:
a High-Risk or Critical override exists;
a serious report is under active review;
evidence has been concealed;
retaliation is alleged credibly;
required safety information is materially false;
the entity refuses necessary verification;
a current public alert applies.
Payment may not reduce the concern score or remove a hold.
06.45 Anti-Manipulation Signals
Potential manipulation includes:
coordinated false reports;
coordinated suppression of legitimate reports;
copied incident narratives;
mass account creation;
location spoofing;
employee or owner impersonation;
competitor attacks;
retaliation reporting patterns;
incentives for safety ratings;
edited screenshots;
forged official notices;
mass downvoting of safety concerns;
repeated withdrawal pressure;
false emergency claims;
deliberate category misclassification;
review flooding after publicity;
coordinated defense campaigns;
hidden financial relationships;
fabricated corrective action;
deletion attempts targeting historical evidence.
06.46 Anti-Manipulation Responses
Level	Response
1	Monitor signal
2	Discount questionable evidence
3	Require verification
4	Freeze public score change
5	Hold alert or recommendation
6	Restrict contributor influence
7	Human investigation
8	Governance escalation
9	Account or entity restriction
10	Removal, legal escalation, or emergency referral
The system must protect legitimate reporters from being classified as manipulators merely because their report is unpopular.
06.47 Reporter Protection Rules
The Engine must:
permit appropriate anonymity;
separate public display identity from internal verification;
restrict access to sensitive reports;
detect retaliation;
avoid notifying an accused party before evidence is preserved where doing so could increase harm;
protect minors;
minimize location and contact exposure;
preserve appeal rights;
distinguish malicious reporting from disputed reporting;
avoid requiring public confrontation.
06.48 Accused-Entity Fairness Rules
The Engine must also:
distinguish allegation from verified finding;
provide an appropriate response pathway;
prevent permanent harm from a single weak report;
preserve corrective evidence;
allow factual correction;
identify conflicts of interest;
avoid public categorical labels unsupported by evidence;
maintain timelines;
document holds;
provide appeal and Governance review.
Protecting reporters and preserving fairness are simultaneous obligations.
06.49 Public Explanation Rules
Approved public explanations include:
“Recent safety information affected this recommendation.”
“Evidence is limited, so this result is shown with caution.”
“Multiple independent reports describe similar concerns.”
“An official alert is active.”
“This concern appears to be historical and may not reflect current conditions.”
“Verified corrective action has been reported.”
“Accessibility information could not be confirmed.”
“This result remains searchable but is not currently eligible for promotion.”
Prohibited explanations include:
confidential scores;
reporter identity;
internal thresholds;
anti-gaming signals;
unsupported guilt;
unsupported safety guarantees;
hidden identity assumptions;
precise private-location information.
