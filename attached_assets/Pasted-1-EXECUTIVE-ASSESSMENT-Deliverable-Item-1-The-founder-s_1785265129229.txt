1. EXECUTIVE ASSESSMENT (Deliverable Item 1)
The founder’s vision is coherent, unusually well documented, and — critically — already encoded in governance documents inside the repository (the Community Intelligence Constitution’s eleven principles, the Foundation Build Strategy’s trust-phase model, the Cross-Role Profile Privacy Standard, and the Community Signal Strength Standard). Replit’s phased plan is directionally faithful to that vision. The trust-phase sequencing logic (“if Memory ships before Transparency, people won’t trust it”) is genuinely good product thinking, and the “stop-safe” rule — every build must leave a product you would be proud to ship if development halted for three months — is the right governing constraint.

However, this review finds four structural problems that will distort the vision if not corrected before the plan executes:

First, the current code contradicts the founder’s most explicit cultural-governance rule. The deployed KinfolkAI system prompt couples profanity permission to AAVE Level 3 — the prompt text literally reads “User has opted into Level 3 cultural voice. Casual profanity is permitted” (`kinfolk.ts` line 1018). The founder’s controlling instruction states that profanity must be a separate, explicit opt-in and that “AAVE must never be treated as synonymous with profanity, slang, broken English, informality, or youth language.” This is not a future-state gap; it is a live defect in the founding voice system that Build 100/101’s three-setting decoupling must fix — and the schema currently has no separate profanity field to decouple into (`user_preferences` has only `aaveLevel smallint`).

Second, the cultural registry — the platform’s founding asset — is architecturally fragile. The 36-city registry is hardcoded TypeScript, duplicated across three route files (`kinfolk.ts` lines 143–592, `maps.ts` line 99 where it is constructed inline per-request, and `travel.ts` line 14), uncited, unversioned, with no review dates, no founding-vs-living separation, and no rollback capability. Any Living Community Layer built on top of this foundation inherits its fragility, and any diaspora expansion (Black Miami, Haitian Miami, Cuban Miami) would today mean editing three parallel hardcoded maps.

Third, the data model lacks the future state’s core entities. Across 186 schema tables there is no city entity, no community entity, no neighborhood entity (cities exist only as `varchar` strings), no consent-record table, no citation model for cultural claims (cultural sites carry a single `verified_source varchar(255)`), no language-term table, no Life Chapter concept (only a flat 8-value `journeyType` enum with phases in one jsonb blob), and no ambassador data model beyond `trustLevel = 4`. The plan’s later builds assume entities the schema cannot yet express.

Fourth, the plan’s largest build is dangerously overloaded. Build 103 as described (Life Chapters + My Story + Cultural Journeys + city-as-container + Business Intelligence) combines the two highest-privacy-risk systems on the roadmap (life-event memory and business intelligence briefings) with the largest data-model refactor (city/community/neighborhood containers) in a single release. This must be split.

The roadmap is salvageable and largely correct in intent. My verdict is CONDITIONAL GO (confidence 62/100) — proceed, with the resequencing, splits, and prerequisite architecture work specified in this review. Details in Sections 4, 6, and the final deliverable items.

An evidence caveat applies throughout: the founder’s message describes an 8-section Replit plan with 189 capabilities across categories A–I. That unified document is not in the repository. The closest artifacts are `docs/vision/FOUNDATION_BUILD_STRATEGY.md` (Builds 97–104+) and `docs/product/BUILD_97_SCOPE_AND_ROADMAP.md` (Phases 1–9, 29 outputs), plus the Future-State Register containing 55 FSR entries, not 189. Where the founder’s build numbering (97, 98, 99/100, 100/101, 102, 103, 104, 105, 106+) extends beyond the repo documents, I reviewed against the phase content the numbering maps to, and I flag every claim that could not be verified against a committed document as REQUIRES EVIDENCE. Replit should be required to commit the full 8-section plan and the 189-capability register to `docs/product/` before implementation begins — an unversioned plan cannot govern nine builds.


2. RESTATEMENT OF THE FOUNDER’S VISION (Deliverable Item 2)
To guard against drift, this is the vision this review holds the plan accountable to, restated from the controlling description and verified against the repository’s vision documents.

Mapping With Melanin™ is becoming a trusted Community Intelligence Platform — not a directory, not a travel app, not a social network, not a generic chatbot. Its guiding sentence, encoded verbatim in the Community Intelligence Constitution, is:

“KinfolkAI exists to strengthen the relationship between people, businesses, professionals, organizations, and culture through trusted community understanding.”

The platform helps people find community, discover culturally relevant businesses and places, move through cities with cultural understanding and safety, reconnect with heritage, respectfully explore communities beyond their own, receive life guidance without pressure, document journeys when they choose, learn from lived experience, contribute knowledge back, and always understand why a recommendation is shown. It prioritizes community before engagement, transparency, gradual adaptation, evidence-based guidance, privacy with meaningful control, and contribution over popularity. The goal is explicitly not time-in-app.

The connected ecosystems are: (A) discovery/maps/heritage/safety, including sundown towns, HBCUs, discrimination reports, Officer Watch, and scale-aware community signals; (B) Kinfolk cultural intelligence, preserving the founding 36-city Black cultural registry while evolving toward city/community/neighborhood/context/preference distinctions; (C) community voice and language governance, with a founding cultural registry (researched, cited, stable) and a living community layer (governed contributions, moderation, rollback, no automatic adoption from private chats), profanity off by default with separate opt-in; (D) library, cultural reconnection, and learning with no identity proof required and no guilt-based gamification; (E) Life Chapters containing Journeys — opt-in, private by default, editable, pausable, deletable, never inferred solely from sensitive behavior; (F) the Cultural Ambassador ecosystem measuring community impact rather than popularity; and (G) community intelligence and opportunity matching with proportional interpretation and purposeful collection.

The repository’s own governance documents already operationalize this vision into testable principles — notably the Constitution’s eleven principles and its eleven-question ship test. This review treats those documents as the acceptance criteria for the plan.

10. JOURNEY & MEMORY, AMBASSADOR, PRIVACY, FAMILY, AND CULTURAL-GOVERNANCE FINDINGS (Deliverable Items 10–14)
Journeys and memory (Item 10). The founder’s model — Chapter contains Journeys; transactional needs support Chapters rather than becoming Journeys — requires: `life_chapters` (user-declared, open type list with user-defined labels, not a closed enum), `journeys(chapter_id, …)`, `milestones` as rows (not jsonb, so they can be individually edited/deleted), and `journey_settings` (visibility, notifications on/off, paused, exportable). The risks the founder lists are all live in the current design: one giant journey table with jsonb phases (present), AI creating journeys without consent (the chat flow can create journeys — must require explicit confirmation UI), inference from sensitive behavior (searchHistory retention makes this temptingly easy — prohibit by policy and by excluding search history from journey-suggestion inputs), irreversible history (no deletion routes for journeys/milestones today), and notification pressure (no governance). Opt-in graduation summaries and pay-it-forward invitations (the HBCU example) are Build 105 features requiring consented attribution collected years earlier — start collection at Build 99/100.

Ambassadors and creators (Item 11). The plan’s intent is SUPPORTED, the architecture is absent (§5 items 11–12). The founder’s five conflation warnings should be encoded structurally: trust ≠ popularity (impact metrics primary, reach metrics secondary and clearly labeled external); influence ≠ expertise (expertise areas are declared and community-reviewed, not follower-derived); expertise ≠ credentials (display “lived experience” vs. “credentialed” distinctly); identity ≠ authority (community-reviewer roles require review history, not identity claims); views ≠ impact (impact events only from consented attribution). Multiple creator identities and life-branch evolution (travel creator → parenting branch) map naturally onto Chapter-linked creator profiles — one creator entity, multiple branches, never forced re-categorization. Content ownership, licensing, and removal rights must be database fields on contributions (license type, granted rights, removal-requested state) — never only UI copy, per the request’s warning.

Privacy and consent (Item 12). Applying the founder’s Section 8 question set across sensitive categories produces one uniform architecture rather than per-category improvisation: collection only when a feature requires it (Constitution Principle 9’s four questions applied per new column); explicit over inferred always — the platform must never infer race, religion, orientation, pregnancy, health, or family status (its personalization asks; `diasporaCountries` and `culturalInterests` are member-stated, which is the correct pattern); consent recorded per category and version with revocation; storage limited to member-stated values and consented events, never raw sensitive free-text tags (the request’s warning — audit `dietaryNotes`-style free-text fields at each build); personalization uses only stated+consented data; nothing sensitive is ever shared cross-role (Cross-Role Privacy Standard’s technical separation becomes enforceable once visibility tiers are schema-backed); inspect/correct/pause/delete/export arrives with the Build 100/101 transparency panel and Build 103b export; family mode suppresses sensitive personalization entirely; for minors see below; and legal review gates: biometric (102), minors/COPPA-adjacent (before youth journeys), health-adjacent journey types (before 103b wellness journeys), and creator licensing (before 105).

Minors and family (Item 13). The current family system is a set of booleans plus a keyword filter that stores violation snippets (its own retention question — set a short TTL). There is no age assurance, no parental-consent flow, and family mode is not enforced in Kinfolk. Before any youth-oriented journey (HBCU exploration is explicitly a teenager scenario in the vision): wire family mode into every AI surface as deterministic code; define the minor experience (13–17) explicitly — what personalization is disabled, what memory is retained (minimum), what notifications are permitted (near none); require parental consent flows consistent with COPPA posture even for 13+ where sensitive; and never link family-mode status to membership tier (the request’s warning — currently they are independent; keep it that way as a tested invariant).

Cultural governance (Item 14). Before evolving AAVE/local language, adding community city layers, publishing resident videos, using historical superlatives, discussing contested history, or creating Indigenous, religious, or LGBTQIA+ profiles, the platform needs an operating framework — not a document. Minimum viable framework, which must be exercised at least once before Build 103c: reviewer qualifications (documented lived experience and/or subject expertise per community; for Indigenous profiles, reviewers from the relevant nation/community); minimum two reviewers per cultural claim, three for contested-history items; conflict-of-interest disclosure (reviewers do not approve their own or affiliated-business content); disagreement handling (contested flag displayed with both positions rather than silent resolution); review cadence (founding-layer entries re-reviewed annually; living-layer entries carry `review_due_at`); expiration for time-sensitive claims; correction and appeal processes with visible version history; source requirements (every historical claim cites at least one verifiable source — superlatives like “first” or “oldest” require two); contributor attribution with consent; and a content-removal process honoring community objections. The framework must guarantee future diaspora and minority community profiles the same depth as the founding Black-city registry — structurally, this means the same `cultural_profiles` schema, citation standards, and reviewer thresholds apply to every community, with no second-class data shape.
