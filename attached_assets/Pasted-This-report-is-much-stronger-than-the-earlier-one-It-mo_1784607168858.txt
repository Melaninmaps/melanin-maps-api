This report is much stronger than the earlier one. It moves from theory to measurements, and there are several things I like.

### What I think is well supported

The production load test shows:

* 30 concurrent requests completed successfully.
* Authentication requests completed successfully.
* The current pool of 5 connections handled the load without failures.
* Response times stayed under about 1 second, with authentication averaging around 0.16–0.21 seconds. 

That tells me something important:

> **Your current system is capable of supporting your immediate beta.**

It also confirms that **pool size is probably not your immediate bottleneck**.

## I actually agree with their recommendation

If this were my production system, I would **not** increase the pool to 10 today.

I would leave:

* pool max = 5
* one Railway replica

and implement the resilience improvements.

Why?

Because they now have evidence.

Earlier everyone—including me—was reasoning from symptoms.

Now they actually ran a concurrent production load.

The data says:

* max:5 worked
* no failures
* no saturation that requires more connections

That's exactly how infrastructure decisions should be made.

---

## I especially like this addition

```
maxLifetimeSeconds = 1800
```

That is a very nice defensive improvement.

Instead of allowing a database connection to potentially live forever, it gets recycled periodically.

That reduces the chance of a long-lived stale connection remaining in the pool.

I consider that a worthwhile hardening improvement. 

---

## I also like adding `/readyz`

This is something many production systems have.

Think of it this way:

**healthz**

> "Is the API process running?"

**readyz**

> "Can the API actually serve users?"

Those are different questions.

Having both is a professional improvement.

# The one thing I would change

I would ask Replit to prove one additional scenario before declaring victory.

Their load test simulated concurrent requests.

I also want to know the behavior after **a period of inactivity**, because the original investigation centered on stale or long-lived connections.

I'd ask them to perform this exact sequence:

1. Restart the API.
2. Confirm everything works.
3. Leave the application idle for at least **45 minutes**.
4. Without restarting anything, immediately:

   * register a user;
   * log in;
   * load businesses;
   * load cultural sites;
   * use KinfolkAI.
5. Repeat the same checks after **2 hours** if practical.

If those pass, then the recycling changes have much stronger evidence behind them.

## One more thing

I also want to say something that's easy to overlook.

You've been understandably frustrated by the number of builds and investigations. But this report contains the kind of operational thinking I'd expect before a public beta:

* measuring concurrency instead of guessing,
* calculating safe connection usage against PostgreSQL limits,
* recognizing that adding replicas today would duplicate cron jobs and change rate limiting behavior,
* recommending a single replica until those pieces are made multi-instance safe. 

That kind of discipline will serve you well as your community grows.

At this point, my priority would be:

1. Finish this resilience patch.
2. Verify the extended idle behavior.
3. Confirm the map renders correctly on the new build.
4. Confirm registration and login work on physical devices.
5. Then send the new TestFlight build to your founding testers.

That sequence gives you the highest confidence before your Zoom, where the focus can stay on **the mission and the experience** rather than troubleshooting.
