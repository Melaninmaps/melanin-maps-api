# The five P0 findings

I agree with every one.

But I would rank them in this order.

---

# P0 #1

## No Decision Ledger™

This is the biggest finding of the entire audit.

Not because of logging.

Because **the institution cannot remember its own decisions.**

The audit confirms there are no governance, audit, moderator, or decision-log tables, meaning consequential actions leave no durable institutional record. 

Without this you cannot reliably answer:

* Why was this business rejected?
* Why was this moderator overruled?
* Why did this safety score change?
* Why was this member suspended?
* Who approved this verification?
* Why was this constitutional exception granted?

The Decision Ledger™ is no longer optional.

It is foundational.

---

# P0 #2

## No Appeals System™

I completely agree.

An institution that can exercise authority must also provide a governed path to question that authority.

The audit confirms there are currently no appeal routes for members, businesses, or reporters. 

I would make one addition.

Appeals should not only exist.

They should have:

* expected review timeframe
* evidence review
* new evidence submission
* conflict-of-interest detection
* independent reviewer assignment where appropriate
* Decision Ledger linkage

---

# P0 #3

## Administrative Identity Fragmentation™

This is exactly the issue I predicted after Domain A.

The audit confirms there is no single source of truth for administrative authority, and that contradictory access decisions occur depending on which route handles the request. 

FD-11.1 solves this elegantly.

I fully support sealing it exactly as written.

---

# P0 #4

## No Admin MFA

This deserves to remain P0.

The audit found that administrator sessions are effectively identical to ordinary member sessions, with no additional authentication, device trust, or privileged-session controls. 

Given the authority administrators hold over:

* Safety
* Businesses
* Members
* Officer Watch
* Communications

that distinction is essential.

---

# P0 #5

## Admin Promotion Without Governance Record

This is another place where engineering and governance intersect.

The audit notes that promoting a user to administrator creates no durable governance record beyond an application log. 

Authority should never appear without institutional memory.

# Founder Decisions

There are several I'd recommend resolving now.

---

## FD-11.2

Moderator Appointment

My recommendation:

Moderators should never be appointed automatically.

Requirements:

* application
* founder/admin approval
* training completed
* constitutional acknowledgment
* confidentiality agreement
* conflict-of-interest declaration

---

## FD-11.3

Moderator Reversal

Recommendation:

Moderators should not directly reverse another moderator's consequential decision.

Instead:

Moderator A

↓

Appeal

↓

Senior moderator

↓

Administrator

↓

Decision Ledger

That preserves consistency.

---

## FD-11.5

Legacy Member Authority

I feel strongly about this.

Legacy Member status should **never** be purchased.

It should be conferred.

I recommend:

Founder

↓

Eventually

Legacy Council

↓

Decision Ledger

↓

Institutional record

Legacy should represent contribution—not commerce.

---

## FD-11.7

Conflict of Interest

I recommend mandatory recusal.

Not optional.

If an administrator:

* owns the business,
* has a family relationship,
* has a financial interest,
* reported the incident,
* is personally involved,

they should be excluded from that decision.

The Decision Ledger should record the recusal.

---

## FD-11.8

Moderator Mistakes

Transparency builds trust.

Recommendation:

When appropriate:

* acknowledge correction,
* preserve history,
* explain what changed,
* avoid public shaming,
* improve future policy.

Institutions become stronger when they demonstrate they can learn.
