I also think the sequencing is exactly right.

This isn't really a "billing audit." It's an audit of the **institution's promise enforcement system**.

The biggest insight from Audits 1–7 is something I don't think we appreciated when we started:

* Audit 1 exposed authentication gates.
* Audit 2 exposed onboarding promises.
* Audit 3 exposed business trust promises.
* Audit 4 exposed Navigator+ routing.
* Audit 5 exposed Community feature permissions.
* Audit 6 exposed Safety permissions.
* Audit 7 exposed **three explicit TODO tier gates** plus unauthenticated AI access.  

They all converge on one subsystem:

> **Membership Enforcement.**

If that subsystem is inconsistent, it doesn't matter how good the individual features are because members will either receive benefits they didn't purchase or be denied benefits they did purchase.

# I would add five new audit sections

These are unique to Membership.

---

## A. Entitlement Consistency Audit™

This becomes the heart of Audit 8.

For every feature ask:

Who can access it?

Who should access it?

Who actually can?

Who actually can't?

Example:

Navigator+

Explorer

Free

Business

Family

Founder

Administrator

Guest

Every mismatch becomes evidence.

---

## B. Institutional Promise Audit™

This is different.

Take every advertised benefit.

Example:

Navigator+

Ask:

Can the platform actually deliver it?

Evidence.

No marketing assumptions.

---

## C. Upgrade Integrity™

If someone upgrades:

Does everything unlock?

Immediately?

After refresh?

Next login?

Next webhook?

Next purchase sync?

Likewise:

Downgrades.

Expired cards.

Refunds.

Chargebacks.

RevenueCat outages.

Stripe outages.

---

## D. Tier Drift™

This is one I don't think most startups ever audit.

Suppose:

Navigator

receives

Trailblazer

features because one route forgot to check.

Or:

Business Premium

receives

Legacy

analytics.

Find every inconsistency.

Audit 7 already found three examples. 

---

## E. Institutional Fairness™

Ask:

If a payment fails...

Does the member lose:

their data?

their AI?

their saved places?

their Circle?

their Recognition?

their history?

their conversations?

their Community Journey™?

Or merely premium capabilities?

That distinction matters enormously.

A lapsed subscription should not erase a member's identity or contributions unless you've explicitly decided that policy.

# Constitutional Commentary

I would include:

> **Membership is not merely a financial transaction.**
>
> It is an agreement between the institution and the member.
>
> Every paid benefit should exist because it creates additional value—not because it withholds belonging.
>
> A member's contribution to the community should never disappear because a payment method expired.
>
> Premium memberships should expand opportunity, intelligence, and convenience while preserving the dignity, identity, history, and relationships of every member.
>
> Commerce exists to sustain the mission.
>
> It must never become the mission.
