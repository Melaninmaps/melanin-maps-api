# 04. COMMUNITY ENGINE™

## Future-State Production Specification v1.0

**Classification:** Core Community Intelligence Engine
**Status:** Review Mode — designed as the launch-state model
**Confidentiality:** Highest restriction; community scoring methodology, evidence weights, reciprocity calculations, intervention thresholds, and recognition logic are confidential trade secrets and are not publicly disclosed

---

## 04.1 Purpose

The Community Engine™ evaluates the strength, quality, accessibility, reciprocity, and lived experience of community surrounding a person, business, neighborhood, city, organization, event, opportunity, or resource.

It determines whether community is merely present, meaningfully connected, actively supportive, structurally accessible, or capable of sustaining belonging over time.

The Community Engine™ does not measure popularity.

It does not treat activity volume, follower count, review quantity, population size, or online engagement as proof of community health.

It evaluates whether people can:

1. find one another;
2. enter without unnecessary barriers;
3. participate with dignity;
4. contribute meaningfully;
5. receive support;
6. build trusted relationships;
7. access opportunities;
8. return and remain connected;
9. influence what affects them;
10. help others do the same.

The Engine’s governing question is:

> **Does this environment help people become meaningfully connected, supported, visible, useful, and able to belong—not only present?**

---

# 04.2 Foundational Principle

Community is not defined solely by proximity.

People may live near one another without connection.

They may gather without trust.

They may participate without influence.

They may be welcomed once without being supported over time.

They may consume from a community without contributing to it.

They may be highly visible while the people most affected remain unheard.

The Community Engine™ therefore distinguishes among:

```text
Presence
Participation
Connection
Reciprocity
Support
Influence
Continuity
Belonging
```

These are related but not interchangeable.

A high number of people present does not establish belonging.

A high number of posts does not establish connection.

A large number of transactions does not establish reciprocity.

A visible cultural identity does not establish authentic community stewardship.

---

# 04.3 Definition of Community

For Mapping With Melanin™, **community** is a network of people, institutions, businesses, places, resources, relationships, histories, and opportunities connected by one or more shared conditions and capable of producing mutual support, recognition, access, continuity, or collective benefit.

Community may be:

* geographic;
* cultural;
* relational;
* professional;
* educational;
* historical;
* interest-based;
* identity-informed;
* purpose-based;
* temporary;
* digital;
* intergenerational;
* diaspora-connected;
* institution-centered;
* crisis-responsive.

The Community Engine™ must not assume that all members of a shared identity experience community in the same way.

# 04.4 Community Evaluation Objects

The Engine may evaluate community at the following levels.

| Object        | Example                                             |
| ------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| Person        | Community member, Cultural Ambassador, organizer    |
| Business      | Restaurant, retailer, service provider              |
| Event         | Festival, workshop, networking gathering            |
| Organization  | Nonprofit, association, cultural institution        |
| Neighborhood  | Local geographic community                          |
| City          | Metropolitan or municipal community environment     |
| Resource      | Food bank, scholarship program, legal clinic        |
| Opportunity   | Job, grant, mentorship, contract                    |
| Institution   | HBCU, museum, foundation, historic organization     |
| Digital space | Kinfolk Circle, community discussion, online group  |
| Network       | Business collective, alumni group, diaspora network |
| Platform area | Local feed, topic community, resource channel       |

The scoring interpretation changes by object type.

A business is not expected to perform the same community role as a city.

An event is not expected to provide the same continuity as an institution.

# 04.5 Core Evaluation Dimensions

The Community Engine™ returns a normalized score from **0 to 100** based on seven dimensions.

| Dimension                    | Baseline weight |
| ---------------------------- | --------------: |
| Welcoming Access             |             15% |
| Meaningful Connection        |             17% |
| Reciprocity                  |             17% |
| Community Support            |             16% |
| Voice & Influence            |             13% |
| Continuity & Return          |             12% |
| Stewardship & Shared Benefit |             10% |
| **Total**                    |        **100%** |

These are baseline weights.

The Context Engine™ and approved Weight Registers may adjust them by context.

---

# 04.6 Dimension One — Welcoming Access

Welcoming Access measures whether people can discover, enter, understand, and participate without avoidable exclusion.

It evaluates:

1. discoverability;
2. clarity of entry;
3. affordability;
4. accessibility;
5. language access;
6. cultural familiarity;
7. newcomer treatment;
8. family suitability;
9. digital access;
10. transportation access;
11. transparent eligibility;
12. absence of unnecessary gatekeeping.

## 04.6.1 Welcoming Access Score

```text
Welcoming Access Score =
  Discovery Access
+ Entry Clarity
+ Practical Accessibility
+ Cultural Accessibility
+ Newcomer Experience
- Exclusion Penalties
```

The result is normalized to 0–100.

## 04.6.2 Welcoming Access subweights

| Component               | Weight |
| ----------------------- | -----: |
| Discovery Access        |    15% |
| Entry Clarity           |    15% |
| Practical Accessibility |    25% |
| Cultural Accessibility  |    20% |
| Newcomer Experience     |    25% |

## 04.6.3 Positive indicators

* clear public information;
* transparent hours and requirements;
* confirmed physical accessibility;
* welcoming first-time participation;
* multiple entry pathways;
* affordable or free options;
* community members willing to assist newcomers;
* no requirement for insider relationships;
* culturally respectful communication;
* visible explanation of norms;
* family or caregiver accommodations;
* responsive contact methods.

## 04.6.4 Exclusion indicators

* hidden eligibility requirements;
* repeated reports of gatekeeping;
* inaccessible location without disclosure;
* discriminatory entry behavior;
* unclear pricing;
* insider-only access;
* hostile treatment of newcomers;
* public welcome language inconsistent with lived experience;
* unnecessary documentation requirements;
* selective enforcement of rules;
* exploitative membership barriers.

---

# 04.7 Dimension Two — Meaningful Connection

Meaningful Connection measures whether people form useful, trusted, or enduring relationships rather than merely occupying the same space.

It evaluates:

1. repeated interaction;
2. relationship depth;
3. mutual recognition;
4. ease of finding aligned people;
5. small-group connection;
6. mentorship;
7. friendship formation;
8. business collaboration;
9. information exchange;
10. connection across generations;
11. connection across neighborhoods or diaspora communities;
12. continued contact after first interaction.

## 04.7.1 Meaningful Connection Score

```text
Meaningful Connection Score =
  Repeat Interaction Quality
+ Relationship Depth
+ Connection Accessibility
+ Cross-Network Bridge Value
+ Continuation Rate
- Superficiality Penalty
```

## 04.7.2 Positive indicators

* users return to engage with the same people;
* introductions lead to later interaction;
* mentorship relationships form;
* community members refer one another;
* businesses collaborate;
* members form Kinfolk Circles;
* relationships continue outside a single event;
* newcomers report meeting useful or supportive contacts;
* users identify people they can call on;
* local and diaspora relationships are sustained.

## 04.7.3 Superficiality indicators

* large attendance with no repeat engagement;
* engagement driven only by giveaways;
* high follower counts but low relationship continuity;
* one-directional broadcasting;
* repeated participation without reciprocal recognition;
* staged networking with no follow-through;
* high interaction volume from coordinated or automated accounts.

---

# 04.8 Dimension Three — Reciprocity

Reciprocity measures whether community benefit moves in more than one direction.

It evaluates whether people, businesses, organizations, and institutions both receive and contribute.

## 04.8.1 Reciprocity Score

```text
Reciprocity Score =
  Contribution Rate
+ Benefit Distribution
+ Mutual Support
+ Resource Circulation
+ Response to Community Need
- Extraction Penalty
```

## 04.8.2 Reciprocity evidence

Positive evidence includes:

* referrals given and received;
* community members helping one another;
* businesses supporting local initiatives;
* paid opportunities returned to the community;
* useful information shared;
* mentorship offered;
* resources contributed;
* volunteer support;
* community concerns acknowledged;
* revenue circulating through local businesses;
* Cultural Ambassadors directing attention to community benefit;
* institutions creating pathways rather than one-time appearances.

## 04.8.3 Extraction indicators

* gathering community data without returning value;
* cultural promotion without compensation or recognition;
* businesses benefiting from community identity without community investment;
* repeated requests for labor without reciprocity;
* influencer activity that extracts visibility but does not create access;
* institutions using community stories without consent;
* paid partnerships hidden from the community;
* resources consistently flowing outward without measurable return;
* predatory pricing during community need;
* using crisis conditions for promotion.

---

# 04.9 Dimension Four — Community Support

Community Support measures whether useful help is available when people need it.

Support may be emotional, informational, practical, economic, educational, safety-related, professional, or institutional.

## 04.9.1 Community Support Score

```text
Community Support Score =
  Resource Availability
+ Responsiveness
+ Practical Usefulness
+ Reliability
+ Reach to Underserved Members
- Support Failure Penalties
```

## 04.9.2 Support categories

* food and basic needs;
* housing;
* employment;
* education;
* business development;
* transportation;
* childcare;
* elder support;
* legal assistance;
* safety information;
* emergency support;
* healthcare navigation;
* mentorship;
* relocation assistance;
* social and emotional connection;
* cultural continuity.

## 04.9.3 Positive indicators

* verified resources available;
* community questions receive useful responses;
* urgent needs are routed appropriately;
* businesses share opportunities;
* users report receiving actionable help;
* resource eligibility is clear;
* assistance reaches people with fewer connections;
* community organizations collaborate;
* information remains current;
* support is not conditioned on public praise.

## 04.9.4 Support failure indicators

* outdated or false resources;
* repeated unanswered requests;
* performative offers;
* referral loops with no actual assistance;
* assistance available only to insiders;
* public promises without operational capacity;
* exploitative support conditions;
* excessive data collection before aid;
* emergency needs treated as promotional opportunities.

# 04.10 Dimension Five — Voice & Influence

Voice & Influence measures whether people can meaningfully shape what affects them.

It evaluates:

1. ability to speak;
2. likelihood of being heard;
3. responsiveness to feedback;
4. participation in decisions;
5. correction pathways;
6. representation;
7. transparency;
8. recognition of lived experience;
9. protection from retaliation;
10. influence over community priorities.

## 04.10.1 Voice & Influence Score

```text
Voice & Influence Score =
  Expression Access
+ Decision Participation
+ Responsiveness
+ Correction Power
+ Representation Quality
- Silencing Penalties
```

## 04.10.2 Positive indicators

* community feedback leads to visible action;
* users can dispute inaccurate information;
* businesses respond respectfully to concerns;
* local members influence recommendations;
* decisions are explained;
* community members participate in governance;
* historically overlooked voices are included;
* anonymity is available when necessary;
* disagreement does not automatically produce exclusion;
* appeals are reviewed.

## 04.10.3 Silencing indicators

* retaliation for criticism;
* deleting legitimate concerns without review;
* requiring public identity for sensitive reports;
* allowing popularity to override evidence;
* treating business-owner preference as more important than community safety;
* excluding affected people from decision-making;
* presenting consultation without actual influence;
* suppressing negative evidence for payment or partnership reasons.

# 04.11 Dimension Six — Continuity & Return

Continuity & Return measures whether people remain connected over time and whether community value survives beyond a single transaction, event, post, visit, or campaign.

## 04.11.1 Continuity & Return Score

```text
Continuity & Return Score =
  Return Participation
+ Relationship Persistence
+ Institutional Continuity
+ Knowledge Retention
+ Multi-Stage Engagement
- Abandonment Penalty
```

## 04.11.2 Positive indicators

* people return voluntarily;
* relationships continue;
* businesses retain community trust;
* events become recurring institutions;
* knowledge is preserved;
* community members advance from participant to contributor;
* resources remain available;
* alumni or former residents stay connected;
* users return to help others;
* community history is recorded;
* partnerships survive leadership transitions.

## 04.11.3 Abandonment indicators

* one-time extraction;
* temporary campaigns with no follow-through;
* community spaces disappearing without notice;
* organizations leaving after funding ends;
* users repeatedly required to restart relationships;
* unresolved commitments;
* disappearing resources;
* platform recognition based only on temporary activity.

---

# 04.12 Dimension Seven — Stewardship & Shared Benefit

Stewardship & Shared Benefit measures whether community assets, trust, stories, opportunities, and resources are handled responsibly.

It evaluates whether benefits are durable, distributed, and protected for future participants.

## 04.12.1 Stewardship Score

```text
Stewardship & Shared Benefit Score =
  Responsible Resource Use
+ Community Benefit Distribution
+ Cultural Respect
+ Long-Term Accountability
+ Future Capacity Building
- Stewardship Violations
```

## 04.12.2 Positive indicators

* community stories are used with consent;
* creators and contributors receive recognition;
* economic benefit reaches community members;
* institutions invest in future capacity;
* local knowledge is preserved;
* leadership succession exists;
* data use is transparent;
* partnerships include community protections;
* short-term success strengthens long-term access;
* youth and future generations are considered.

## 04.12.3 Stewardship violations

* unauthorized use of community stories;
* displacement caused by promotion without safeguards;
* cultural exploitation;
* undisclosed commercial influence;
* misuse of community data;
* removal of community control;
* short-term profit that weakens long-term access;
* failure to preserve important records;
* exclusion of future stewards;
* abandonment after visibility or funding is obtained.

# 04.13 Standard Community Score Formula

The baseline Community Score is:

```text
Community Score =
  (Welcoming Access × 0.15)
+ (Meaningful Connection × 0.17)
+ (Reciprocity × 0.17)
+ (Community Support × 0.16)
+ (Voice & Influence × 0.13)
+ (Continuity & Return × 0.12)
+ (Stewardship & Shared Benefit × 0.10)
```

Example:

```text
Welcoming Access:              82 × 0.15 = 12.30
Meaningful Connection:         76 × 0.17 = 12.92
Reciprocity:                   71 × 0.17 = 12.07
Community Support:             79 × 0.16 = 12.64
Voice & Influence:             68 × 0.13 =  8.84
Continuity & Return:           74 × 0.12 =  8.88
Stewardship & Shared Benefit:  81 × 0.10 =  8.10

Raw Community Score = 75.75
Rounded Community Score = 76
```

The raw score must then be confidence-adjusted under the BIF.

# 04.14 Community Confidence

The Community Engine™ returns both:

* a Community Score;
* a Community Confidence level.

## 04.14.1 Community Confidence Formula

```text
Community Confidence =
  Evidence Quality
× Evidence Volume
× Evidence Diversity
× Community Representation
× Recency
× Independence
```

The result is normalized to the BIF confidence scale.

## 04.14.2 Evidence Volume Factor

| Qualified evidence items | Factor |
| -----------------------: | -----: |
|                      50+ |   1.00 |
|                    30–49 |   0.92 |
|                    15–29 |   0.82 |
|                     8–14 |   0.70 |
|                      4–7 |   0.55 |
|                      2–3 |   0.40 |
|                        1 |   0.25 |
|                        0 |   0.00 |

Evidence items must be qualified and non-duplicative.

## 04.14.3 Evidence Diversity Factor

| Distinct evidence categories | Factor |
| ---------------------------: | -----: |
|                    7 or more |   1.00 |
|                            6 |   0.92 |
|                            5 |   0.84 |
|                            4 |   0.72 |
|                            3 |   0.58 |
|                            2 |   0.42 |
|                            1 |   0.25 |
|                            0 |   0.00 |

## 04.14.4 Community Representation Factor

This factor measures whether evidence reflects more than one narrow subgroup or highly coordinated source.

| Representation                          | Factor |
| --------------------------------------- | -----: |
| Broad, relevant, balanced participation |   1.00 |
| Strong representation with minor gaps   |   0.90 |
| Moderate representation                 |   0.75 |
| Narrow representation                   |   0.55 |
| Highly concentrated representation      |   0.35 |
| Unknown                                 |   0.20 |
| No relevant community evidence          |   0.00 |

Representation must not be calculated through protected-identity profiling.

It may use relevant participation roles, geography, relationship to the entity, visit type, time, and independent evidence sources.

# 04.16 Evidence Eligibility

Community evidence is eligible only when:

1. it relates to the evaluated object;
2. it falls within the relevant timeframe;
3. its source relationship is known or appropriately classified;
4. conflicts of interest are disclosed where applicable;
5. duplicate activity is removed;
6. coordinated manipulation is screened;
7. private evidence is used only as permitted;
8. the evidence is specific enough to interpret;
9. the evidence is not based solely on popularity;
10. the evidence does not rely on prohibited identity inference.

---

# 04.17 Evidence Relationship Types

Every evidence item must identify the contributor’s relationship to the evaluated object.

| Relationship               | Example                                    |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Direct participant         | Attended event, used resource              |
| Customer                   | Purchased from business                    |
| Resident                   | Lives in neighborhood                      |
| Visitor                    | Visited location                           |
| Employee                   | Works or worked for entity                 |
| Business owner             | Owns evaluated or related business         |
| Organizer                  | Organized event or initiative              |
| Partner                    | Formal collaborator                        |
| Volunteer                  | Contributed time                           |
| Recipient                  | Received support or opportunity            |
| Observer                   | Witnessed but did not directly participate |
| Institution representative | Represents organization                    |
| Cultural Ambassador        | Promoted or interpreted experience         |
| Platform moderator         | Reviewed evidence                          |
| Unknown                    | Relationship cannot be established         |

Unknown relationships reduce confidence.

Interested-party relationships do not automatically disqualify evidence but require conflict-of-interest adjustment.

# 04.25 Entity-Type Interpretation Rules

## 04.25.1 Businesses

Businesses are evaluated on:

* accessibility;
* customer welcome;
* local reciprocity;
* community support;
* responsiveness;
* repeat relationships;
* employment and opportunity;
* cultural respect;
* community contribution.

Businesses are not required to act as charities.

Their score reflects whether their community relationship is responsible and mutually beneficial.

## 04.25.2 Neighborhoods

Neighborhoods are evaluated on:

* access;
* social connection;
* resource availability;
* local institutions;
* community voice;
* continuity;
* resident participation;
* newcomer integration;
* economic circulation;
* safety and trust interactions.

## 04.25.3 Events

Events are evaluated on:

* entry access;
* participant connection;
* inclusion;
* community benefit;
* follow-up pathways;
* creator recognition;
* recurring value;
* safety and accessibility.

A one-time event cannot receive full continuity credit without verified follow-through.

## 04.25.4 Cultural Ambassadors

Cultural Ambassadors are evaluated on:

* authentic connection;
* community reciprocity;
* opportunity creation;
* responsible influence;
* disclosure;
* continuity;
* stewardship;
* treatment of community stories.

Follower count alone may contribute no more than 10% to related influence calculations and does not directly establish Community Score.

## 04.25.5 Resources

Resources are evaluated on:

* discoverability;
* eligibility clarity;
* accessibility;
* reliability;
* successful use;
* responsiveness;
* reach;
* continuity;
* non-exploitative operation.

## 04.25.6 Cities

Cities are evaluated through aggregated community evidence and coordinated with:

* Community Health Index™;
* City Maturity Model™;
* Safety Intelligence Engine™;
* Opportunity Engine™;
* Discovery Engine™;
* Trust Engine™.

The Community Engine™ must not treat a city as culturally unified.

# 04.26 Cold-Start Rules

## 04.26.1 New entity

For a new entity with limited evidence:

1. calculate provisional dimension scores only where evidence exists;
2. mark unsupported dimensions as Unknown;
3. do not substitute platform averages for entity evidence;
4. permit search eligibility where otherwise allowed;
5. restrict strong recommendation or promotion;
6. show emerging status where appropriate;
7. invite community evidence without pressuring positive reviews;
8. apply anti-gaming monitoring.

## 04.26.2 New community or underrepresented area

The Engine must not punish a community for having less platform data.

It may use:

* verified institutional evidence;
* public resource data;
* local organization information;
* official event history;
* business and community submissions;
* historical evidence;
* cautious regional context.

Low data produces lower confidence, not automatic low community quality.

# 04.27 Community Evidence Decay

Different evidence types decay at different rates.

## 04.27.1 Rapid-decay evidence

Examples:

* current response time;
* active resource availability;
* event participation;
* temporary access issue;
* active conflict.

Suggested decay:

```text
0–30 days:     100%
31–90 days:     75%
91–180 days:    50%
181–365 days:   25%
Over 365 days:  10%
```

## 04.27.2 Moderate-decay evidence

Examples:

* business community participation;
* repeated support;
* local collaboration;
* mentorship activity;
* accessibility practices.

```text
0–90 days:      100%
91–180 days:     90%
181–365 days:    75%
1–2 years:       55%
2–3 years:       35%
Over 3 years:    20%
```

## 04.27.3 Slow-decay evidence

Examples:

* institutional continuity;
* historical stewardship;
* long-term community investment;
* sustained local presence.

```text
0–1 year:       100%
1–3 years:       90%
3–5 years:       80%
5–10 years:      70%
Over 10 years:   60%
```

## 04.27.4 Persistent evidence

Certain verified findings remain historically relevant:

* major community milestones;
* verified severe extraction;
* formal accountability outcomes;
* institutional founding;
* long-term stewardship commitments;
* documented historical significance.

Persistent evidence may lose current operational influence but remains in the Legacy & Learning Engine™.

---

# 04.28 Conflict Resolution

Community evidence may conflict.

Examples:

* newcomers report welcome while long-term residents report exclusion;
* an event creates strong connection but provides no continuity;
* a business supports community organizations but ignores customer concerns;
* high participation coexists with low voice;
* an institution preserves history but restricts practical access.

The Engine applies these rules.

## Rule 1 — Dimension-specific conflict

Conflicting evidence must first be assigned to the correct dimension rather than averaged indiscriminately.

## Rule 2 — Direct experience outweighs marketing claims

Verified lived experience ordinarily outweighs self-description.

## Rule 3 — Repeated independent evidence outweighs isolated reports

Volume alone is insufficient; independence and evidence quality must also be considered.

## Rule 4 — Harm is not canceled by popularity

High attendance, sales, or follower count may not erase verified exclusion, retaliation, or extraction.

## Rule 5 — Positive impact in one dimension does not erase failure in another

Strong cultural stewardship does not automatically correct poor accessibility.

Strong support does not automatically establish voice.

## Rule 6 — Community disagreement may be genuine

The Engine must preserve materially different experiences when evidence supports them.

## Rule 7 — Recent operational evidence may control current recommendations

Long-term reputation does not override current verified conditions.

## Rule 8 — Serious unresolved conflict lowers confidence

When conflicts cannot be resolved, the system must communicate uncertainty rather than force consensus.

---

# 04.29 Community Segmentation Restrictions

The Engine may distinguish relevant experience groups only when necessary to understand material differences.

Permitted examples:

* residents versus visitors;
* customers versus employees;
* newcomers versus long-term participants;
* direct recipients versus observers;
* first-time attendees versus repeat attendees;
* business owners versus customers;
* event participants versus organizers;
* daytime versus nighttime visitors.

The Engine may not create hidden community segments based on inferred protected identity, socioeconomic profiling, political belief, or other sensitive attributes.

# 04.30 Anti-Gaming Rules

Potential manipulation signals include:

1. coordinated positive submissions;
2. reciprocal rating rings;
3. mass account creation;
4. copied community narratives;
5. event attendance inflation;
6. fabricated referrals;
7. self-reported community impact without corroboration;
8. business employees posing as unrelated community members;
9. Cultural Ambassadors concealing paid relationships;
10. repeated support claims with no completed outcome;
11. artificial return behavior;
12. duplicate resource submissions;
13. suppressing negative community evidence;
14. incentive programs requiring positive ratings;
15. geographic spoofing;
16. rapid score changes unsupported by real events.

Responses may include:

* evidence discount;
* confidence reduction;
* temporary score freeze;
* verification request;
* promotion hold;
* contributor weighting reduction;
* network investigation;
* human review;
* Governance escalation.

# 04.31 Community Intervention Rules

The Community Engine™ may recommend intervention when a community pattern is materially weak or at risk.

Interventions are not punishments.

They identify what may strengthen access, trust, participation, or continuity.

## 04.31.1 Intervention levels

| Level | Name            | Meaning                                                         |
| ----: | --------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
|     0 | No intervention | Normal operation                                                |
|     1 | Informational   | Offer best practices or missing information                     |
|     2 | Developmental   | Recommend specific community-building action                    |
|     3 | Priority        | Material weakness affecting recommendation or recognition       |
|     4 | Governance      | Serious exclusion, extraction, retaliation, or systemic concern |
|     5 | Safety-linked   | Safety or urgent harm issue routed to appropriate engine        |

## 04.31.2 Example interventions

* improve accessibility information;
* create newcomer guidance;
* establish a feedback process;
* add community referral pathways;
* disclose partnership compensation;
* create follow-up after events;
* confirm resource availability;
* improve dispute response;
* add local hiring or mentorship pathways;
* preserve community history;
* create community-benefit reporting;
* submit issue for Governance review.

The public platform must not expose confidential intervention status unless approved.

# 04.32 Community Growth Recognition

The Community Engine™ may identify meaningful growth.

Growth must reflect improvement in evidence, not merely increased platform activity.

Examples:

* improved newcomer access;
* increased completed referrals;
* stronger return participation;
* better issue resolution;
* broader community representation;
* sustained resource availability;
* increased mentorship;
* verified economic circulation;
* improved community voice;
* repaired trust after accountability.

Growth may qualify an entity for:

* Community Milestones™;
* Hidden Gem review;
* Community Recognition;
* Living Legacy™ progression;
* Community Catalyst evaluation;
* increased recommendation eligibility.

No recognition is automatic solely because a score increased.

# 04.33 Relationship to Community Health Index™

The Community Engine™ evaluates the quality of community relationships and lived participation.

The Community Health Index™ evaluates broader structural community conditions.

The distinction is:

```text
Community Engine™:
How well are people connecting, supporting, influencing, and returning?

Community Health Index™:
How healthy, resilient, resourced, and sustainable is the broader
community environment?
```

The two engines may share evidence but must not duplicate final calculations.

---

# 04.34 Relationship to Community Catalyst Model™

The Community Engine™ identifies community conditions and activity.

The Community Catalyst Model™ evaluates whether an action, person, business, event, or institution produces measurable positive movement.

Example:

```text
Community Engine:
The event created meaningful connection and repeat engagement.

Community Catalyst Model:
The event led to three new business partnerships, two mentorship
relationships, and a recurring community program.
```

---

# 04.35 Relationship to Community Ripple™ and Community Echo™

The Community Engine™ evaluates current community quality.

Community Ripple™ tracks downstream impact chains.

Community Echo™ tracks repeated or recurring influence over time.

Example:

```text
Community Engine:
A resource was useful and accessible.

Community Ripple:
The recipient used the resource, gained employment, and later referred
three others.

Community Echo:
The resource continues to be recommended and produce successful outcomes
across multiple cycles.
```

---

# 04.36 Relationship to Trust Engine™

Trust and community are separate.

A community may be active but not trustworthy.

An entity may be trustworthy but have limited community reach.

The Trust Engine™ evaluates evidence reliability, integrity, consistency, accountability, and verification.

The Community Engine™ evaluates access, connection, reciprocity, support, voice, continuity, and shared benefit.

Neither may replace the other.

---

# 04.37 Relationship to Safety Intelligence Engine™

The Community Engine™ may detect safety-relevant signals but does not independently declare an entity unsafe.

Safety-relevant community signals must be routed to the Safety Intelligence Engine™.

Examples:

* retaliation;
* harassment;
* discriminatory exclusion;
* unsafe event conditions;
* predatory support;
* community fear;
* repeated unresolved incidents.

A safety override may cap or suspend Community-based recommendation eligibility.

---

# 04.38 Relationship to Discovery Engine™

The Discovery Engine™ determines whether an entity should be found and where it ranks for relevance.

The Community Engine™ provides evidence of:

* community value;
* local connection;
* reciprocity;
* support;
* newcomer suitability;
* continuity;
* stewardship.

A high Community Score may improve discovery relevance.

It does not automatically authorize promotion.

---

# 04.39 Relationship to Cultural Influence Engine™

The Cultural Influence Engine™ measures the quality and impact of cultural influence.

The Community Engine™ measures whether that influence contributes to connection, reciprocity, support, voice, continuity, or shared benefit.

An influential person may have:

* high Cultural Influence;
* low Reciprocity;
* low Stewardship;
* moderate Community Score.

That distinction must remain visible internally.

# 04.46 Institution Test Application

Before approving a Community Engine™ rule, Mapping With Melanin™ must ask:

> **Would we defend this rule as a responsible way to evaluate community 25 years from now?**

A rule fails the Institution Test when it:

* mistakes activity for belonging;
* rewards extraction;
* privileges insiders;
* cannot distinguish marketing from lived experience;
* suppresses disagreement;
* centralizes power without appeal;
* treats communities as data products;
* disadvantages emerging communities;
* gives commercial partners hidden influence;
* cannot be explained with dignity;
* would become harmful at scale.

---

# 04.47 Generational Test Application

The Engine must ask:

> **Would future generations inherit a system that helps them strengthen community, or one that quietly defines community for them?**

A rule fails the Generational Test when it:

* freezes current cultural assumptions;
* prevents future correction;
* concentrates community authority permanently;
* extracts stories without stewardship;
* rewards short-term visibility over continuity;
* cannot survive leadership change;
* depends on founder intuition rather than documented governance;
* leaves no pathway for future communities to redefine their own needs.

# 04.53 Final Governing Rule

The Community Engine™ exists to ensure that Mapping With Melanin™ does not mistake being seen for being supported, being present for belonging, or being popular for being valuable to community.

It must recognize communities that are small but strong.

It must identify environments that are visible but extractive.

It must preserve the difference between one-time participation and durable relationship.

It must value the people who connect, support, teach, refer, protect, preserve, and return—even when their contribution is not publicly celebrated.

Its final governing rule is:

> **Community is strongest when people can enter with dignity, connect with meaning, contribute without exploitation, receive support, influence what affects them, and leave something stronger for those who come next.**
