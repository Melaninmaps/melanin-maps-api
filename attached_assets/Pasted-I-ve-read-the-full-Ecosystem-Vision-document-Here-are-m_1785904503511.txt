3. SAFETY INTEGRATION

Community member reports discrimination at a gas station

Step 1 — The report is filed. The system captures: location (lat/lng), business ID, discrimination category, timestamp, user ID (anonymized in public display), and report text. This goes into the safety incident queue.

Step 2 — Who gets notified. First, an admin flag is created for moderation review. The business's safety rating drops immediately into a "pending review" state — it doesn't disappear from results, but its community safety score is downweighted. If the report is verified (either by admin review or by a second corroborating report), a community alert is published: "Discrimination reported at [location] — community-verified."

Step 3 — The recommendation engine changes. The business is deprioritized in search rankings for users who have indicated sensitivity to safety (i.e., users who have previously engaged with safety features, saved safe spaces, or whose Kinfolk AI profile shows they prioritize community safety). Critically, the system doesn't universally hide the business — it surfaces the warning badge ("⚠ Discrimination Reported") and the alternative recommendation ("Amara's Corner Store, 0.5 mi — Minority-owned, 4.9★") for those who encounter it.

Step 4 — The next person on that route. If another user's navigation passes within 0.3 miles of the gas station, Kinfolk AI intercepts: "Community members have reported issues at [location]. A community-verified alternative is 0.5 miles ahead." They don't have to know to search for safety — the system brings safety to them.

Step 5 — The nearby minority-owned store benefits. Amara's Corner Store gets a demand signal: "You've been recommended as an alternative to a flagged business 12 times this week — your profile views are up 40%." Kinfolk AI may also prompt them: "New customers are finding you through safety recommendations — would you like to add a flash deal to convert them?" The discrimination report at a competitor becomes a direct business development opportunity for a community-aligned business. The flywheel turns: one safety report improves safety outcomes AND drives economic activity to the right place.

5. DATA ARCHITECTURE

When a user saves a business:

Business ID, user ID, timestamp, location context (were they near the business or browsing remotely?), session context (what did they search before saving?), device type
What consumes this: Business receives a "Saves This Week" metric in their dashboard. Kinfolk AI adds the business category to the user's taste profile and life-stage model. If enough users from a specific demographic save this business, it becomes a "Community Favorite" signal. If the user never visits after saving, the system learns the conversion gap and may prompt the business to offer a flash deal to saved-but-not-visited users.
How it improves over time: As saves accumulate across users, the system builds neighborhood-level demand maps. "10 users in NoDa saved this Ethiopian restaurant in the last 3 days" becomes a signal that surfaces it more prominently in search results and triggers a creator opportunity alert.
When a user leaves a review:

Review text, star rating, business ID, user ID, timestamp, visit-verified flag (did they check in or just review remotely?), specific complaint or praise categories (parsed by Kinfolk AI from the text), any photos attached
What consumes this: The review feeds the business's community safety score and star rating. Kinfolk AI parses the text for safety signals (mentions of being followed, ignored, or mistreated → routes to safety incident queue). Positive review text is chunked for training the business dashboard's "Recent Community Feedback" display. The Kinfolk AI review response tool uses the review text as input for generating brand-voice responses for the owner.
How it improves over time: Patterns across reviews (multiple mentions of "long wait" on Saturdays) become predictive for the business's demand forecasting. The safety parsing becomes more accurate as more community-specific language is reviewed and categorized. Review velocity (sudden increase in negative reviews) triggers an admin alert before it becomes a reputation crisis.
When a search returns zero results:

Search query (exact text), location, timestamp, user ID, what category filters were applied, whether the user rephrased and searched again or left
What consumes this: This is arguably the most valuable data in the system. Zero results feed directly into three places: (1) the city content gap queue — "Ethiopian restaurants searched 47 times in Raleigh this month with no listings" becomes a business recruitment target for the MWM team; (2) the creator opportunity matching engine — it becomes an ambassador prompt; (3) Kinfolk AI's community needs model — it learns what the community is looking for that the platform hasn't yet provided.
How it improves over time: Zero-result searches are the platform's roadmap. As the database fills in, yesterday's zero-result query becomes tomorrow's top result — and the user who searched first may receive a push notification: "Remember when you searched for Ethiopian restaurants in Raleigh? We just added 3." That closes the loop, brings the user back, and demonstrates that the platform listens.
