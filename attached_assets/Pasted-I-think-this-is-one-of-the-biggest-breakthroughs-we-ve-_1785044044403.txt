# Pricing

This is the one area where I actually think we should revisit your membership model after launch.

Not because your prices are necessarily wrong.

Because Kinfolk changes the value proposition.

Originally, people were paying for access.

Now they're paying for an intelligent assistant that saves time, surfaces opportunities, and helps them grow.

That means pricing should eventually align with **outcomes and usage**, not simply feature lists.

For example:

* Free members receive foundational community discovery, safety features, and a limited number of Kinfolk interactions.
* Paid members unlock deeper personalization, longer memory, more conversations, richer planning, business intelligence, and proactive recommendations.
* Business tiers can scale by the depth of insights, collaboration tools, automation, and reporting—not by removing the core ability to be discovered.

I would not change pricing **before launch**. You need real usage data first. After launch, you'll be able to see:

* How often people use Kinfolk.
* Which features they value most.
* What your AI costs per user.
* Which business outcomes drive upgrades.

Then you can adjust pricing based on evidence rather than assumptions.

## One final thought

I think you should add one more principle to Kinfolk's constitution:

> **Kinfolk never tells a business what to become. It helps a business become more of who it already is.**

That's a subtle but profound distinction.

A generic AI might say, "Start a brunch menu because brunch is popular."

Kinfolk should instead say:

> "Businesses similar to yours in your area have seen increased interest in brunch. Based on your menu, operating hours, and business goals, brunch could be worth exploring. Would you like to evaluate the costs, staffing, licensing, and menu ideas together?"

That keeps Kinfolk grounded in real information, respects the owner's goals, avoids overpromising, and preserves the individuality that you want every business on Mapping With Melanin™ to have. I think that philosophy should become one of the core design principles for the entire platform.
