Yes. At this point I would authorize implementation, but **not as one giant rewrite**. The safest approach is to build the shared foundation first, wire each existing surface into it, preserve every existing endpoint as a compatibility layer until verified, and only then activate the demand-notification flywheel.

The reason I prefer this over simply telling Replit **“implement the eight layers”** is that their audit proved the pieces already exist in fragments: collaborative filtering is already live, business save/community-trend infrastructure exists, Library `search-or-create` already exists, creator profiles already have locations, and there are already several forms of search-event storage.  

So we should **connect and normalize**, not rebuild.

The one piece I would deliberately keep behind a feature flag even after it is coded is **business/Cultural Ambassador demand notifications**. The audit found no aggregation threshold or anonymization layer yet, which would make that unsafe to expose prematurely.  Once Replit proves that, turn it on.

Everything else—especially Universal Search, “thing” search, Library Books/countries, Kinfolk retrieval, fallback results, and the Sundown Town History improvements—can move forward now.

And the product rule I would put at the very top of the code/spec is:

> **Every action should either help the member now, help the community later, or both.**

A Kenya save enriches travel. A waffle search improves business discovery. A video improves search. A check-in improves Kinfolk. A failed search identifies unmet demand. A business response creates new supply. A Cultural Ambassador fills a content gap. The Library holds it all together.

That is the flywheel Replit now needs to implement—not just another search box.
