11.5 Core Evaluation Dimensions
The Community Echo™ Engine returns a normalized score from 0–100.
Dimension	Weight
Persistence of Impact	20%
Institutional Adoption	16%
Community Memory	14%
Relationship Continuity	13%
Knowledge Preservation	11%
Successor Development	10%
Cultural Integration	9%
Long-Term Benefit	7%
Total	100%
11.6 Dimension One — Persistence of Impact
Measures whether measurable outcomes continue after the originating action has concluded.
Examples include:
recurring mentorship;
ongoing scholarship recipients;
continued business success;
recurring volunteerism;
repeat educational use.
Formula:
Persistence Score =
Continuing Outcomes
× Outcome Quality
× Duration
× Independence from Original Actor
11.7 Dimension Two — Institutional Adoption
Measures whether an idea becomes part of an institution.
Examples:
university curriculum;
annual conference;
city initiative;
nonprofit program;
recurring community partnership.
Institutional adoption dramatically increases Echo potential because institutions often outlive individuals.
11.8 Dimension Three — Community Memory
Community Memory measures whether a contribution becomes part of the community's shared understanding.
Examples include:
stories retold;
traditions maintained;
historical markers;
annual remembrance;
oral histories;
community references;
local identity.
Memory is measured through continued recognition—not nostalgia.
11.9 Dimension Four — Relationship Continuity
Measures whether relationships formed through an originating action continue independently.
Examples:
mentor-mentee relationships;
business partnerships;
alumni networks;
Kinfolk Circles;
community collaborations.
Relationships that continue without direct management receive higher scores.
11.10 Dimension Five — Knowledge Preservation
Measures preservation of useful knowledge.
Examples:
archived educational material;
oral histories;
local guides;
research;
best practices;
historical records.
Knowledge that continues informing future decisions receives greater value.
11.11 Dimension Six — Successor Development
Healthy communities create successors.
Measures:
leadership succession;
mentor succession;
new organizers;
business ownership continuity;
institutional continuity;
volunteer leadership.
Communities dependent upon a single person receive lower scores.
