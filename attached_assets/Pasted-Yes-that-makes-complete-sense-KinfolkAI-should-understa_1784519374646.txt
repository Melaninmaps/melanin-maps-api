Yes, that makes complete sense. KinfolkAI should understand that one human can have **multiple roles and contexts** without becoming multiple people.

The core rule should be:

> **One user identity, multiple context profiles, one connected memory system with clear boundaries.**

A business owner may be:

* a community member personally,
* a business owner professionally,
* an event organizer in another context,
* and possibly an admin or Cultural Ambassador later.

KinfolkAI should recognize the overlap, but it should not blur everything together.

## How it should work

For a personal/community context, KinfolkAI might say:

> “You saved an outdoor event for Saturday. The forecast may affect it.”

For that same person in business-owner context:

> “Your business has a block party scheduled Saturday. Rain is expected in the afternoon. Would you like help drafting a weather update, reviewing an indoor backup plan, or notifying attendees?”

For budgeting:

* **Community member cadence:** household spending, bills, savings goals, transportation, childcare.
* **Business-owner cadence:** revenue, event costs, inventory, payroll, vendor deposits, cash flow, taxes, and business reserves.

The same underlying person may choose to let those contexts inform each other, but KinfolkAI should not automatically treat personal money and business money as interchangeable.

The roadmap already establishes that KinfolkAI is tier-based across platforms and that existing enrichment uses preferences, Life Journey, events, safety, weather, and saved places.  What is missing is an explicit **role-and-context framework**.

Add this to Replit’s launch-safe implementation instructions:

> ## Role-, Profile-, and Tier-Aware KinfolkAI Behavior
>
> KinfolkAI must understand that one canonical user may hold multiple roles or profiles, including:
>
> * Community Member
> * Business Owner
> * Event Organizer
> * Cultural Ambassador
> * Admin or Staff
>
> These are not separate user identities. They are distinct contexts attached to the same canonical user ID.
>
> ### Required behavior
>
> 1. KinfolkAI must adapt its wording, suggestions, priorities, and follow-up cadence based on the user’s active context.
>
> 2. Personal/community context and business/professional context may inform one another only when relevant and permitted.
>
> 3. KinfolkAI must never merge personal and business finances, schedules, private notes, or goals without the user’s permission.
>
> 4. When context is ambiguous, KinfolkAI should ask:
>
>    “Are you asking about this for yourself or for your business?”
>
> 5. A business owner must still receive the normal community-member experience when using their personal profile.
>
> 6. The business context should consider business-specific information such as:
>
>    * business events;
>    * hours;
>    * promotions;
>    * customer communications;
>    * inventory or vendor needs;
>    * business budgeting and cash flow;
>    * business travel;
>    * business safety and weather exposure.
>
> 7. The personal context should consider personal information such as:
>
>    * saved events;
>    * household budget;
>    * personal travel;
>    * family needs;
>    * accessibility and dietary preferences;
>    * personal goals and Life Journey.
>
> 8. Cross-context alerts may be offered when useful, but should disclose the connection and allow the user to dismiss it.
>
> Example:
>
> * Personal context: “You have a community event saved Saturday, and rain is expected.”
> * Business context: “Your business block party is scheduled Saturday, and rain is expected. Would you like help creating a backup plan or attendee message?”
> * Cross-context conflict: “You saved a personal event at 2 PM, but your business calendar also shows a meeting then. Would you like to compare the details?”
>
> ### Tier behavior
>
> Preserve the existing tier-depth model. Role context and membership tier are separate:
>
> * Role determines **which context and responsibilities matter**.
> * Tier determines **how much depth, proactive enrichment, and breadth of assistance is available**.
>
> A business owner on a lower tier should not receive premium-depth outputs merely because they own a business. A higher-tier community member should receive deeper personal assistance without being treated as a business owner.
>
> ### Launch-safe implementation boundary
>
> For tonight, implement only:
>
> * system-prompt awareness of active role/profile context;
> * clarification when personal versus business intent is ambiguous;
> * business-specific versus personal examples and response rules;
> * preservation of existing tier-depth rules.
>
> Do not build tonight:
>
> * a new database schema;
> * new calendar synchronization;
> * autonomous cross-profile notifications;
> * long-term role-specific memory storage;
> * financial-account integrations;
> * new permissions or authentication flows.
>
> Before implementation, confirm:
>
> 1. Where the active role/profile context currently comes from.
> 2. Whether both iOS and Android already pass that context to KinfolkAI.
> 3. Whether a user with both community and business roles can switch contexts today.
> 4. Which exact files would change.
> 5. That no authentication or user-ID logic will be modified.

One subtle but important design rule: **role context should be visible to the user**. KinfolkAI should not silently guess whether it is speaking personally or professionally. A small selector such as:

> Personal | Business

would eventually make this clear. For tonight, the prompt-level clarification is enough, but the selector belongs on the near-term roadmap.
