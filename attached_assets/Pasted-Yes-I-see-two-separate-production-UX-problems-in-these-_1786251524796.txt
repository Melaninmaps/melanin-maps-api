Yes — I see **two separate production UX problems** in these screenshots, and both need to be fixed before Kinfolk feels usable.

The first is the one you called out: **the page jumps/scrolls to the bottom when the user submits a message**, so the footer and input area take over the screen and the user has to scroll back up to find Kinfolk’s response. That is confusing and makes the chat feel broken even when it technically responds.

The second is the message:

> **“Your KinfolkAI pool of 0 requests has been used for August. Upgrade your plan or wait until next month.”**

That is also wrong. A pool of **0** means the user effectively never had an allowance in the first place. Replit needs to diagnose whether your membership/quota configuration is missing, whether this account has the wrong tier, or whether the frontend is interpreting an undefined allowance as zero.

And there is a design point I would make now: **Kinfolk should visually feel like its own workspace, not a long webpage with a chat inserted into it.**

Your screenshot makes that very clear. The site footer is useful on ordinary pages, but once I'm actively talking to Kinfolk, I shouldn't suddenly be staring at **Discover / Community / Platform / About Us** because a message was sent. The conversation should own that viewport.

I would also have Replit hold off on deciding that “requests” are the right quota unit. Given how conversational Kinfolk is supposed to become, charging/counting every follow-up as a standalone “request” may feel punitive. We should separately review whether your membership tiers should measure **messages, conversations/sessions, generous monthly AI usage, or some hybrid** before they lock the quota UX in.
