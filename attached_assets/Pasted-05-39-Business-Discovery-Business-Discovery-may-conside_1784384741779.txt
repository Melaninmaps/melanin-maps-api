05.39 Business Discovery
Business Discovery may consider:
exact service match;
location;
operating status;
verified ownership;
trust;
safety;
community value;
accessibility;
price;
hours;
booking availability;
website or social link;
customer experience;
local rootedness;
Hidden Gem potential.
The Engine must not become a passive directory.
It must also not imply that every searchable business has been independently endorsed.
05.40 Non-Minority-Owned Business Rules
A non-minority-owned business may appear in discovery when:
community members add or review it;
it is relevant to the user’s request;
no exclusive ownership filter is active;
it satisfies search eligibility;
it has no active safety or governance block.
Such a business:
may be searchable;
may receive safety or community information;
may be recommended when contextually appropriate;
must not be falsely labeled;
must not receive melanated diaspora-owned promotional privileges;
must not be invited through minority-owned business recruitment workflows unless policy changes;
may be subject to different promotion rules.
The Engine must preserve truthful ownership representation.
05.41 Verified Ownership Discovery
When verified ownership is explicitly required:
only candidates meeting the approved verification threshold may satisfy the hard filter;
pending verification candidates may appear separately if the user permits;
self-identification alone must not be presented as verified ownership;
community-submitted ownership claims require appropriate labeling;
expired verification may reduce confidence;
disputes trigger hold or review;
paid status has no effect.
05.42 “Someone Is Searching for You” Logic
When users repeatedly search for an identifiable business not in the platform:
the Engine may create a non-public missing-entity candidate;
duplicate and spelling resolution must occur;
search volume must reach an approved threshold;
the business may be notified that community members are searching for it;
individual user identities must not be disclosed;
the business may claim, decline, or ignore;
no listing may be falsely represented as owner-created;
notification frequency must be limited;
safety-sensitive searches must not trigger marketing communication;
business contact use must comply with applicable rules.
05.43 Cold-Start Discovery
05.43.1 New user
For a new user:
rely on explicit query and context;
avoid demographic assumptions;
use baseline diversity;
offer understandable filters;
do not require prior behavior;
allow exploration;
clearly label emerging evidence;
avoid excessive personalization.
05.43.2 New entity
For a new entity:
confirm basic identity;
allow search where appropriate;
apply low-confidence treatment;
avoid immediate top ranking;
permit Hidden Gem review only with sufficient external or community evidence;
do not penalize absence of platform history;
monitor manipulation risk.
05.43.3 New city or low-data market
The Engine may use:
verified official data;
trusted partner data;
local institutions;
community submissions;
historical records;
public resources;
cautious proximity logic.
Low data reduces confidence, not presumed quality.
05.44 Discovery Evidence Decay
05.44.1 Rapid-decay evidence
Examples:
open status;
event availability;
opportunity deadline;
resource capacity;
live route condition.
Current:          100%
1–7 days old:      80%
8–30 days old:     50%
31–90 days old:    20%
Over 90 days:       0–10%
05.44.2 Moderate-decay evidence
Examples:
service availability;
business attributes;
accessibility information;
community activity;
pricing.
0–90 days:        100%
91–180 days:       85%
181–365 days:      65%
1–2 years:         40%
Over 2 years:      20%
05.44.3 Slow-decay evidence
Examples:
historical significance;
long-term ownership;
institutional role;
enduring cultural relevance.
0–1 year:         100%
1–3 years:         95%
3–5 years:         90%
5–10 years:        80%
Over 10 years:     Context-dependent
05.45 Conflict Resolution
Rule 1 — Exact requirement over general quality
An exact eligible match ranks above a broadly strong but nonmatching result.
Rule 2 — Safety over relevance
A highly relevant result may be restricted or excluded by safety rules.
Rule 3 — Current availability over historical popularity
A currently available resource may outrank a more famous unavailable one.
Rule 4 — Verified evidence over marketing claims
Self-description may not outweigh verified contrary evidence.
Rule 5 — User requirement over platform preference
The Engine must not replace the user’s stated objective with what the platform believes is more beneficial.
Rule 6 — Diversity does not override hard constraints
Result variety is applied only among eligible candidates.
Rule 7 — Low evidence reduces confidence, not necessarily relevance
A new candidate may be highly relevant but cautiously presented.
Rule 8 — Paid placement does not alter organic score
Sponsored status is processed separately.
Rule 9 — Community value does not erase poor intent match
A beloved business may still be irrelevant to the user’s request.
Rule 10 — Popularity does not erase underrepresentation concerns
The Engine may intentionally preserve qualified emerging alternatives.
05.46 Duplicate Resolution
Duplicate candidates may arise from:
spelling variants;
multiple submissions;
former and current names;
branch locations;
owner and community records;
partner data;
business claims;
historical entities.
The Engine must distinguish:
true duplicate;
branch location;
rebrand;
successor entity;
historical record;
unrelated similar name.
Duplicate consolidation requires provenance preservation.
No community evidence may be silently lost.
