Yes. I would change **Businesses from a “show businesses” map layer into a “Find a Business” search control.** That fits your platform much better.

The current behavior—tap **Businesses** and suddenly every business in the area appears—treats the map like a database dump. It becomes especially problematic as you grow. Fifty businesses today becomes 500 or 5,000 later.

### What I think the map should do

The map should start **quiet**. Heritage/safety information can have persistent optional layers because geography itself is the information. Businesses are different: the member should tell us what they're trying to find.

So instead of:

**📍 Businesses**

I would have:

**🔎 Find a Business**

Tapping it opens a compact search panel:

> **What are you looking for?**
> Search businesses, services, specialties, vibes, or needs...
>
> `Hair salon`
> `Alopecia stylist`
> `Black-owned brunch`
> `Tax attorney`
> `Date night`
> `Tattoo cover-up`
> `Beauty supply`
> `Open now`
>
> **Location**
> ○ Near Me
> ○ This Map Area
> ○ City / ZIP
>
> **Distance**
> 5 mi · 10 mi · 25 mi · 50 mi
>
> **Refine**
> Category · Specialty · Ownership · Vibe/THE REAL · Price · Accessibility · Community Signals
>
> **Search**

And importantly, that search box should understand **all of the taxonomy we're creating**, not just business names.

So someone doesn't have to know that alopecia care is:

`Beauty → Hair Salons & Stylists → Alopecia Care`

They can type:

**“someone who does alopecia hair”**

and get the correct businesses.

### “Near Me” should be an action, not 100 pins

I think this distinction matters.

**Businesses Near Me** should mean:

> “Show me a manageable number of relevant businesses close to where I am.”

Not:

> “Put every business within some enormous area onto my map.”

I'd probably start with around **10–15 results**.

Then offer:

**Show More** | **Expand to 10 miles**

If there are 72 matching businesses, the member doesn't need 72 pins to know there are choices.

And ranking should become increasingly intelligent:

**distance + exact search match + specialty + community feedback + safety + user's explicit preferences + current Kinfolk context**

—not simply “closest first.”

### Your salon example is exactly right

If you search **Hair Salon**, I don't think you want 30 salon pins.

I'd rather show perhaps the **10 most relevant nearby** and say:

> **10 of 34 nearby salons shown**
>
> Ranked for your search and preferences.
>
> `Show More` · `Expand Area` · `Refine`

Then the map remains readable.

But if you search:

**Alopecia Care**

perhaps only three businesses qualify within 10 miles. Show all three.

Search:

**Loc specialist + children's hair**

maybe there are two.

Search:

**Come As You Are + natural hair + under $$**

maybe there are four.

**That is where all this taxonomy work starts paying us back.**

### There should also be two ways to search

I would support both.

**1. Natural-language search**

> “I need a Black-owned salon near me that specializes in alopecia.”

Eventually Kinfolk can interpret that.

**2. Structured filters**

For people who don't want AI involved:

**Category:** Beauty & Personal Care
**Subcategory:** Hair Salon
**Specialty:** Alopecia Care
**Ownership:** Black-Owned
**Distance:** 10 miles
**Vibe:** Come As You Are

Same underlying data. Two different ways into it.

### And I would keep the map layers separate

This cleans up the whole interface.

**FIND**

* 🔎 Find a Business
* 🔎 Find a Place
* 🔎 Find an Event

**CULTURE**

* HBCUs
* Heritage Sites
* Museums
* Cultural Landmarks

**SAFETY**

* Sundown Town History
* Community Safety
* other approved safety layers

The first group is **search**.

The other groups are **map layers**.

That's the distinction that feels missing right now.

A person may genuinely want to turn on **HBCUs** and look across a whole region because seeing their geographic distribution has meaning.

Nobody needs to turn on **“all businesses”** and see hundreds of unrelated plumbers, restaurants, lawyers, salons and photographers simultaneously.

### One more feature: “Search this area”

Once someone searches for salons and then moves the map, don't immediately load another giant group.

Show:

**🔎 Search this area**

They decide when to refresh.

And if they zoom way out, don't suddenly show 800 businesses. Either cluster the existing results or tell them:

> **Zoom in or choose a city to see nearby businesses.**

### This also creates the right relationship with Kinfolk

The regular search could say:

> **Hair Salon — 14 matches within 5 miles**

Kinfolk could say:

> **I found 14 nearby. These 4 seem closest to what you're looking for.**

Then explain why:

**2.1 mi · Alopecia Care · Black-owned**
*Kinfolk members frequently say: “They Know Our Hair” and “Healthy Hair First.”*

That's where your platform stops behaving like a directory and starts behaving like **community intelligence**.

I would therefore tell Replit **not to redesign this yet**. First have them audit what powers the current Businesses button, how the business pins are queried, whether search already supports category/subcategory/vibes/etc., and whether result limiting/radius filtering already exists. Then we can give them the exact approved **Find a Business** specification without accidentally destroying functionality that's already underneath the current UI.
