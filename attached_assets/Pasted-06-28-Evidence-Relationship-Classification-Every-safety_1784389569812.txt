06.28 Evidence Relationship Classification
Every safety report must identify, where possible, the source relationship.
Relationship	Example
Direct subject	Person who experienced the event
Direct witness	Person who observed the event
Accompanying party	Person present with affected individual
Employee	Current or former worker
Customer or visitor	Person interacting with entity
Resident	Person living in evaluated area
Organizer	Event or activity organizer
Business owner	Owner or authorized representative
Official source	Government or emergency authority
Partner organization	Approved safety or community partner
Secondary reporter	Person relaying another’s account
Anonymous	Identity not known to platform
Unknown	Relationship cannot be established
A report is not automatically unreliable because it is anonymous.
Its confidence treatment must reflect the evidence that can be verified.
06.29 Report Intake Requirements
Every safety report should collect only the information necessary to evaluate and route it.
Launch-state fields may include:
evaluated entity;
location;
date;
approximate time;
report category;
reporter relationship;
firsthand or witnessed status;
severity;
whether immediate danger exists;
whether emergency services are needed;
whether discrimination was involved;
whether children or dependents were affected;
optional description;
corroborating material;
permission for follow-up;
anonymity preference;
public-sharing permission;
moderation status.
The platform must not require a person in immediate danger to complete a long form before receiving emergency guidance.
06.30 Evidence Quality Application
Every safety evidence item receives the BIF Evidence Quality Score.
Additional safety-specific factors may include:
event specificity;
location precision;
time precision;
directness;
corroboration;
supporting material;
reporter relationship;
conflict of interest;
history of reliable reporting;
evidence consistency;
material-fact verification.
The Safety Intelligence Engine™ must preserve both:
the original evidence; and
the transformed scoring value.
06.31 Evidence Decay
Safety information must decay according to its type.
Decay reduces present influence.
It does not erase history.
06.31.1 Immediate operational conditions
Examples:
live threat;
weather warning;
road closure;
active event disruption;
current crowd condition;
unavailable emergency exit.
Active period:        100%
1–6 hours after:       75%
6–24 hours after:      45%
1–3 days after:        20%
After confirmed end:    0–10%
06.31.2 Recent incident evidence
Examples:
harassment;
theft;
discrimination;
unsafe business condition;
event incident.
0–30 days:       100%
31–90 days:       85%
91–180 days:      65%
181–365 days:     45%
1–2 years:        25%
Over 2 years:     10%
