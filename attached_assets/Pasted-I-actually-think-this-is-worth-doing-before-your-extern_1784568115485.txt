I actually think this is worth doing **before** your external testers see it.

Not because the app is broken—but because **the first 10 seconds define Kinfolk's personality.**

Looking at your screenshots, here's what I notice.

### What feels right

I actually love this.

> **"Kinfolk's here. Let's map it out."**

It feels warm.
It feels human.
It feels like someone sitting beside you instead of above you.

I wouldn't change that.

---

### What doesn't fit your vision

The chips at the bottom immediately change the tone.

They make Kinfolk feel like ChatGPT.

Your platform isn't trying to say:

> "I solve homework."

It's trying to say:

> "I help people navigate life."

Those are completely different experiences.

## I would actually remove the chips entirely.

Imagine opening Kinfolk.

You see:

---

**KinfolkAI™**

*Ask me anything • Create lists & reminders*

---

**Kinfolk's here. Let's map it out.**

---

Then nothing.

Just the text field.

Placeholder:

> **What's on your mind today?**

or

> **Where should we start?**

That's it.

No assumptions.

No categories.

No labels.

---

## Then, underneath the input...

Instead of suggestion chips...

I'd have one small sentence.

Something like:

> **Ask me about travel, business, everyday life, planning, writing—or something completely different. If I don't know, I'll tell you honestly. I'm learning every day.**

Or even shorter:

> **Ask me anything. If I don't know, I'll be honest—and we'll figure it out together.**

I absolutely love that last sentence.

Because it builds trust.

You're teaching users:

> Kinfolk doesn't pretend.

That is incredibly important for AI.

# What I would ask Replit to do

I would actually keep this build extremely simple.

---

**KinfolkAI Welcome Experience Refinement (UI Only)**

**Goal**

The first screen should feel welcoming, respectful, and conversational. Kinfolk should invite curiosity without implying users need help or lack knowledge.

### 1. Keep the welcome message

Keep:

> **Kinfolk's here. Let's map it out.**

No change.

---

### 2. Remove the starter prompt chips

Remove all default suggestion chips, including examples such as:

* Help me understand a math problem
* Help me complete a job application
* Draft a professional email
* etc.

Do not replace them with new chips.

---

### 3. Update the input placeholder

Replace:

> Ask me anything...

with one of:

> **What's on your mind today?**

(or the final approved wording)

---

### 4. Add a small trust statement

Immediately above the text input, add subtle helper text:

> **Ask me anything. If I don't know, I'll tell you honestly—and we'll figure it out together.**

Keep this visually secondary.

This is not marketing copy.

It establishes trust.

---

### 5. Preserve launch scope

Do not change:

* authentication
* AI behavior
* prompts
* memory
* routing
* APIs
* memberships
* analytics

UI text only.

---

I actually think this is a stronger first impression than the chips.

The moment someone opens Kinfolk, they don't need to decide whether they're solving a math problem or writing an email. They just need to feel that they can start a conversation.

And there's one sentence I'd add to your Kinfolk design principles because I think it captures what you're building:

> **Kinfolk begins every conversation with curiosity, never assumptions.**

That feels like the kind of principle that will continue to guide the product years from now, no matter how many capabilities Kinfolk gains.
