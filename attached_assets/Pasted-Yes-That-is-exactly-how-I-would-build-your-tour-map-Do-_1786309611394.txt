Yes. **That is exactly how I would build your tour map.** Do not think only in terms of “places tourists visit.” You are building a living economic and cultural map. A plumber in North Carolina, dentist in South Carolina, Black Catholic church in D.C., childcare center in Atlanta, Ethiopian grocer in Maryland, and travel advisor in Houston can all be equally valuable.

And this is important for Kinfolk: **a business does not need to be glamorous, tourist-facing, or nationally known to matter.** A reliable mechanic may be more important to a traveler in trouble than a famous restaurant.

### One distinction I think is crucial

Replit should **not force all of these into the same Vibe model.**

A restaurant might have:

**Vibes:** Romantic • Turn Up • Chill • Upscale • Family-Friendly • Late Night

But a childcare center should have **The Real / practical decision factors**, such as:

**The Real:** Infant Care • After-School • Weekend Care • Extended Hours • Transportation • Meals Provided • Special-Needs Support • Multilingual • Parent Recommended

A plumber could have:

**The Real:** Emergency Service • 24/7 • Residential • Commercial • Same-Day • Licensed/Insured • Transparent Pricing • Weekend Service

A dentist:

**The Real:** Family Dentistry • Pediatric • Emergency Dental • Cosmetic • Orthodontics • Sedation Available • Insurance Accepted • Payment Plans • Accessibility • Anxiety-Friendly

An elder-care provider:

**The Real:** In-Home Care • Companion Care • Transportation • Dementia Support • Overnight Care • Respite Care • Meal Assistance • Mobility Assistance • Family Recommended

That is the principle I would have Replit retain permanently:

> **Vibes describe an experience when an experience is relevant. The Real describes the practical information someone needs to make a decision. Never manufacture a “Vibe” for a category where trust, care, safety, qualifications, accessibility or capability are the meaningful factors.**

And there is another category I would add: **“I needed this.”** These are businesses people often don't search for until something happens: mechanic, towing, urgent dentist, pharmacy, locksmith, plumber, childcare, elder care, immigration attorney, emergency lodging, etc. Kinfolk should become exceptionally good at those searches.

So on your tour, I would **not worry about filling every category evenly in every city.** If your cousin gives you a trusted childcare center with three locations in South Carolina, add it. If somebody in Raleigh tells you about an incredible Black plumber, add him. If a woman in Montgomery tells you which mechanic has treated her family fairly for 20 years, that belongs too.

Those recommendations are actually some of the most valuable community intelligence you can collect because they're difficult to get from ordinary tourism sites.
