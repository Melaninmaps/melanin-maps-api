Your LGBTQIA+ event example
Kinfolk may know that a person searched for or attended LGBTQIA+ events because they deliberately used the platform.
Kinfolk should not automatically display:
Marcus attended three LGBTQIA+ events this month.
That could disclose sexual orientation, allyship, relationships, or location patterns. None should be inferred or published.
The correct interaction is:
“You attended several community events this month. Would you like to share any of that activity on your profile?”
Then show granular options:
Share only the total number of community events
Share a specific event
Share the event category
Share the organization supported
Share a private impact milestone only to me
Do not share
Never ask me about sharing this type of activity
For sensitive categories, the safest default is:
Private unless explicitly shared.
Silence, previous sharing, or accepting an event invitation should never count as public-sharing consent.
Better public language
Instead of automatically exposing the event type:
Marcus supported 4 community events this month.
If Marcus expressly selects a particular event:
Marcus attended the Phoenix Pride Community Resource Fair.
If Marcus wants to share charitable involvement but not attendance details:
Marcus supported local LGBTQIA+ community organizations this month.
The member controls the level of detail.
Business owners as community members
A business owner should be able to display both identities without having their private behavior attached to the business.
For example:
Business profile
Johnson Family Bakery supports youth literacy programs and hosted two neighborhood food drives this year.
Owner’s Community Member profile
Marcus is a Community Member, mentor, and volunteer.
Those can be linked only when Marcus chooses something such as:
“Show me publicly as the owner of Johnson Family Bakery.”

Anonymous contributors
Anonymous should remain anonymous permanently unless the person makes a separate, explicit decision to reveal themselves.
Kinfolk may privately tell them:
“Your anonymous workplace insight helped 18 Community Members understand this employer more clearly.”
But it must not:
Display the contribution on their profile
Tell a business owner who submitted it
Use their public profile to imply authorship
Reveal enough timing or detail to identify them
invite them publicly to mentor based on an anonymous report
Kinfolk could privately ask:
“Your workplace insight has helped others. Would you like to explore mentoring in your field? This would not reveal or connect you to your anonymous submission.”
The systems must remain technically separated.
Safety rules that should be non-negotiable
No sensitive inference.
Kinfolk does not infer sexuality, religion, health, immigration status, political views, disability, or family status from searches, attendance, names, photographs, or location.
No automatic publication.
Private behavior never becomes profile activity by default.
Specific consent, not blanket consent.
“Share my community activity” is too broad for sensitive activity. Members choose the item and level of detail.
No retroactive exposure.
Changing a visibility setting should not automatically publish past activity.
No cross-role leakage.
Personal searches and attendance do not appear in a business dashboard or business profile.
No location trail.
Public event participation should not allow someone to reconstruct a member’s movements.
Minor protections.
Children and teens should have stricter defaults, parent-managed controls where appropriate, and no public attendance history.
Anonymous means technically separated.
Anonymous reports should be excluded from public-profile attribution and ordinary recommendation explanations.
Easy withdrawal.
A member can remove shared activity later without deleting their account.
Safety over growth.
A recommendation, community connection, or engagement opportunity must be suppressed when disclosure could create meaningful risk.
The deeper principle
You are trying to create a world where people can be seen, supported, and connected without forcing them to expose themselves before the world is safe enough to hold that information responsibly.
That should become a constitutional rule:
Kinfolk helps members express community belonging without requiring disclosure of sensitive identity or activity. Visibility is chosen, contextual, reversible, and never the price of participation.
And another:
A member’s private activity may improve their own experience, but it must not become someone else’s information without explicit permission.
