# I think the Community Feed should become a Living Community Feed

Not chronological.

Not viral.

Not engagement bait.

Not "For You."

Instead...

> **For This Season of Your Life**

That doesn't mean Kinfolk decides who you are.

It means it notices what you've chosen to engage with and gently adapts.

---

## Imagine the Feed has four layers

### Layer 1 — My Community

The people, businesses, organizations, ambassadors, and places I've intentionally connected with.

This should always remain the foundation.

---

### Layer 2 — My Season

This is where gradual evolution happens.

Examples:

Someone becomes engaged.

↓

Some wedding-related posts appear.

↓

Marriage.

↓

Moving.

↓

Homeownership.

↓

New baby.

↓

Parenting.

↓

Elementary school.

↓

Middle school.

↓

High school.

↓

College.

Not because Kinfolk assumed anything.

Because the member's own actions, searches, and choices gradually painted that picture.

And if they stop engaging with that content, the feed adjusts again.

---

### Layer 3 — My Community Needs Me

This is brilliant because it shifts from consumption to contribution.

Kinfolk might notice:

"You've lived in Phoenix for six months."

Instead of just showing more Phoenix content, it could ask:

> "Is there one thing you wish someone had told you before you moved here?"

Or:

> "What's one place from home you still miss?"

Now imagine someone else moving from Philadelphia to Phoenix seeing:

> "One thing I miss from Philadelphia is my neighborhood cheesecake shop."

That creates an emotional connection no algorithm can manufacture.

---

### Layer 4 — Community Momentum

Kinfolk notices opportunities to strengthen the community.

Examples:

A thoughtful employer review receives meaningful engagement.

Kinfolk:

> "Your perspective has helped others. Would you like to become a Workplace Mentor?"

A parent consistently shares college-prep resources.

Kinfolk:

> "Families have found your advice helpful. Would you like to create a guide for parents beginning the college search?"

A Community Member frequently answers relocation questions.

Kinfolk:

> "Would you be interested in becoming a Local Guide for Phoenix?"

The invitations are earned through meaningful participation, not popularity.

# One principle I would add to Kinfolk's constitution

I think this deserves to become a permanent design principle.

> **Kinfolk should never optimize for attention. It should optimize for contribution, belonging, and meaningful connection.**

That principle changes countless product decisions:

* The feed surfaces information because it is relevant to the member's current interests and community—not because it is the loudest or most sensational.
* Businesses are recommended because they fit the member's needs—not simply because they paid.
* Ambassadors are recognized for the community impact they create—not for accumulating followers.
* Members are invited to contribute because their lived experience can genuinely help someone else—not because the platform wants more content.

I also think there is one final idea that ties everything together:

### Kinfolk should understand **life seasons**, not just life events.

A move, a graduation, a new child, retirement, caring for an aging parent, starting a business, changing careers—those aren't isolated moments. They are seasons that unfold over months or years. If Kinfolk responds gradually, with the member's permission and based on their engagement, the platform feels less like software and more like a community that grows alongside them.

That's a very different philosophy from a traditional recommendation engine, and I think it's one of the defining characteristics of the platform you're building.
