A 16-year-old generally should **not need parental permission for every ordinary action**, but they should still have stronger default protections than an adult.

For your app, I would use age bands rather than one blanket “minor” rule:

### Under 13

Require verified parental consent before collecting personal information or allowing features such as account creation, location sharing, messaging, profile photos, videos, or social links. COPPA generally requires verifiable parental consent for children under 13 when an online service collects personal information from them. ([Federal Trade Commission][1])

### Ages 13–15

Allow a teen account, but require parent or guardian involvement for higher-risk features, such as:

* public social-profile links;
* precise or background location;
* direct messages from unknown adults;
* meetups;
* marketplace transactions;
* public video posting;
* purchases or subscriptions;
* joining adult-oriented groups or events.

### Ages 16–17

Allow more independence, but keep teen safeguards by default:

* age-appropriate result filtering;
* no public precise location;
* restricted adult-to-minor messaging;
* no 18+/21+ businesses, groups, events, or videos;
* privacy-first social-link visibility;
* parental oversight available but not required for every search, saved item, group, or ordinary post.

A 16-year-old could search cars, join an age-appropriate anime group, follow an HBCU, save businesses, and view verified resources without asking a parent each time. They should need approval—or be blocked entirely—for genuinely higher-risk actions.

### Age 18+

Full adult controls.

## Important distinction

There are three different things:

1. **Legal parental consent** for collecting a child’s data.
2. **Parental controls** that help families manage teen activity.
3. **Age-based safety restrictions** enforced by the platform even when a parent approves.

A parent should not be able to approve a 16-year-old into a 21+ nightclub, explicit video, adult dating environment, or unsafe direct-message scenario. Some restrictions should remain platform-enforced.

Apple also expects apps capable of collecting or sharing minors’ personal information—such as names, location, photos, videos, or chat—to maintain an appropriate privacy policy and comply with children’s privacy laws. ([Apple Developer][2])

## Recommended structure for Mapping With Melanin™

Use these profile levels:

* **Child Account:** under 13, parent-managed
* **Teen Account:** 13–15, parent-linked with higher-risk approvals
* **Older Teen Account:** 16–17, greater independence with safety defaults
* **Adult Account:** 18+

For older teens, I would offer a **Family Connection** rather than calling every action “parental permission.” That feels less infantilizing.

Example:

> **Family Connection**
> Link a trusted adult for safety settings, purchases, emergency contacts, and higher-risk permissions. Your ordinary searches, interests, and age-appropriate community activity remain private unless your family settings say otherwise.
