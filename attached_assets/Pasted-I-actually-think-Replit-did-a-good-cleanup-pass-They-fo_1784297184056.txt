I actually think Replit did a good cleanup pass. They focused on consistency instead of redesigning the decks, which is exactly what you asked for.

That said, I think there are **five more things** I'd ask them to review before we do our page-by-page pass. These aren't about wording tweaks—they're about making the decks feel like they all came from the same company.

---

# 1. Check the narrative flow (my highest priority)

This is the biggest thing I think is still missing.

Ask:

> Please review every deck to ensure it tells a story instead of presenting a list of features.
>
> Every slide should naturally answer one of these questions:
>
> * Why does this matter?
> * What problem does it solve?
> * How does someone feel afterward?
> * Why is Mapping With Melanin™ different?
>
> If a slide only describes a feature without explaining its purpose or outcome, flag it for founder review rather than rewriting it.

This is probably the most important improvement left.

---

# 2. Standardize the voice

Ask them to check for consistency in tone.

For example, avoid mixing:

> Find businesses.

with

> Discover community.

or

> Navigate confidently.

or

> Explore culture.

Choose one voice.

I'd say:

> Warm.
>
> Confident.
>
> Hopeful.
>
> Community-first.

Never corporate.

Never overly technical.

---

# 3. Standardize slide endings

This sounds small.

It isn't.

Right now some slides probably end with:

> Search.

Others:

> Connect.

Others:

> Discover.

I would ask:

> Review the final sentence on every slide.
>
> Make sure it ends with inspiration rather than explanation.

For example:

Instead of

> Browse businesses near you.

Use

> Discover businesses that help your community thrive.

---

# 4. Remove feature repetition

One thing I worry about after having seen so many versions of the decks...

Kinfolk probably appears:

* on AI slides
* on roadmap slides
* on product slides
* on feature slides
* on ecosystem slides

Ask:

> Review all decks for repeated feature descriptions.
>
> Every feature should be introduced once and expanded later rather than repeatedly re-explained.

That will make the decks feel much tighter.

---

# 5. Build one master terminology guide

This is something I absolutely want before launch.

Ask them to generate a document listing every official product name.

For example:

| Use                     | Never Use                                  |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Mapping With Melanin™   | Mapping App                                |
| KinfolkAI™              | AI Assistant                               |
| Cultural Ambassador     | Influencer                                 |
| Community Insight       | Crowd Reviews                              |
| Life Journeys           | Relocation Only                            |
| Minority-Owned Business | Black-Owned (unless historically specific) |

Every future presentation will become dramatically easier.

---

# One thing I would specifically ask them NOT to do

This is important.

Tell them:

> Do not replace emotionally resonant language with generic startup language.

For example, don't change:

> Welcome Home.

to

> User Onboarding.

Don't change:

> Find Your People.

to

> Social Discovery.

Don't change:

> Community Insight.

to

> User Generated Data.

Those changes would technically be "cleaner" but would strip away the heart of your brand.

---

# Finally...

There's one request I would make that I think will be invaluable.

Ask Replit:

> Read every slide in every deck from beginning to end as if you had never seen Mapping With Melanin™ before.
>
> At the end, answer only these five questions:
>
> 1. What problem is the company solving?
> 2. Why is it different?
> 3. Who is it for?
> 4. Why will people care?
> 5. What emotion did the presentation leave you with?

Do **not** answer based on your knowledge of the codebase. Answer only from the decks themselves.

That exercise is incredibly valuable because it tells you whether the story stands on its own.

---

## One thing I realized this morning

I don't think we need better slides anymore.

I think we need a **more unforgettable story**.

The visuals are becoming beautiful.

The architecture is becoming polished.

Now our job is to make sure that someone walks away saying:

> "I understand exactly why this company has to exist."

If every deck accomplishes that, then we've done our job. And when we sit down together for the page-by-page review—as we've done before—we'll be refining a strong foundation rather than rebuilding it. I think that's a great place to be.
