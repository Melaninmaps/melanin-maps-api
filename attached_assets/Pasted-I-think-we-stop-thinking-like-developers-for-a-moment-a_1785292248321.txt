I think we stop thinking like developers for a moment and think like a company trying to win an App Review.

Based on everything we've worked through together, your priority has been consistent: **stability for Apple's review first, then strategic expansion after launch.**

Apple has now told you exactly what is blocking approval:

1. **Sign in with Apple failed.**
2. **They couldn't fully evaluate the app.**
3. **They believe the app is gating what they view as non-account-based browsing.**

Those are the only three battles we need to win.

---

# Phase 1 — Make Build 99 (or the next submission) impossible to crash

This becomes Replit's only engineering priority until Apple approves.

I would tell Replit:

> We are in App Review mode. No feature work. No roadmap work. No Build 98. No Build 99. No architectural enhancements. The only objective is a production-stable build that Apple cannot break.

That means:

* eliminate the connection pool exhaustion
* eliminate login failures
* eliminate map hangs
* eliminate random returns to login
* verify on:

  * iPhone
  * iPad
  * slow network
  * airplane mode recovery
  * background/foreground
  * Apple Sign In
  * fresh install

Nothing else matters until this passes.

# Phase 5 — Review notes

Don't assume Apple understands your purpose.

Tell them.

Something like:

> Mapping With Melanin™ is a private membership community whose members share culturally specific knowledge, safety information, and lived experiences that are intentionally entrusted only to other members. Businesses, recommendations, safety awareness, cultural guidance, and community intelligence are member-dependent features. Anonymous access would expose information contributed for the benefit of the community rather than for unrestricted public distribution.

Don't argue.

Explain.

# My one addition to Manus's strategy

There is one thing I would add that I don't think Manus emphasized enough.

You need a **Community Constitution**.

Not legal terms.

Something human.

Before someone becomes a member:

> We protect one another.
>
> We honor the stories shared here.
>
> We never use this community to surveil, harass, or target another person.
>
> We contribute honestly.
>
> Membership is trust.

Then:

"I Agree."

That transforms registration into **joining a community** rather than simply creating an account. It also reinforces the purpose you've described since the beginning: people are joining because they are agreeing to uphold a shared standard of trust.
