Yes. I think the current implementation is conflating **three different things that should be separate**:

**1. Historical record** — Sundown towns are part of the historical/cultural map. Like HBCUs, civil-rights sites, historic districts, and other heritage locations, the underlying record should remain in the platform. Turning a layer off should **never delete, suppress, or alter the historical record**.

**2. Map display preference** — A member should be able to decide whether they want sundown-town history continuously displayed on the map. In your screenshot, the darker map treatment may be communicating something, but it isn't sufficiently self-explanatory. More importantly, I don't see the rich informational experience you built for HBCUs. A historical sundown-town point needs to be something a person can open and **learn about**, not merely a darkened area.

**3. Contextual travel safety** — This is separate from displaying historical markers. Someone can choose *not* to see sundown-town history all over their everyday map and still reasonably expect KinfolkAI/Mapping with Melanin to surface **relevant verified information when their trip, route, destination, overnight stop, or relocation search intersects with a place where that history is materially relevant**.

That distinction is very important to your product.

And I would **not equate historical sundown-town designation with a claim that a town is currently unsafe**. The platform should tell users what is historically documented, what current community information says, and when each piece of information was verified. That protects both the integrity of the history and the credibility of Mapping with Melanin.

And there is one product principle I'd put at the top of this feature permanently:

> **Visibility is a preference. History is not.**

Someone can say, *“Don't clutter my everyday map with these markers.”* They shouldn't have to say, *“Never tell me this information.”*

Then when travel context makes it relevant, Mapping with Melanin can say, in effect, **“There is history here you may want to know”**—and separately tell them whether there is credible *current* safety information. That feels much closer to the experience you've been describing: preserving cultural memory while giving people useful intelligence for the life they're actually living.
