I would make **one change** to Replit's sequence before saying "go."

I **agree with the order**, but I **don't agree with Step 1 exactly as written** because it still says "3 lines in `auth.tsx`." Authentication is your most sensitive subsystem, and we've already seen how easy it is for a small auth change to create unexpected login behavior.

## Here's the sequence I would approve

### ✅ Step 1 — LB-008 (Authentication)

**Approved with one condition:**

Before changing a single line, Replit must first show:

* the current logout flow;
* the exact three lines that will change;
* why each change fixes the race condition;
* why it cannot affect:

  * email login;
  * phone login;
  * Apple Sign-In;
  * session restoration;
  * password reset;
  * admin login.

Then implement **only those approved lines**.

No cleanup.
No refactoring.
No "while we're here."

---

I actually think this is the **most important user-facing fix**.

I would expand the scope slightly.

* Apple Maps renders.

# I would also add one new rule

Because you've caught several "literal AI" implementations recently.

Tell Replit:

> **Implementation Rule**
>
> Do not infer additional product decisions.
>
> Implement only the approved behavior.
>
> If implementation requires a product decision that has not been explicitly made, stop and ask instead of guessing.
>
> Do not expand scope.
>
> Do not simplify requirements.
>
> Do not replace approved language with developer-generated alternatives.

I think this rule will save you a tremendous amount of time because many of the issues you've pointed out—like the Kinfolk prompts or the "Black businesses" wording—weren't coding problems. They were product interpretation problems.

# My only concern: the map

If I could move one thing ahead of everything else, it would be this:

**Do not build another iOS candidate until someone can demonstrate, on an iPhone, that:**

* the base map renders;
* business pins appear;
* heritage sites appear;
* a business profile opens from a pin;
* the map no longer shows a blank or gray experience.

That's the one feature I want to see working before another submission, because it's central to the experience you want users to have.

Overall, I think the plan is strong. With the additional safeguards above, I'd be comfortable moving into implementation one blocker at a time.
