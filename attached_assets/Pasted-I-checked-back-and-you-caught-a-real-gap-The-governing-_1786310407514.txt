### One additional gap this exposed

We should **not make THE REAL merely a renamed Vibe system.**

For high-trust categories, some statements carry much more weight than others. “My child likes it here” is useful community sentiment. But “They contacted me immediately when my child was injured” speaks to **responsiveness and safety**.

So THE REAL should eventually have underlying dimensions such as:

**Trust • Communication • Reliability • Respect • Safety • Expertise/Competence • Advocacy • Responsiveness • Cultural Understanding • Accessibility/Accommodation**

The user doesn't necessarily need to see those clinical-looking categories. They can still tap the human statement:

> **“They actually listen.”**

Behind the scenes, MWM knows that contributes to **Communication/Respect**.

That becomes extremely valuable for Kinfolk.

If someone asks:

> “I need a daycare for my autistic son where they aren't going to treat him like a problem.”

Kinfolk shouldn't search for a cute “family-friendly” Vibe. It should understand the request and look for community evidence such as **“They don't shame kids,” “They know how to teach different learners,” “They advocate for the student,”** and **“They don't give up on kids.”**

### Two more categories I would flag now

This same gap probably exists anywhere **caregiving is embedded inside an otherwise experiential category**.

For example, **pet boarding/daycare** is trust-based even though other pet businesses may be experiential. And **home health/medical transportation** shouldn't inherit generic transportation language.

So rather than manually discovering these forever, I would add one permanent taxonomy field:

**`experience_system = VIBE | THE_REAL`**

at the **subcategory/service level**, not merely the top-level business category.

That prevents “Education = Vibe” from accidentally making daycare a Vibe business while an adult pottery class genuinely should be.

And one implementation gap is already documented: **THE REAL was not available to Kinfolk's context and was not searchable.**  That needs to be corrected when Replit connects the shared retrieval layer, or all this excellent language will exist in the database without actually making Kinfolk smarter.
