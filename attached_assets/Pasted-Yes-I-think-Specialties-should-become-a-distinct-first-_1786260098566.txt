Yes. I think **Specialties should become a distinct, first-class field in the business profile**, and I prefer it to burying everything as more subcategories.

The clean hierarchy is:

**Category → Subcategory → Specialties**

That solves exactly the problem you're describing.

For example:

| Category                      | Subcategory                     | Specialties                                                                     |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Legal & Government Services   | Attorneys & Law Firms           | Tax Law, Estate Planning, Immigration, Family Law, Criminal Defense             |
| Beauty & Personal Care        | Hair Salons / Stylists          | Alopecia Care, Natural Hair, Silk Press, Locs, Protective Styles                |
| Beauty & Personal Care        | Tattoo Artists & Studios        | Cover-Ups, Fine Line, Black & Grey, Color, Dark-Skin Tattooing, Scar Camouflage |
| Health & Wellness             | OB-GYN & Women's Health         | Fibroids, Endometriosis, High-Risk Pregnancy, Menopause, Fertility              |
| Financial & Business Services | Accountants & Tax Professionals | Small Business Tax, Personal Tax, Tax Resolution, Nonprofit Accounting          |
| Home & Property Services      | Real Estate Agents & Brokers    | First-Time Buyers, Investment Properties, Relocation, Luxury, VA Buyers         |

And I think this actually improves something in the taxonomy we've been building.

### Subcategory answers **“What are you?”**

A business chooses its primary business type.

**Beauty & Personal Care → Hair Salons & Stylists**

### Specialties answer **“What are you particularly good at?”**

They should be searchable and displayed prominently.

**Specializes in:**
Alopecia Care · Natural Hair · Protective Styles · Silk Press

### Vibes answer **“What does being there feel like?”**

Come As You Are · Auntie Energy · Soft Life · For Us, For Real

### What Stands Out answers **“What did Kinfolk consistently notice?”**

They Know Our Hair · Healthy Hair First · Listened To What I Asked For · I'd Book Again

### THE REAL answers **“What does the community trust them for?”**

For professional/high-trust services, this gets deeper:

**Tax Attorney**

Specialties: Tax Controversy · IRS Audits · Business Tax · Tax Debt

THE REAL:
Explained Every Step · Didn't Talk Down To Me · Made Sure I Understood Before I Signed · Didn't Make Promises They Couldn't Keep

That separation is excellent because **Specialties come primarily from the business; THE REAL comes from the community.** A lawyer can tell us they specialize in tax law. They cannot award themselves *“Explained Everything Perfectly.”*

## I would make Specialties searchable

This is where your approach can become much easier than traditional directories.

Someone shouldn't have to know the exact category hierarchy. They could search:

**“alopecia”**

and receive stylists whose profiles include **Alopecia Care**.

Or:

**“cover up tattoo”**

→ Tattoo Artists & Studios
→ Specialty: Cover-Ups

Or:

**“tax attorney”**

→ Attorneys & Law Firms
→ Specialty: Tax Law

Or eventually ask Kinfolk:

**“I need a Black hairstylist who works with alopecia.”**

Now we can combine:

**service + specialty + ownership + location + community experience**

instead of making the person hunt through hundreds of salon listings.

That is much more powerful.

## One important rule: don't let Specialties become uncontrolled free text

I would give businesses a curated, **subcategory-specific specialty list**, while allowing:

**+ Add a specialty we missed**

That suggestion should go into an admin review queue rather than immediately creating a new system-wide specialty.

Otherwise we will eventually get:

`Tax Attorney`
`Tax Law`
`Taxes`
`Tax Lawyer`
`IRS Attorney`

all meaning approximately the same thing and search becomes messy.

And I'd allow businesses to select **multiple specialties**, probably up to **5–8 initially**, rather than forcing only one.

### There's another field I think we just uncovered: **Services**

We should distinguish:

**Specialty:** Alopecia Care
**Services:** Silk Press · Trim · Wash & Go · Braids

Or:

**Specialty:** Cover-Up Tattoos
**Services:** Consultations · Custom Tattoos · Touch-Ups

Or:

**Specialty:** Tax Controversy
**Services:** IRS Audit Representation · Tax Debt Resolution · Appeals

That's valuable because **specialty means expertise**, while **service means something I can actually book/hire them to do**.

So I would now make the searchable business architecture:

**Category → Subcategory → Specialties → Services**

Then layer the community intelligence on top:

**Ownership / Identity**
**The Vibe OR THE REAL**
**What Stands Out?**
**What Locals Are Saying**
**Written Community Context**

That is a very clean system.
