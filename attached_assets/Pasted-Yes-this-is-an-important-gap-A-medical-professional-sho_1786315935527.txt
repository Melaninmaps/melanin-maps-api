Yes — this is an important gap. **A medical professional should be discoverable as a person even if they do not own the practice where they work.**

I would separate **Healthcare Professionals** from **Healthcare Businesses/Practices** in MWM. A user may be searching for “Black female OB-GYN near me,” “Latina pediatrician,” “LGBTQ+-affirming therapist,” or “doctor who understands fibroids” — the individual clinician matters just as much as the practice.

For MWM, I would also make some **cross-specialty search attributes** available because these may matter more to users than the formal specialty itself:

**Believed My Pain · Listened to Me · Explained My Options · Did Not Rush Me · Culturally Responsive · LGBTQIA+ Affirming · Trauma-Informed · Disability Accessible · Experienced With Darker Skin · Experienced With Black Maternal Health · Fibroid Knowledgeable · Endometriosis Knowledgeable · Sickle Cell Knowledgeable · HIV Knowledgeable · Trans-Inclusive Care · Spanish-Speaking / Language Options · Takes My Insurance · Self-Pay Friendly · Telehealth Available · Evening/Weekend Appointments · Accepting New Patients**

And I would distinguish three separate records:

**Professional profile** → Dr. Jane Smith, OB-GYN
**Practice profile** → Women's Health Associates
**Location** → Center City office / South Philadelphia office

That way a doctor changing employers does **not** disappear from MWM, and reviews of the clinician do not become confused with reviews of the office staff, billing department, or facility.

For your tour searches, I would especially prioritize clinicians in areas where community disparities make trusted recommendations extremely valuable: **OB-GYNs, maternal-fetal medicine, fertility, pediatricians, primary care, dentists, therapists, dermatologists, cardiologists, endocrinologists/diabetes care, sickle-cell specialists, HIV care, and elder-care clinicians.**

And one more field I would absolutely add to the professional profile: **“Why people seek this provider.”** That lets someone search naturally for *“doctor who listens about fibroid pain”* instead of having to know the term “gynecologic surgeon.” That fits your entire search philosophy much better.
