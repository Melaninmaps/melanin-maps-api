Yes — and this is important to solve **now**, because three childcare locations should not become three unrelated businesses in MWM.

I would model it as **one business/brand with multiple locations**.

For your cousin's example, imagine the company is “ABC Learning Center” with three South Carolina locations. The business should have **one parent business profile** containing the things that apply to the company overall—ownership designation, owner verification, main website/social accounts, general description, specialties, business type, and claim status. Under it would be **three location profiles**, each with its own address, coordinates/map pin, phone/hours, photos/video links when location-specific, services/specialties, accessibility information, and other location-specific details.

The critical distinction is **reviews and safety information should belong primarily to the location visited**. Someone having a wonderful experience at Location A should not automatically give Location B the same safety/review score. But the main business page could still show an aggregated company view such as “4.7 across 3 locations,” clearly identified as an aggregate.

Vibes should work similarly. There can be **brand-level attributes** and **location-level community intelligence**. Maybe all three locations specialize in early childhood education, but members consistently describe one location as “Feels Like Family” while another gets “Great for Working Parents.” We shouldn't erase that distinction.

This also makes Kinfolk much smarter. If someone asks:

> “Do you know a Black-owned childcare center near Columbia?”

Kinfolk could know the company has three locations and recommend **the closest appropriate location**, rather than displaying the same company three times as though they were competitors.

And your flywheel gets even more interesting: if members keep searching for that childcare center in Charleston but all three locations are around Columbia, MWM can eventually tell the owner, **“Members are searching for your business in the Charleston area.”** That's useful expansion intelligence.
