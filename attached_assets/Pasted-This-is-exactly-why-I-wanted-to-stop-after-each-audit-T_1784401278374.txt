## My overall grade

**Engineering:** A-

The core authentication architecture is solid:

* Email/password registration
* Login
* Password reset
* Apple Sign-In verification
* Phone OTP
* Server-side sessions

# Here's what I would change or expand

## 1. Email verification — I agree it's a P0

I completely agree this blocks launch. Right now the database has the fields, but users are never actually asked to verify their email, and the flag is never enforced. 

I would implement it like this:

**After registration:**

* Welcome screen
* "We've sent a verification email."
* Resend button (rate limited)
* Change email option if they made a typo
* Allow browsing with limited functionality
* Unlock community participation after verification (and your planned 48-hour trust period)

That aligns perfectly with your community-first philosophy.

---

## 2. Rate limiting — P0

I agree.

Unlimited login attempts, password resets, and OTP requests create both security and cost risks. 

I would also add:

* progressive delays after repeated failures;
* suspicious-IP monitoring;
* optional CAPTCHA only after unusual behavior (not for every login).

That keeps the experience friendly while protecting the platform.

---

## 3. Hardcoded test phone/OTP — P0

This one is non-negotiable.

The report found test credentials in a production route. 

Those must be removed or completely disabled before public launch.

---

## 4. Password reset should invalidate all sessions — P0

I agree.

If someone changes their password because they think their account was compromised, every active session should end immediately. 

---

# Here's where I want to improve the design

## 5. Apple account merge

Replit correctly identifies silent merging as risky. 

I would not automatically merge accounts.

Instead:

> "We found an existing Mapping With Melanin™ account using this email. Sign in to confirm it's yours before linking your Apple account."

That gives the user control and creates a clear audit trail.

---

## 6. Account states

Replit recommends adding suspension and deactivation states. 

I'd go a little further.

Suggested lifecycle:

```text
Registration Started
↓
Email Verification Pending
↓
Onboarding Incomplete
↓
Trust Period (48 hours)
↓
Active
↓
Verified Contributor
↓
Business Owner
↓
Community Leader
```

Exceptional states:

* Suspended
* Appeal Pending
* Deactivated
* Deletion Requested
* Scheduled for Deletion
* Deleted

This provides a clearer model for future moderation and recognition systems.

---

## 7. Consent tracking

I agree that a simple boolean isn't enough. 

I recommend recording:

* Terms version
* Privacy Policy version
* Community Guidelines version
* AI disclosure version
* timestamp
* device/platform

That gives you a complete record without overcomplicating the experience.

---

# One item I think is missing from the report

## Security notifications

I want users to receive emails (or push notifications) for important account events, such as:

* new device login;
* password changed;
* email changed;
* phone linked or removed;
* Apple account linked;
* account deletion requested.

This helps users detect unauthorized access early.

---

# Another feature I'd add

## Trusted devices

When someone signs in:

> "Trust this device for 30 days"

Trusted devices reduce friction while still allowing heightened verification on new devices.

This is a P2 feature—not required for launch, but worthwhile.

---

# One product decision I'd make now

## Phone-only accounts

The audit notes that phone-only users have no recovery path if they lose access to their number. 

For Day 1, I'd require every account to have a verified email address.

Phone login can still exist, but every account should ultimately be anchored to an email for recovery.

---

# Children

The report asks us to decide the minimum age. 

Given everything we've discussed over the past months, my recommendation remains:

* Adults may create independent accounts.
* Child accounts should wait until the family-management system is fully designed and implemented.

That protects both children and the platform.
