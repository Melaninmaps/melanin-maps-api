By **location-specific topic**, I mean a **location-tagged entry inside the broader topic book**—not a separate, isolated Library category.

For example, Kinfolk should work like this:

| Member asks | Kinfolk does | Library keeps |
|---|---|---|
| “What opportunities are there for women in STEM?” | Applies the Black/minority-women research lens, finds approved sources, and gives a concise answer. | An entry in the **STEM** book, tagged as **national/general**. |
| “What STEM opportunities are there in Charlotte?” | Recognizes **STEM** as the subject and **Charlotte** as the location, then searches for reputable, location-relevant sources and opportunities. | Another entry in the **same STEM book**, tagged **Charlotte, NC**. |
| “I want Black women in STEM programs near me.” | Uses the same lens, resolves the member’s stated location or asks for it only if missing, and researches the best verified sources. | A reusable STEM entry with the applicable city/region tag and citations. |

The key point is that **STEM remains one living book**. Within it, Kinfolk can surface national guidance, prior Charlotte research, Atlanta research, scholarships, local organizations, and other source-cited entries. It does not force all STEM knowledge into a “Charlotte only” folder, and it does not lose the earlier answer.

> **What Kinfolk should be:** a community research companion that understands the question, applies the MWM community lens before research, uses subject-appropriate reputable sources, gives a readable answer with citations, and continuously strengthens the Library so future members can retrieve that knowledge.

The intended sequence is: **understand the member’s question → resolve topic and location → apply the Black/minority-women context where the member has not already specified it → search approved sources → answer with citations → save the reusable explanation into the relevant living book → make it discoverable through Library search, topic pages, and Read More.**

For highly personal, sensitive, or urgent matters, Kinfolk should provide educational information and reliable next-step resources—not pretend to diagnose, give personal legal advice, or replace a qualified professional.

This means the implementation should store the **research entry and its sources**, not a private member’s full raw conversation. If several members ask similar questions, Kinfolk can reuse a current cited entry, refresh it when it becomes stale, and add new local context when a city is named.
