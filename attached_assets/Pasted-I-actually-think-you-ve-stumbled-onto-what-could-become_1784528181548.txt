I actually think you've stumbled onto what could become one of Mapping With Melanin™'s biggest differentiators.

I'd elevate this from a "promotion rule" to a **Marketplace Fairness & Partnership Charter**, and I'd absolutely treat it as a trade secret because it's about *how* your platform balances mission, trust, user needs, and commercial partnerships.

There are a few principles I would establish.

---

# Principle 1: Mission First

I would write it exactly like this internally:

> **Mapping With Melanin™ exists to maximize opportunities for minority-owned businesses while ensuring users can safely and confidently meet their needs wherever they are.**

That sentence answers almost every future edge case.

---

# Principle 2: Promotion ≠ Availability

This distinction is critical.

A non-minority-owned business can be:

* available,
* discoverable,
* recommended when necessary,

without receiving the same promotional treatment as a verified minority-owned business.

For example:

**Searching in Atlanta**

Minority-owned hotels exist.

Result:

* Minority-owned hotels appear first.
* Partner hotels appear afterward if relevant.

---

**Searching in rural Arizona**

No verified minority-owned hotels exist within the search area.

Result:

KinfolkAI might say:

> "I couldn't find a verified minority-owned hotel within your preferred area. Here are highly rated alternatives that meet your accessibility, safety, and travel preferences."

That's fulfilling the user's need without compromising your mission.

---

# Principle 3: Recommendations should happen in layers

Instead of a single ranking score, think about a decision sequence.

1. Is there a verified minority-owned option that meets the need?
2. Is it trustworthy?
3. Is it reasonably nearby?
4. Does it satisfy the user's filters?
5. If not, broaden the search.
6. Then include trusted partners or other businesses.

The weighting inside those layers can remain proprietary.

---

# Principle 4: National brands should never be treated as one business

Your Hyatt example is perfect.

Hyatt Scottsdale

≠

Hyatt Salt Lake City

≠

Hyatt Philadelphia

Each location should have its own:

* community ratings,
* safety reports,
* discrimination reports,
* accessibility information,
* owner responses,
* local management engagement.

Then the parent company can also have an enterprise profile.

Think of it like this:

```
Hyatt (Enterprise)

├── Hyatt Scottsdale
├── Hyatt Phoenix
├── Hyatt Salt Lake City
├── Hyatt Denver
└── Hyatt Philadelphia
```

KinfolkAI recommends the **location**, not just the brand.

---

# Principle 5: Enterprise accountability

This is where you become different.

Imagine Hyatt partners with Mapping With Melanin™.

Corporate receives an enterprise dashboard showing trends across all participating properties.

For example:

```
Overall Partnership Score
```

The corporate team immediately sees where attention is needed.

That's valuable to them because it helps them improve specific locations rather than treating every property the same.

---

# Principle 6: Partnership is earned

I would avoid permanent partner status.

Instead:

Partners should continually meet standards.

For example:

* community trust;
* responsiveness;
* safety;
* verified ownership information;
* policy compliance;
* unresolved serious issues.

If they consistently fall below established standards, they can lose benefits such as featured placement or partnership status.

That's not punitive—it's maintaining the integrity of the partnership.

---

# Principle 7: Recovery matters

Businesses should have a path to improve.

If a location has problems, the platform should encourage corrective action rather than simply punishing it.

For example:

> "This location has shown measurable improvement over the past six months following corrective actions."

That's useful to users and fair to businesses.

---

# KinfolkAI

Eventually KinfolkAI could explain recommendations naturally.

Suppose someone asks:

> "Where should I stay in Salt Lake City?"

It might answer:

> "There aren't currently any verified minority-owned hotels that match your search. Among other available options, this Hyatt location has consistently strong community safety feedback and accessibility ratings."

Or:

> "Although Hyatt is one of our partners, recent community reports for this specific location indicate unresolved concerns. Here are other nearby accommodations with stronger community feedback."

Notice what happened.

KinfolkAI isn't recommending or avoiding a business because it's a partner.

It's using location-specific information.

That builds trust.
