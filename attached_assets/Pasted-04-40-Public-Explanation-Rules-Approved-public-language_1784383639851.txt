04.40 Public Explanation Rules

“Community members report strong connection and repeat participation.”
“This business shows consistent community support and local engagement.”
“There is promising community activity, but more evidence is needed.”
“This resource is accessible and has helped community members successfully.”
“Community experiences are mixed.”
“This organization has demonstrated long-term community stewardship.”
“This result is visible in search but does not yet have enough community evidence for a strong recommendation.”
Prohibited public language includes:
“The Community Engine scored this neighborhood 62.”
“This business lost 18 points for weak reciprocity.”
“The algorithm believes this community is unhealthy.”
“People like you usually belong here.”
“This community is low value.”
“We secretly reduced this entity’s visibility.”
“The platform determined that these people do not support one another.”

04.44 Human Review Requirements
Human review is required when:
verified exclusion allegations exist;
retaliation is reported;
severe extraction is alleged;
conflicting evidence could materially affect promotion or recognition;
a community score changes by more than 20 points in 30 days;
manipulation is suspected;
a paid partner receives an unusually high score;
a low-confidence score would materially affect an entity;
evidence involves minors or sensitive community harm;
a community appeals the result;
the Engine proposes a Governance-level intervention;
public explanation could create reputational harm;
a score cap would block recognition or promotion;
a new evidence type is used;
the Institution Test or Generational Test is implicated.
04.45 Constitutional Rules
The Community Engine™ must:
distinguish visibility from value;
distinguish participation from belonging;
distinguish popularity from trust;
distinguish community presence from reciprocity;
protect newcomers and long-term members;
preserve disagreement where experiences differ;
avoid treating communities as monolithic;
recognize emerging communities without fabricating confidence;
prevent payment from determining Community Score;
prevent follower count from dominating community evaluation;
preserve community voice and correction pathways;
protect community stories and data;
recognize contribution without requiring public performance;
avoid penalizing communities for limited platform representation;
value long-term stewardship;
support community growth without forcing conformity;
permit appeal;
maintain full auditability;
comply with the Institution Test;
comply with the Generational Test.
04.46 Institution Test Application
Before approving a Community Engine™ rule, Mapping With Melanin™ must ask:
Would we defend this rule as a responsible way to evaluate community 25 years from now?
A rule fails the Institution Test when it:
mistakes activity for belonging;
rewards extraction;
privileges insiders;
cannot distinguish marketing from lived experience;
suppresses disagreement;
centralizes power without appeal;
treats communities as data products;
disadvantages emerging communities;
gives commercial partners hidden influence;
cannot be explained with dignity;
would become harmful at scale.
04.47 Generational Test Application
The Engine must ask:
Would future generations inherit a system that helps them strengthen community, or one that quietly defines community for them?
A rule fails the Generational Test when it:
freezes current cultural assumptions;
prevents future correction;
concentrates community authority permanently;
extracts stories without stewardship;
rewards short-term visibility over continuity;
cannot survive leadership change;
depends on founder intuition rather than documented governance;
leaves no pathway for future communities to redefine their own needs.
