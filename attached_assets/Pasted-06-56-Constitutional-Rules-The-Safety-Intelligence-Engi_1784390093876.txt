06.56 Constitutional Rules
The Safety Intelligence Engine™ must:
never promise safety;
never treat safety as binary;
distinguish concern from confidence;
distinguish risk from protective capacity;
distinguish historical evidence from current conditions;
distinguish allegation from verified finding;
distinguish low evidence from low concern;
distinguish no reports from proof of safety;
preserve context;
protect reporter privacy;
preserve accused-entity fairness;
prevent retaliation;
protect minors and dependents;
avoid unsupported identity inference;
avoid predictive-policing logic;
avoid racialized or culturally biased geographic labeling;
prevent payment from influencing safety outcomes;
preserve search access where appropriate without concealing accountability;
apply stricter standards to promotion than search;
permit corrective action without deleting history;
preserve materially different lived experiences;
communicate uncertainty;
support appeal and correction;
maintain complete auditability;
preserve emergency access during system failure;
comply with the Institution Test;
comply with the Generational Test.
06.57 Institution Test Application
Before a safety rule, evidence source, alert threshold, risk category, or intervention pathway is approved, Mapping With Melanin™ must ask:
Would we defend this as a responsible institutional safety practice 25 years from now?
A rule fails the Institution Test when it:
treats race, identity, or neighborhood demographics as danger;
resembles predictive policing;
converts popularity or silence into safety;
cannot distinguish allegation from finding;
exposes reporters;
prevents correction;
permanently punishes without review;
hides serious concerns for commercial reasons;
allows payment to reduce restrictions;
amplifies isolated weak evidence as certainty;
suppresses lived experience merely because it is uncomfortable;
cannot be audited;
cannot be explained without revealing bias;
produces greater harm when scaled;
lacks an appeal pathway;
stores sensitive data without necessity;
creates fear without actionable benefit;
creates false reassurance.
06.58 Generational Test Application
The Safety Intelligence Engine™ must also ask:
Would future generations inherit a safety system that protects their dignity and agency, or one that watches, labels, and limits them?
A rule fails the Generational Test when it:
normalizes surveillance;
preserves current bias as permanent risk logic;
cannot be governed under future leadership;
depends on founder judgment rather than documented rules;
categorizes communities permanently by historical harm;
prevents future evidence from demonstrating repair;
exposes sensitive identity information;
cannot be corrected by affected communities;
uses community reports for unrelated commercial purposes;
expands beyond its original protective purpose;
creates authority without accountability;
cannot preserve safety during leadership or technology change.
