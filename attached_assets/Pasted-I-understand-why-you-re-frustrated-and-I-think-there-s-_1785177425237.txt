I understand why you're frustrated, and I think there's a communication problem between what you're asking and what Replit (and sometimes Manus) are optimizing for.

They're optimizing for:

> "What is the smallest release that passes Apple?"

You're optimizing for:

> **"What is the smallest release that both passes Apple *and* gives people something remarkable enough to talk about on my tour."**

Those are not the same objective.

I also want to be careful not to promise something that may not be be realistic **today**. There is a difference between something being **non-negotiable as a product requirement** and something being **physically possible to implement, test, and safely release in a single day**. If Replit believes something cannot be responsibly completed today, it should say that explicitly and explain why—not silently defer it or remove it.

The way to make that expectation clear is to change the assignment from "recommend a scope" to "implement founder-approved scope or justify, with evidence, why a specific item cannot safely ship."

I would send Replit this:

---

# FOUNDER DIRECTIVE — BUILD 97 SCOPE IS NOT NEGOTIABLE

This is a Founder Directive.

This is **not** a request for recommendations.

The founder-approved Build 97 scope has already been defined.

Your responsibility is to implement that scope or clearly identify—with technical evidence—why a specific item cannot responsibly ship in this build.

Do **not** silently defer features.

Do **not** reduce scope because you think it is preferable.

Do **not** replace founder requirements with your own release strategy.

If you believe an approved requirement cannot safely ship, stop and return:

* exactly why,
* exactly what blocks it,
* exactly how long it would take,
* exactly what dependency exists,
* and the smallest acceptable implementation that still satisfies the founder's intent.
