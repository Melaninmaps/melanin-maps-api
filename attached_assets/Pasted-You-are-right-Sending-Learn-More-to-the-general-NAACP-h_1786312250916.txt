You are right. **Sending “Learn More” to the general NAACP homepage is not sufficient evidence and should be removed.** A Sundown Town entry is a serious historical designation. MWM needs to show **why the place is included, what evidence supports it, what period the evidence covers, how strong the evidence is, and whether there is any separate current safety information.**

The most important rule is that MWM must never turn **historical racial exclusion into an unsupported present-day danger claim**. A town may have documented exclusionary history without evidence that it is currently unsafe. Conversely, current community reports may reveal concerns that deserve attention even where the historical record is incomplete. Those need to remain two different evidence systems.

I would have the card work progressively. The small map card gives the short answer; **“Read the History”** opens the full MWM record instead of sending the user away. From that record, users can open the actual primary and secondary sources.

I would also change the wording on the map itself from **“Sundown Town History”** to something slightly broader like **“Historical Racial Exclusion”**, with “Sundown Town history” as one of the historical classifications underneath it. That gives you room to document places where the history involves restrictive covenants, housing exclusion or other racial barriers without forcing every location into precisely the same historical mechanism.

And the **full page should feel almost like a mini Library Book**. Someone who wants only travel context can read four sentences and close it. Someone doing a school paper, researching their family's history, or trying to understand why their grandparents could not live somewhere should be able to go all the way down to newspaper clippings, deeds, archival records, timelines and source citations.

That is treating these places with the same seriousness as Mother Bethel, an HBCU or another cultural landmark—**the painful history is part of the heritage map too.**
