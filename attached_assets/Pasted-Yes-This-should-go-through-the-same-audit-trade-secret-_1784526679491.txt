> # Product requirements to evaluate
>
> ## 1. Community-added businesses
>
> Confirm how a community member can currently add a business that has not joined.
>
> Evaluate adding the following limited fields when applicable:
>
> * audience type:
>
>   * All Ages
>   * Family Friendly
>   * Teens Welcome
>   * Adults 18+
>   * Adults 21+
>   * Unknown
> * age-restriction reason, only when reliably known;
> * environment tags;
> * basic amenities;
> * source of the information;
> * community confidence or verification status.
>
> Community contributors must be allowed to select **Unknown** and must not be encouraged to guess.
>
> Do not add these questions to the safety-report flow. A person reporting discrimination, harassment, or another safety concern should not be required to complete business-profile metadata.
>
> The owner remains the authoritative source after the profile is claimed. Owner-confirmed information should supersede community-supplied information while preserving the audit history.
>
> ## 2. Profile states
>
> Review or propose these states:
>
> * Community Listed
> * Claim Pending
> * Claimed
> * Business Verified
> * Community Confirmed
> * Information Unknown
>
> Confirm how each status affects:
>
> * search visibility;
> * map visibility;
> * KinfolkAI recommendations;
> * organic discovery;
> * promotional eligibility;
> * analytics access;
> * ability to edit information;
> * ability to respond to reviews.
>
> ## 3. Discovery versus promotion
>
> Apply this distinction:
>
> * Legitimate community-listed minority-owned businesses may remain discoverable in search, maps, and relevant organic recommendations.
> * An unclaimed profile must be clearly labeled as community listed.
> * Unclaimed businesses should not receive paid-style, featured, campaign, or premium promotional placement until claimed and appropriately verified.
> * Claiming a profile should not automatically guarantee promotion.
> * Paid participation must never be the sole determinant of recommendation order.
>
> Cross-reference this against the existing discovery and promotion trade-secret framework and identify any conflicts.
>
> ## 4. Business notifications
>
> Evaluate an aggregated notification system for eligible unclaimed minority-owned businesses.
>
> Do **not** send an email for every search, profile view, save, or review.
>
> Recommend safe trigger thresholds and digest frequencies, such as:
>
> * first meaningful-interest threshold;
> * weekly digest when activity exists;
> * monthly digest for lower activity;
> * no email when there is no meaningful new activity;
> * frequency cap and unsubscribe controls.
>
> Candidate anonymous signals:
>
> * searches;
> * profile views;
> * saves;
> * direction requests;
> * website or call actions;
> * community recommendations;
> * new reviews or feedback awaiting claim.
>
> The business must never receive:
>
> * the identity of a searching user;
> * user profiles;
> * individual search history;
> * exact user locations;
> * private saved-list information;
> * reviewer contact information;
> * private messages;
> * unmoderated safety-report details.
>
> ## 5. Review and feedback notices
>
> Recommend whether an unclaimed business should receive:
>
> * “Your business received new community feedback,”
>
> rather than the full review or reviewer identity.
>
> Any review or safety concern that requires moderation must be reviewed before triggering an external notification.
>
> Claiming the business may allow the verified owner to view and respond according to the Review Policy, but it must not reveal private user information.
>
> ## 6. Claim invitation
>
> Proposed message:
>
> **“Your community has been looking for you.”**
>
> The claim invitation may show aggregated counts, such as:
>
> * searches in the last 30 days;
> * profile views;
> * saves;
> * direction requests;
> * number of new feedback items.
>
> It must not show individual names, profiles, comments, or sensitive details before ownership verification.
>
> The call to action should be:
>
> **Claim your profile to confirm your information, respond to community feedback, and understand how people are finding your business.**
>
> ## 7. Ownership verification
>
> Recommend the minimum verification required before granting access to profile management, reviews, or analytics.
>
> Evaluate available evidence such as:
>
> * email verification;
> * business-domain email;
> * business registration or ownership documentation;
> * authorized representative documentation;
> * phone verification using a publicly listed business number;
> * website or social-account verification;
> * manual admin review for conflicts.
>
> Email possession alone should not automatically prove ownership when the email is unrelated to the business.
>
> Before verification, the claimant must not receive detailed analytics, review content, or management rights.
>
> ## 8. Contact-source integrity
>
> Identify where the business email or contact information comes from.
>
> Do not scrape, infer, or use a private address without authorization.
>
> Categorize contact sources, such as:
>
> * owner supplied;
> * community supplied;
> * publicly listed business contact;
> * previously verified;
> * unknown.
>
> Recommend which source types are safe for claim invitations and what validation is required before email is sent.
>
> ## 9. Protection against abuse and gaming
>
> Evaluate protections against:
>
> * fake searches intended to trigger a business email;
> * repeated searches by the same account;
> * competitors claiming a profile;
> * fabricated audience classifications;
> * coordinated review activity;
> * repeatedly inviting a business that declined;
> * inflated analytics;
> * exposing small-number activity that could identify a user.
>
> Recommend aggregation thresholds, rate limits, cooldown periods, and audit logging.
>
> ## 10. Admin rights
>
> Recommend the admin permissions needed to:
>
> * review community-added businesses;
> * correct or mark disputed classifications;
> * approve or reject claim requests;
> * view verification evidence securely;
> * suspend claim access;
> * manage notification eligibility;
> * suppress an incorrect contact address;
> * preview an email before sending;
> * see notification history;
> * see profile-status history;
> * resolve conflicting ownership claims;
> * export audit records;
> * restore prior profile data.
>
> Separate super-admin rights from ordinary moderation or support rights.
>

* community members can add applicable audience information with **Unknown** available;
* unclaimed profiles are clearly labeled;
* claimed owners can correct and confirm the information;
* unclaimed minority-owned businesses remain organically discoverable;
