06. SAFETY INTELLIGENCE ENGINE™
Future-State Production Specification v1.0
Continuation — Batch 2
06.9 Standard Safety Output Model
The Safety Intelligence Engine™ must never return a single unlabeled “safety score.”
Every consequential assessment returns at least four separate values:
Concern Score — the estimated level of current concern;
Protective Capacity Score — the strength of available safeguards and support;
Confidence Score — the reliability of the assessment;
Immediacy Level — how quickly the condition may require attention.
The four values must remain independent.
Example:
Concern Score:             72
Protective Capacity Score: 81
Confidence Score:          88
Immediacy Level:           1 — Time-sensitive
This means:
Meaningful concern exists, but strong protections and reliable support are also present.
A high concern score does not automatically mean “avoid.”
A high protective capacity score does not erase a serious risk.
A high confidence score does not make a moderate concern severe.
06.10 Concern Score
The Concern Score measures the estimated level of adverse safety conditions associated with the evaluated object and context.
It is normalized from 0 to 100.
Score	Internal classification	General interpretation
90–100	Critical	Severe, immediate, or highly credible concern
75–89	High	Significant concern requiring strong caution
60–74	Elevated	Meaningful concern affecting recommendation
40–59	Moderate	Mixed or situational concern
20–39	Limited	Some concern, but not strongly established
1–19	Minimal	Little current evidence of concern
0	No usable concern signal	No eligible concern evidence identified
A score of zero does not mean safety is guaranteed.
It means the Engine does not currently have eligible evidence supporting a concern.
06.11 Protective Capacity Score
The Protective Capacity Score measures the availability, reliability, and accessibility of safeguards that may reduce harm or improve response.
It may evaluate:
staff responsiveness;
emergency exits;
security procedures;
public transportation availability;
nearby healthcare;
community support;
accessibility accommodations;
verified incident response;
lighting;
emergency communication;
organizer preparation;
route alternatives;
trusted contact availability;
complaint-resolution pathways;
institutional accountability.
06.11.1 Protective Capacity Formula
Protective Capacity Score =
  Prevention Capacity
+ Response Capacity
+ Access to Assistance
+ Accountability Capacity
+ Recovery Capacity
- Protection Failure Penalties
The result is normalized to 0–100.
06.12 Immediacy Levels
Every concern receives an Immediacy Level.
Level	Name	Meaning
0	Historical or informational	Relevant but not currently active
1	Time-sensitive	May affect a current or upcoming decision
2	Priority	Material risk may arise soon
3	Urgent	Credible near-term threat or rapidly worsening condition
4	Emergency	Immediate danger or emergency assistance request
Immediacy is not the same as severity.
A severe historical event may have low immediacy.
A moderate weather condition may have high immediacy.
06.13 Core Safety Dimensions
The launch-state Safety Intelligence Engine™ evaluates eight dimensions.
Dimension	Baseline concern weight
Physical Safety	22%
Cultural & Discrimination Safety	18%
Identity-Related Safety	12%
Environmental Safety	12%
Health & Emergency Access	10%
Accessibility Safety	10%
Family & Dependent Safety	8%
Digital & Transaction Safety	8%
Total	100%
The Context Engine™ may change these weights.
A dimension that is not relevant must be marked NOT_APPLICABLE, not assigned an artificial zero.
06.14 Dimension One — Physical Safety
Physical Safety evaluates the likelihood or presence of bodily harm, dangerous conditions, harassment, violence, theft, unsafe infrastructure, or physical obstruction.
It may include:
violence;
threats;
assault;
theft;
robbery;
stalking;
harassment;
unsafe traffic;
poor lighting;
blocked exits;
overcrowding;
structural hazards;
unsafe transportation;
weapons-related incidents;
inadequate emergency access;
dangerous environmental conditions.
06.14.1 Physical Concern Formula
Physical Concern =
  Incident Severity
× Credibility
× Context Relevance
× Recency
× Exposure Likelihood
× Immediacy
Normalized to 0–100.
06.14.2 Physical severity factors
Condition	Severity factor
Immediate threat to life	1.00
Serious bodily harm	0.95
Credible threat or stalking	0.85
Physical harassment or intimidation	0.75
Theft or significant property threat	0.60
Hazardous physical condition	0.55
Minor physical concern	0.30
Unclear or non-specific concern	0.15
Severity is only one component.
A severe but unverified allegation must not automatically produce a Critical public classification.
