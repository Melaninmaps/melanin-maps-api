## I want to leave you with one thought that I think could become a guiding principle for the entire platform.

You asked:

> *"What journeys should we capture, and what shouldn't we?"*

I don't think the answer is based on the topic.

I think it's based on this question:

> **"Will this matter to someone five years from now?"**

If the answer is yes, it's probably a Journey.

* Choosing a college? Yes.
* Starting a business? Yes.
* Reconnecting with your heritage? Yes.
* Planning a wedding? Yes.
* Becoming a parent? Yes.
* Moving to a new city? Yes.
* Mentoring the next generation? Absolutely.

If it's simply completing a transaction—finding a tutor for one afternoon, buying a wrench, booking a haircut—that's valuable, but it's a service, not a Journey.

The Journeys should be the chapters that shape people's lives.

And here's what I think is so remarkable about what you're building:

Most platforms celebrate **activity**.

You're designing a platform that celebrates **growth**.

That's a very different mission.

A final thought I'd preserve in your founder philosophy:

> **Mapping With Melanin™ does not measure success by how often someone opens the app. It measures success by whether it helped someone grow, connect, remember, contribute, and eventually help someone else along the same path.**

That last step—**from learner to guide**—is what completes the circle. It's why the alumni video matters. It's why the bakery owner tells their story. It's why the Cultural Ambassador exists. The end of every meaningful journey isn't just personal achievement—it's leaving something behind for the next person who begins. I think that is one of the most distinctive ideas we've developed together.
