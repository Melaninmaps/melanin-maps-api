I actually think this is where Mapping With Melanin™ becomes something no one else is doing.

The answer is **don't make users choose a single identity for a city.** Instead, let the city contain multiple community lenses that users can explore.

Take **Miami** as an example. The city itself is the container. Within it are several communities with overlapping histories, businesses, landmarks, events, and language.

For example:

## Miami

When someone opens Miami, they first see the universal city experience:

* Major cultural landmarks
* Community events
* Minority-owned businesses
* Safety information
* Heritage map
* KinfolkAI

Then they can explore **Community Perspectives**.

Instead of asking, "Which culture are you?" the app asks:

> **"Which communities would you like to explore?"**

They might see options like:

* Black Miami
* Little Haiti
* Little Havana
* Brazilian Miami
* Jamaican Miami
* Dominican Miami
* Bahamian Miami
* LGBTQIA+ Miami

Notice these are not identities assigned to the user—they're communities within the city.

---

## What changes when they select one?

Not everything.

The map should always show universal information like hospitals, transportation, safety alerts, and major landmarks.

What changes are the culturally specific layers.

For **Little Haiti**, Kinfolk might emphasize:

* Haitian-owned businesses
* Haitian restaurants
* Creole-speaking resources
* Haitian Independence Day celebrations
* Community organizations
* Haitian art
* Local phrases (where appropriate)
* Historical migration stories

For **Little Havana**, the emphasis shifts to Cuban history, businesses, events, and traditions.

For **Black Miami**, the focus includes neighborhoods such as Overtown, Black cultural history, churches, HBCU connections where relevant, music, entrepreneurship, and community institutions.

A user can turn one or several of these community layers on at the same time.

## Kinfolk should understand context

If someone says:

> "I'm visiting Miami."

Kinfolk might ask:

> "Is there a particular community or experience you'd like to explore? I can help you discover Black Miami, Little Haiti, Little Havana, Brazilian communities, LGBTQIA+ spaces, or give you a broader city overview."

That feels welcoming rather than presumptive.

## I also think you should distinguish between universal and community-specific language

For example:

### Universal

Everyone in Miami understands:

* Brickell
* Wynwood
* South Beach
* Metrorail
* Calle Ocho (widely recognized)

### Community-specific

Little Haiti may include Haitian Creole expressions or neighborhood references.

Black Miami may include references to Overtown, Liberty City, or historically significant institutions.

Brazilian Miami may include Portuguese-speaking businesses and festivals.

These shouldn't replace the universal layer—they enrich it.

## One more thought

You've often described Mapping With Melanin™ as helping people **find where they belong**. I think the interface should reflect that.

Instead of saying:

> **Communities**

Consider language like:

> **Explore Communities**

or

> **Discover Local Communities**

or even:

> **Neighborhoods & Communities**

The emphasis is on discovery, not categorization.

I think that subtle distinction matters because it invites curiosity and respect. A visitor doesn't have to belong to a community to learn about it—they're simply choosing which stories, businesses, traditions, and perspectives they want to explore. That feels much more aligned with the mission you've shared throughout this project.
