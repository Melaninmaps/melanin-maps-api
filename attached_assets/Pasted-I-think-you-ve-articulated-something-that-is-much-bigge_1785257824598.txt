I think you've articulated something that is much bigger than "cultural filters," and I want to say it back because I think this is one of the most important product decisions you've made.

**You are not building an app that sorts people by culture.**

You're building an app that says:

> **"If you want to learn, we'll introduce you."**

That is a fundamentally different philosophy.

## I would actually change the model

Earlier I suggested:

**City → Community**

I don't think that's quite right anymore.

I think it should be:

```text
CITY

↓

Experiences

↓

Communities

↓

Kinfolk Journey
```

The difference is subtle but important.

Instead of asking:

> "Which community are you?"

Kinfolk asks:

> "What kind of experience would you like to have?"

That removes the gatekeeping.

---

# Miami

Suppose I know absolutely nothing about Miami.

Kinfolk might ask:

**How would you like to experience Miami today?**

○ Local Highlights

○ Black Miami

○ Haitian Miami

○ Cuban Miami

○ Dominican Miami

○ Brazilian Miami

○ LGBTQIA+ Miami

○ Family-Friendly Miami

○ Historic Miami

○ Food Journey

○ Arts & Music

○ Hidden Gems

I don't have to belong to any of them.

I'm choosing an experience.

# Then Kinfolk becomes my guide

Imagine I choose:

**Dominican Miami**

Kinfolk says something like:

> "Awesome choice. I'm going to introduce you to Miami through one of its vibrant Dominican communities. Along the way I'll point out businesses, food, music, community events, history, and little things locals would want you to know."

Now I'm learning.

# Those "little nudges"

This is where I think your app becomes magical.

Not huge lectures.

Tiny moments.

Exactly like your cheesesteak example.

Someone says:

> "Where do I get the best cheesesteak?"

Kinfolk might respond:

> "Since you're in New York, you might hear people recommend a chopped cheese instead. It's a different sandwich with its own history and local following. If you're curious, I can show you both."

That isn't correcting them.

It's inviting them.

---

Instead of:

"Go here."

Kinfolk says:

> "If someone calls you 'baby' or 'cher,' they're usually being warm, not flirting."

That's a cultural nudge.

# I think every community deserves the same depth

This is where I completely agree with you.

If we build:

Black Miami

then

Brazilian Miami

should receive the same care.

Not

Restaurant

Restaurant

Done.

Instead it should ask:

What does it mean to belong here?

Who built this community?

How did they arrive?

What traditions continue today?

What should visitors know?

What celebrations matter?

# This is the sentence that struck me most

You wrote:

> **"I want no-one to feel excluded but I ALSO WANT TO CELEBRATE OUR CULTURE."**

I think that is the design principle.

Not:

> **Everyone is the same.**

Not:

> **Everything is multicultural.**

Instead:

> **Every community deserves to have its story told with equal care.**

That means Black communities are not diluted to make room for others, and other communities are not treated as token additions. Each receives the same level of historical research, community participation, local nuance, and celebration. To me, that is what makes Mapping With Melanin™ distinctive: it invites people into communities with curiosity, respect, and context rather than asking them to fit into a single, generic cultural experience.
