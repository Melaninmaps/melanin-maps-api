The structure is strong, but I would **not approve Phase B exactly as written**. Two parts need to be corrected before any production import.

First, the current production backup should not rely on you copying query results into Replit and then storing personal email data inside the Replit workspace or Git history. Git is difficult to purge, and a repository is not the right permanent location for a database export containing user emails.

Second, the proposed rollback condition is too broad:

```sql
created_at >= import_timestamp
AND notes LIKE 'Sources: %'
```

That could accidentally delete a legitimate website signup created during the import window or another record with similar notes. Every imported record needs a unique, import-specific identifier.

## What should happen after the import

Before building the larger city-release system, Replit should test the existing waitlist process end to end:

1. Submit a new test signup through the public website.
2. Confirm it appears exactly once in Railway production.
3. Confirm it appears in the Admin Waitlist tab.
4. Confirm the submitted fields are accurate.
5. Confirm no welcome email is sent unless that automation is intentionally enabled.
6. Approve the test record and verify what approval actually changes.
7. Deny or defer a separate test record and verify the behavior.
8. Confirm ordinary admins cannot perform super-admin-only actions.
9. Remove only the clearly identified test records after the test, with an audit record.

I would also ask them to define what **Approve**, **Deny**, and **Waiting for City** mean before launch. “Denied” may be unnecessarily harsh for someone who is simply not in the first release wave. A safer initial structure is:

* **Waiting**
* **Selected for Wave**
* **Invited**
* **Registered**
* **Waiting for City**
* **Deferred**
* **Declined/Invalid**

For a city rollout such as “first 20 Atlanta community members and 5 Atlanta businesses,” begin with a **preview-and-confirm release tool**, not a fully automatic release. The system should show exactly which 25 records would be selected, why they qualify, and whether required fields are missing before you press a final confirmation. This protects you from accidentally selecting test accounts, duplicates, invalid addresses, or businesses that have not been verified.
