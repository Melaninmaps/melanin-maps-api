## 1. I agree: "Safety Score" should be removed from Build 97

After everything we've designed today, I no longer think "Safety Score" represents what Mapping With Melanin™ is trying to accomplish.

It is too narrow and too easy to misunderstand.

More importantly, it can unintentionally imply that the platform is judging a community rather than helping people understand it.

I would ask Replit to remove **every member-facing use** of "Safety Score" for Build 97 and replace it with a temporary, more accurate label until the complete Community Health model is implemented.

### My recommendation

For Build 97 use:

> **Community Insights**

or

> **Community Snapshot**

I slightly prefer **Community Insights** because it already hints that multiple factors contribute to what members are seeing.

Then, in future phases, Community Insights naturally evolves into the full **Community Health Profile** without requiring another major terminology change.

# 2. Community Health Profile should absolutely be treated as trade-secret architecture

Not because the phrase itself is protectable.

The phrase **Community Health Profile** is descriptive.

What is valuable—and potentially protectable as a trade secret—is **how Kinfolk calculates and presents it**.

That includes:

* What signals are used.
* How those signals are weighted.
* How recommendations are generated.
* How confidence is determined.
* How conflicting information is resolved.
* How the model avoids reinforcing bias.
* How Community Understanding influences results.

Those internal methods are the secret sauce.

I would not publicly document the scoring or weighting formulas.

# 5. Some dimensions should never dominate

For example:

One discrimination report

≠

Entire neighborhood unsafe.

Likewise:

One positive review

≠

Entire neighborhood welcoming.

Kinfolk should look for patterns over time and clearly communicate uncertainty.

---

# 6. Weighting philosophy

Rather than thinking about numbers first, I'd tell Replit to think about **evidence quality**.

Something like:

### Highest confidence

* Verified member observations.
* Multiple independent reports.
* Confirmed business information.
* Official public information where appropriate.

---

### Medium confidence

* Consistent community trends.
* Community surveys.
* Community organization contributions.

---

### Lower confidence

* Individual opinions.
* Single anecdotal reports.
* Emerging observations awaiting confirmation.

That allows Kinfolk to explain *why* it is surfacing something without overclaiming certainty.
