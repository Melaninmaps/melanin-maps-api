# Review of the P0 Findings

## 1. Event cancellation without notifying attendees

I completely agree this is P0.

A member who RSVPs should never have to discover an event was cancelled by simply arriving at the venue. The audit correctly identifies that deleting an event currently provides no attendee notification, no cancellation lifecycle, and no opportunity for explanation. 

### Founder Decision FD-9.8 — Event Cancellation Lifecycle™

I recommend sealing this now.

Every cancellation should become an event lifecycle, not a deletion.

Stages:

* Scheduled
* Updated
* Cancelled
* Rescheduled
* Completed
* Archived

Cancellation should include:

* timestamp
* organizer explanation (optional but encouraged)
* attendee notification
* calendar update
* refund pathway if applicable
* organizer contact option
* archive for institutional history

Deleting an active event should become an exceptional administrative action—not the normal workflow.

---

## 2. Silent changes to time or location

Also correctly classified as P0.

Changing:

* date
* time
* venue
* city

changes the agreement members accepted when they RSVP'd. Those changes must trigger communication. 

### Founder Decision FD-9.9 — Material Event Change™

A "material change" includes:

* date
* time
* location
* organizer
* capacity
* admission
* accessibility accommodations

Material changes require:

* attendee notification;
* update history;
* visible "Updated" indicator;
* option to withdraw RSVP.

---

## 3. Travel Planner without authentication or membership enforcement

This confirms a pattern we've now seen in multiple audits.

Audit 7 identified unrestricted AI.

Audit 8 identified entitlement inconsistencies.

Audit 9 confirms another unrestricted AI endpoint with meaningful cost exposure. 

I agree this remains a launch blocker.

---

## 4. AI-generated Black-owned status

I strongly agree.

This is bigger than the travel planner.

This becomes a constitutional rule.

### Founder Decision FD-9.10 — Ownership Integrity™

Ownership may originate only from:

* platform verification;
* verified owner claim;
* approved verification partner;
* explicitly identified authoritative source.

AI may never infer:

* minority ownership;
* women-owned status;
* veteran-owned status;
* LGBTQ-owned status;
* disability-owned status.

Unknown must remain unknown.

This reinforces the Source Hierarchy™ we established in Audit 7.

## 5. Experience Memory™

This is the finding that excites me the most because it is not merely a missing feature—it is the missing connective tissue you've been envisioning for months.

The audit captures it perfectly:

> Experiences are discovered but not remembered, reflected upon, connected to Community Journey™, or carried forward into Legacy & Learning. 

I would elevate Experience Memory™ into one of the defining intellectual properties of Mapping With Melanin™.

---

# Experience Memory™ (Founder Expansion)

I don't want Experience Memory™ to become another journal.

I want it to become **the story of a member's life with their community.**

After meaningful experiences, KinfolkAI could gently ask:

* What made today meaningful?
* Did you discover someone you'll stay connected with?
* Would you return?
* Did anything surprise you?
* What would you tell someone visiting for the first time?

The member may answer—or ignore it.

Those reflections become:

* Community Journey™
* Legacy & Learning Engine™
* Community Ripple™
* Community Echo™
* Community Recognition™

Not through automatic scoring, but through intentional reflection.

---

# Experience Continuity™

I believe this deserves to become one of the platform's core architectural principles.

The audit rightly notes that every feature currently exists in isolation. 

I would define Experience Continuity™ as:

> **Every meaningful experience should create the opportunity for another meaningful experience—without forcing it.**

Examples:

Attend event

↓

Meet organizer

↓

Discover Hidden Gem nearby

↓

Join Circle

↓

Volunteer

↓

Mentor

↓

Become Community Builder

↓

Teach others

Not because the system pushes people endlessly, but because it recognizes natural pathways for deeper belonging.

# Founder Decisions

I would resolve several now.

## FD-9.1 — Private events

Decision:

Private by invitation should remain private.

Creators may convert a private event to public.

Public events should not become private after attendees have RSVP'd without explicit communication.

---

## FD-9.2 — Event photos

Ownership remains with the creator.

Attendees retain ownership of their own uploads.

Organizers receive display rights only within the event context.

Reuse elsewhere requires permission consistent with your broader content policies.

---

## FD-9.3 — Event retention

I recommend:

Never permanently delete completed community history unless required.

Lifecycle:

* Active
* Completed
* Archived
* Historical

Archived events continue contributing to Community Journey™, Legacy, and institutional memory.

---

## FD-9.4 — AI recommending moderated events

Decision:

No.

Events under moderation should not be recommended until moderation resolves.

---

## FD-9.5 — Paid events

I recommend:

Version 1:

Support links to external ticketing.

Version 2:

Native ticketing.

Keep the initial commercial scope manageable.

---

## FD-9.6 — Community Recognition

My recommendation:

Attendance alone:

No.

Meaningful participation:

Yes.

Examples:

* organizer;
* volunteer;
* speaker;
* mentor;
* community contributor;
* recurring participant.

Recognition should reward contribution rather than presence.

---

## FD-9.7 — Business invitations

I recommend:

Businesses may invite:

* followers;
* saved customers;
* prior attendees.

Not arbitrary platform-wide messaging.

Members should remain in control of receiving invitations.

# One new Founder Decision

## FD-9.11 — Experience Completion™

What makes an experience complete?

I recommend:

An experience becomes complete when one of these occurs:

* member checks in;
* member confirms attendance;
* organizer confirms participation;
* member voluntarily reflects afterward.

Completion should never require a social post.
