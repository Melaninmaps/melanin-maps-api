# My review of the P0 findings

## 1. Email delivery without retry

I completely agree this is P0.

Password resets, billing confirmations, verification emails, and safety communications should never rely on a single delivery attempt. The audit correctly identifies that there is no retry, backoff, or recovery path if the email provider experiences a temporary failure. 

### Founder Decision FD-10.9 — Critical Communication Reliability™

I recommend sealing this now.

Communications should be classified into delivery classes:

**Class A — Critical**

* Password reset
* Account verification
* Security alerts
* Billing confirmations

These require delivery tracking and retry.

**Class B — Important**

* Event updates
* Membership changes
* Safety follow-ups

Retries are appropriate with reasonable limits.

**Class C — Informational**

* Digests
* Community summaries
* Promotions

Best-effort delivery is acceptable.

Not every email deserves the same delivery guarantee.

---

## 2. Unsubscribe infrastructure

I agree this is one of the most important institutional findings.

The audit shows:

* unsubscribe links exist in only 2 of 38 email types;
* no backend unsubscribe route;
* missing commercial email requirements. 

This is larger than legal compliance.

It is about respect.

A member should never feel trapped into receiving communications.

---

## 3. Quiet Hours

This finding is exactly why audits matter.

The platform already:

* stores the preference;
* lets members configure it;
* saves it successfully.

Then ignores it during push delivery. 

That creates disappointment because the member believes they have control.

---

### FD-10.2

I think we can seal this.

My recommendation:

Quiet Hours apply to:

* community
* promotional
* engagement
* recommendations
* AI nudges
* business broadcasts
* appreciation
* recognition

Quiet Hours do **not** suppress:

* password resets
* account security
* verified emergency safety alerts
* confirmed ICE alerts
* confirmed life-safety notifications
* billing fraud alerts

Those should override Quiet Hours because the purpose is protection, not engagement.

---

## 4. Duplicate notification route

I agree this belongs in P0.

Not because it's a routing bug.

Because communication should have:

> **one authoritative source.**

Having two competing notification routes introduces ambiguity into a system whose job is to reduce ambiguity.

* Can I change this?

That framework aligns beautifully with your constitutional philosophy. 

I would adopt it permanently.

## FD-10.5 — KinfolkAI follow-ups

I recommend:

Default:

Never proactive.

KinfolkAI should respond when invited.

Members may opt into:

* reminders;
* Community Journey reflections;
* saved topic updates;
* Opportunity alerts.

But proactive AI should always be member-controlled.

---

## FD-10.7 — Invitation limits

I recommend:

Daily limits.

Not lifetime.

Examples:

* Circle invitations
* Group invitations
* Friend requests
* Event invitations

Limits should expand as trust increases, but they should never become unlimited. That protects members without discouraging genuine organizers.

---

## FD-10.8 — Communication retention

I agree with the audit's recommendation and would refine it slightly:

* **Security & billing communications:** retained indefinitely and always accessible.
* **Safety communications:** retained for one year with search capability.
* **Moderation decisions:** retained while relevant to the account and appeal process.
* **Community and promotional notifications:** retained for 90 days by default.
* **Members should be able to review their communication history without relying on their email inbox.**

# One new Founder Decision

## FD-10.10 — Explain Every Notification™

Every push notification should answer at least one of these within the notification itself or immediately after opening it:

* Why am I receiving this?
* Why now?
* Why was I selected?
* What action is recommended?
* Where can I change this preference?

That becomes the implementation rule for the Communication Trust Model™.
