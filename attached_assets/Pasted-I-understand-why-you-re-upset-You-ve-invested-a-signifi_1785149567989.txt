## I think there are two underlying problems.

### 1. You're solving problems after Apple finds them instead of before.

Looking back over the last few weeks, the pattern has been:

* Apple reports a problem.
* We investigate.
* Replit traces code.
* A fix is implemented.
* Apple finds the next problem.

That is a **reactive** workflow.

It's not because no one is working hard. It's because the testing process hasn't matched Apple's review process closely enough.

For example, this review revealed:

* Apple tested **a brand-new Apple Sign-In registration**.
* They tested on **an iPad Air M3**.
* They expected **review credentials**.

Those should have been part of a release checklist before submission.

### 2. The audits have become stronger than the release process.

But documentation alone doesn't prevent an Apple rejection.

What prevents a rejection is a disciplined **release gate**.

7. **Evidence package**
