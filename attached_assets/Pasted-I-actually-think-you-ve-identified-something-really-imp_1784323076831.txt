# 17. Visual Identity & Emotional Design

Every presentation should immediately feel like **Mapping With Melanin™** before a single word is read.

## Color Hierarchy

Our visual identity is built on warmth, richness, and belonging.

### Primary Backgrounds

* Warm Espresso Brown
* Deep Walnut
* Rich Cocoa
* Soft Cream

Black should be used **sparingly** and only for moments of intentional emotional impact (such as the KinfolkAI™ reveal or dramatic pauses). It should never become the dominant visual language of the presentation.

Our audience should feel warmth—not darkness.

---

## Accent Colors

Gold represents trust, excellence, celebration, and discovery.

Use gold intentionally to draw attention to:

* Key words
* Important statistics
* Calls to reflection
* Section transitions
* Brand moments

Avoid using gold as decoration alone.

Every use of gold should communicate importance.

---

## Typography Hierarchy

Typography should guide emotion.

### Level 1

Large headline

One emotional thought.

Nothing competing with it.

---

### Level 2

Supporting thought

Conversational.

Readable from the back of the room.

---

### Level 3

Context only.

Never paragraphs unless absolutely necessary.

If the audience is reading instead of listening, there is too much text.

---

## Word Emphasis

Never bold entire paragraphs.

Instead, emphasize only the emotional word.

Example:

> Technology enables the **experience.**

> Community creates it.

or

> We aren't mapping **places.**

> We're mapping **belonging.**

That single highlighted word should naturally draw the audience's eye.

---

## Shading & Contrast

Create visual hierarchy through subtle contrast—not excessive effects.

Use:

* slightly lighter warm browns for supporting content,
* cream for readability,
* muted gold for emphasis,
* soft shadows only when they improve legibility.

Avoid:

* harsh black backgrounds,
* high-contrast neon,
* heavy gradients,
* overly dramatic drop shadows.

Everything should feel refined and timeless.

---

## Custom Mapping With Melanin™ Icons

Whenever a visual marker is needed, use our custom outlined gold icon set.

These icons are part of our visual language and should replace generic bullets whenever appropriate.

Examples include:

* outlined gold location pin
* outlined gold heart
* outlined gold compass
* outlined gold handshake
* outlined gold community circle
* outlined gold business storefront
* outlined gold shield
* outlined gold passport
* outlined gold suitcase
* outlined gold conversation bubble

Standard bullets should only be used when custom icons would reduce clarity.

---

## Lists

Lists should never feel like documents.

They should feel like conversations.

Prefer:

✓ icon

or

outlined gold icon

instead of

• bullet

Keep spacing generous.

Never create dense blocks of bullets.

---

## Section Dividers

Each major act should have its own emotional transition.

Instead of simply changing slides...

Allow the audience to pause.

Think:

warm fade

↓

single sentence

↓

breath

↓

next chapter

The deck should feel like chapters in a story—not slides in a presentation.

---

## Texture

Subtle texture is encouraged.

Think:

* handmade paper
* soft linen
* leather
* wood grain
* warm lighting

Never obvious.

Never distracting.

Just enough to feel human.

---

## The Emotional Palette

Every slide should answer this question:

**How should this slide feel?**

Examples:

Opening → hopeful

Trust → reassuring

Safety → protected

Community → welcomed

KinfolkAI™ → curious

Business → empowered

Mission → inspired

Closing → home

Design should reinforce emotion—not simply decorate information.
