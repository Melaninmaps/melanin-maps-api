I actually think this is the right time to change **how** Replit works on the project, not just **what** it works on.

Based on everything we've done over the past several months, Mapping With Melanin™ has become too large to safely develop one conversation at a time. There are now hundreds of interconnected features. The biggest risk is no longer "forgetting an idea"—it's **breaking an existing system or building a duplicate because no one realized something already existed.**

The heritage audit already proved that. We discovered there are database tables, APIs, and even moderation workflows that neither of us expected were already present. 

## I would move to an "audit → design → implement" workflow

Instead of:

> Build Feature A

I would require:

### Phase 1 — Read-only Capability Audit

Questions like:

* What already exists?
* What is partially built?
* What APIs already exist?
* What database tables already exist?
* What admin functionality exists?
* What UI exists?
* What is duplicated?
* What is hidden behind feature flags?
* What future-state ideas already reference this feature?

No code changes.

---

### Phase 2 — Architecture Review

After the audit:

* What should stay?
* What should change?
* What should be reused?
* What should become reusable?
* What founder decisions are required?

Still no code.

---

### Phase 3 — Founder Approval

Only then:

> Implement Wave 1.

---

### Phase 4 — Post-Implementation Audit

Immediately after implementation:

* Did everything requested get built?
* Did anything break?
* Did new technical debt appear?
* Were future-state documents updated?
* Were new APIs documented?
* Did acceptance tests pass?

That creates a continuous audit trail.

---

## I would also change how Replit implements work

Instead of asking Replit to build an entire feature at once, I would require **implementation waves**.

For example, for Maps:

* **Wave 1:** Make the existing system fully functional.
* **Wave 2:** Improve content quality and administrative tools.
* **Wave 3:** Introduce saved places and official links.
* **Wave 4:** Add place-connected videos.
* **Wave 5:** Add alumni and mentorship.
* **Wave 6:** Add Living Legacy Stories.

Each wave would have:

* A read-only audit before work begins.
* Founder approval.
* Implementation.
* Acceptance testing.
* A post-implementation audit.
* An update to the Future-State Register.

That dramatically reduces the risk of regressions while ensuring every approved idea remains documented and connected to the platform's long-term vision.

I think this approach fits where Mapping With Melanin™ is today. The platform is no longer just an app under construction—it's evolving into a substantial product with many interconnected systems. Treating each major area as a documented, auditable product in its own right will make future development much safer and easier to manage.
