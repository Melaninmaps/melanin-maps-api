Yes. Both points make sense, and they should become explicit architecture rules now.

On the first issue: **we should never treat “Replit cannot find the profile” as proof the user or their content does not exist.** A user account, their profile, their comments, uploads, saves, reviews, check-ins, community posts, media contributions, and future Kinfolk context should all be linked through durable IDs so that a login problem does not equal data loss.

What we can guarantee architecturally is not “nothing can ever go wrong,” but this:

**Authentication failure must never delete user data. Account recovery must reconnect the person to the same user identity and historical data.**

That means we need Replit to prove that comments and media are tied to canonical `user_id` values rather than only email, username, device, or session. If you and your son both created profiles with photos and community comments, we need a read-only identity/content reconciliation before anyone resets anything.

I would make this a hard rule:

> **Recover identity; do not recreate history.**

If a user cannot log in, the recovery process should find the existing user row and reconnect credentials to that same user ID. We should not create a new blank account and then abandon the old one.

And yes, your second change also makes sense:

**Members should be able to contribute as much community media as they want, subject to moderation/storage/security rules. The distinction should be in access to analytics and business/creator tools, not whether ordinary members are allowed to participate.**

So I would separate:

**Community participation rights**
from
**professional analytics/monetization privileges.**

A regular member can post photos, videos, social links, reviews, comments, stories, business media, etc.

A **Business** or **Cultural Ambassador** gets the analytical layer behind that activity: reach, saves, impressions, audience patterns, click-throughs, engagement trends, referral impact, content performance, community response, etc.

That is cleaner than restricting ordinary users to one photo/video just because they are not a business.

The most important principle I would preserve is:

> **A user's login is not their identity. Their `user_id` is.**

And for media:

> **Participation should be broad. Analytics should be earned by role/account type.**
