# I agree with every P0

Especially these.

## P0-1

Medical guidance

Absolutely.

But I'd refine the implementation philosophy.

Instead of inserting a cold disclaimer every time, I recommend **graduated intervention**.

Example:

User:

> "What foods are high in iron?"

No disclaimer.

User:

> "Should I stop taking insulin?"

Different response.

KinfolkAI should recognize:

> This has crossed into individualized medical decision-making.

Now it should:

* answer generally;
* encourage consultation with a healthcare professional;
* offer to help locate culturally affirming care if appropriate.

That preserves warmth without pretending to be a clinician.

---

## P0-2

Legal guidance

Same principle.

General education?

Fine.

Personal legal strategy?

Different response.

Example:

> "What is an LLC?"

General explanation.

versus

> "Should I sign this contract?"

Now KinfolkAI should clearly distinguish between providing general information and personalized legal advice.

# My biggest addition

The audit discusses:

Knowledge Sources.

I think we're missing one constitutional rule.

## Source Hierarchy™

Every AI statement should internally classify itself.

Example:

Level A

Platform verified

Level B

Official public authority

Level C

Trusted editorial source

Level D

Community observations

Level E

AI reasoning

KinfolkAI should know which level produced the answer, even if members only see a simplified explanation. This aligns with your emphasis on transparency without overwhelming people with implementation details.

I think Audit 7 exposed another.

I would seal this now.

My recommendation:

# One more architectural recommendation

I think KinfolkAI deserves something we haven't named yet.

## The Wisdom Layer™

Knowledge answers:

> What is true?

Wisdom answers:

> What is the most responsible guidance for this person, in this situation, given what we know and what we don't know?

That's what separates a general AI assistant from the institutional guide you're creating.
