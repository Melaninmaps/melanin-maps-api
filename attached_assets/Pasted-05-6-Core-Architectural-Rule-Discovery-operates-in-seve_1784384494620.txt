05.6 Core Architectural Rule
Discovery operates in seven stages:
1. Query and Intent Resolution
2. Candidate Generation
3. Eligibility Filtering
4. Evidence and Confidence Adjustment
5. Relevance Scoring
6. Diversity and Exploration Re-ranking
7. BIF Handoff
The Engine may not skip eligibility filtering merely because a result has high relevance.
The Engine may not skip BIF review merely because a result has high popularity.
05.7 Candidate Generation
Candidate generation creates the preliminary set of potentially relevant entities.
Sources may include:
Mapping With Melanin™ verified records;
community-submitted entities;
claimed business profiles;
verified partner data;
official public data;
approved third-party data;
historical and cultural records;
user-created resources;
current events and opportunity feeds;
saved user entities;
route-adjacent data;
Community Engine™ signals;
Hidden Gem candidates;
Community Recognition candidates;
KinfolkAI™ structured retrieval.
Candidate generation must be broad enough to preserve exploration but bounded enough to prevent irrelevant, unsafe, expired, fraudulent, or prohibited entities from entering final ranking.
05.8 Candidate Eligibility
Every candidate receives one of six eligibility states.
State	Meaning
ELIGIBLE	May proceed to ranking
ELIGIBLE_LIMITED	May appear with restrictions
SEARCH_ONLY	May be found but not recommended
HOLD	Pending verification or human review
BLOCKED	Not permitted to appear
EXPIRED	No longer current or available
05.8.1 Baseline eligibility requirements
A candidate must have:
identifiable entity type;
sufficient basic information to distinguish it;
a relevant relationship to the query;
no active disqualifying safety or governance block;
no confirmed fraudulent identity;
no prohibited content status;
current or appropriately historical relevance;
geographic or online applicability;
permissible public display status;
data-source provenance.
05.8.2 Search-only conditions
A candidate may remain searchable but not recommendable when:
ownership is unverified;
evidence volume is low;
operating status is uncertain;
safety evidence is incomplete;
community evidence is emerging;
a business has not been claimed;
an opportunity has incomplete terms;
accessibility is unknown for an accessibility-required request;
the entity is newly added;
conflicting evidence remains unresolved;
a report is pending review;
the item is historically relevant but not operational.
Search-only status must not be presented as punishment.
05.8.3 Hold conditions
A candidate must be held when:
verification documents are under review;
severe negative evidence is uncorroborated but credible;
ownership claims conflict;
fraudulent activity is suspected;
legal or governance review is active;
the entity requests temporary removal;
a sensitive safety report may materially affect public visibility;
duplicate identity resolution is incomplete;
promotion eligibility is disputed;
the entity’s current operating status cannot be established for a time-sensitive result.
05.8.4 Block conditions
A candidate may be blocked when:
it violates platform rules;
it is fraudulent;
it poses a verified critical safety risk;
it contains illegal or prohibited material;
it is impersonating another entity;
it has been removed through governance decision;
it is a duplicate record selected for suppression;
it is a non-minority-owned entity attempting to falsely claim protected promotion eligibility;
it exists solely as demo or test data in production;
it has no lawful or valid display basis.
05.9 Demo and Synthetic Data Isolation
Demo, test, sandbox, synthetic, seeded, or staff-created sample entities must never enter production discovery unless explicitly marked and authorized for a controlled test environment.
The Engine must:
assign all synthetic records a non-production flag;
exclude them from public ranking;
exclude them from analytics;
exclude them from community scoring;
exclude them from promotion;
exclude them from search counts;
prevent them from generating notifications;
prevent them from influencing Hidden Gem status;
prevent them from contributing to city or community maturity;
preserve an audit trail of any temporary exception.
05.10 Query Understanding
The Engine receives structured context from the Context Engine™ and may also parse discovery-specific query elements.
A query may contain:
named entity;
category;
location;
distance;
time;
ownership requirement;
price range;
accessibility requirement;
safety condition;
cultural interest;
family suitability;
business purpose;
resource eligibility;
opportunity deadline;
event date;
transportation mode;
experience type;
popularity preference;
Hidden Gem preference;
open-now requirement;
verification requirement.
