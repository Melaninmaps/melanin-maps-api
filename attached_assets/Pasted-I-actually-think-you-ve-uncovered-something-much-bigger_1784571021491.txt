I actually think you've uncovered something much bigger than wording. You're refining the philosophy of Mapping With Melanin™, and I think it's the right direction.

The screenshots helped.

## 1. I would move away from saying "Black" everywhere

Not because it's wrong—your platform absolutely serves Black communities—but because your platform is becoming **community-first rather than category-first**.

For example, instead of:

> **Evidence-based health articles curated by verified Black physicians.**

I'd rather see something like:

> **Evidence-based health resources reviewed by trusted medical professionals, with culturally relevant perspectives when available.**

or

> **Trusted health information with culturally relevant insights and community resources.**

Now imagine I'm Hispanic.

Imagine I'm Indigenous.

Imagine I'm Filipino.

Imagine I'm Caribbean.

I don't immediately feel like the platform isn't for me.

Instead I think:

> "This platform understands culture."

That's a huge difference.

---

## 2. Let the user's preferences personalize the experience instead of the language

Rather than constantly saying

> Black businesses

> Hispanic businesses

> Asian businesses

> Indigenous businesses

I'd rather the app quietly understands my preferences.

Instead it can simply say

> Businesses matching your preferences

or

> Community Recommended Businesses

or

> Trusted Local Businesses

When appropriate, the detail can appear underneath.

For example:

> Matching your preferences:
>
> ✓ Minority-owned
>
> ✓ Caribbean-owned
>
> ✓ Women-owned

instead of putting ethnicity into every title.

That feels much more welcoming.

---

## 3. I LOVE your search idea.

I honestly think this becomes one of your competitive advantages.

Imagine I search:

> Hispanic plumber

Kinfolk says:

> I found three Hispanic-owned plumbing businesses within 15 miles.

If none exist:

> I couldn't find one within 15 miles.

Then...

> Would you like me to:
>
> ○ Expand to 30 miles
>
> ○ Expand to 50 miles
>
> ○ Search all minority-owned plumbers nearby
>
> ○ Show highly rated plumbers regardless of ownership

That is exactly how a concierge should behave.

Not:

> No results found.

---

Then imagine

> Black therapist

If none exist nearby...

Kinfolk responds

> I couldn't find a Black therapist within your search area.

> Would you like me to expand the search or include therapists from other minority communities?

That feels respectful.

The user stays in control.

---

## 4. I think this should become a platform-wide principle

Instead of thinking

> Search = results

Think

> Search = conversation

Kinfolk keeps helping until the person either

* finds what they wanted

or

* decides to stop.

That is much more human.

## What I'd tell Replit

I would ask them to adopt this as a **design principle**, not a one-off wording change:

> **Platform Language Principle**
>
> Mapping With Melanin™ should feel culturally welcoming without making members feel categorized by labels.
>
> Whenever possible:
>
> * Personalize results using the member's saved preferences rather than repeating demographic labels throughout the interface.
> * Preserve the official names of organizations (for example, "Therapy for Black Girls") but use broader, inclusive section headings (for example, "Mental Health Resources").
> * When a user searches for a culturally specific business and none are found, Kinfolk should offer progressively broader search options instead of simply returning "No results."
> * Search should behave like a concierge: search nearby first, then offer to expand the radius, then offer additional culturally aligned options, and finally offer highly rated businesses if the user chooses.
> * Always let the member decide whether to broaden beyond their original preference.

---

I also want to say something I think is important as your vision has evolved.

When we first started talking, the platform was primarily about helping people find Black-owned businesses. Over the months, you've consistently expanded it into something much broader: a platform that helps people **find community, belonging, and trusted resources while still honoring and elevating minority-owned businesses and culturally specific experiences**.

That distinction matters. It allows someone to feel welcome the moment they open the app, while still making it easy for users to intentionally discover and support the communities they're looking for. I think that's a stronger expression of the mission you've been building toward.
