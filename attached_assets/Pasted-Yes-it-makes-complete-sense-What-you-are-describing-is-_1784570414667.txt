Yes, it makes complete sense. What you are describing is not merely a saved-topic library. It is a **personalized knowledge and community discovery system**.

Right now, the screen makes the user do too much interpretation:

> “Save any interest”
> “Try: Atlanta, diabetes, vintage cars, Nigeria…”

That copy sounds like a list of random examples rather than explaining what the system actually does.

The experience should communicate one simple idea:

> **Search what matters to you. Mapping With Melanin™ will connect you to trustworthy information, community conversations, groups, videos, businesses, events, and resources that match—within your profile’s viewing permissions.**

## A better screen flow

### Page title

Instead of:

> **My Knowledge**
> Your personalized learning library

I would use:

> **My Knowledge**
> **Explore what matters to you.**

or:

> **My Knowledge**
> **Trusted information and community knowledge, personalized for you.**

### Main invitation

Replace the large “Save Any Interest” box with:

> **What are you curious about?**
> Search a topic, place, interest, question, or life goal. We’ll connect you with trustworthy information and relevant community experiences.

Search placeholder:

> **Search any topic…**

No contrived list is needed. The system can demonstrate its range through the results, not by telling people what to type.

## What should happen after a search

Kinfolk should interpret the topic and return a blended results page rather than one undifferentiated list.

For **diabetes**, the user might see:

### Trusted Information

* Articles from recognized public-health, medical, university, or nonprofit sources
* Clear source and publication date
* Medical-information disclaimer
* Links to professional care and emergency help when appropriate

### Community

* A relevant wellness group
* Community posts that mention living with diabetes
* Local support programs
* Culturally relevant nutrition resources

### Videos and Experiences

* Age-appropriate community videos
* Cooking demonstrations
* Interviews with qualified professionals
* Events or workshops

Kinfolk could say:

> “I found verified health information, community discussions, and nearby programs related to diabetes. Medical information is educational and should not replace care from a qualified professional.”

For **anime**, it might return:

* Anime-interest groups
* Community videos and posts
* Local conventions and events
* Minority-owned comic or hobby shops
* Articles or explainers
* Age-appropriate recommendations based on the member’s profile

For **cars**, your son might see:

* Car-restoration groups
* Age-appropriate tutorials
* Auto shows
* Community posts
* Mechanics and minority-owned auto businesses
* Educational articles

He should not see mature music videos, sexually explicit car-show footage, violent content, illegal street-racing promotion, drug references, or other material outside his permitted viewing level merely because the word “cars” appears in it.

## The safety system must filter the result—not just the search word

This is essential.

A child searching “cars” has entered an innocent topic. The danger can be contained inside the video, caption, comments, linked event, audio, or creator profile.

Every result should therefore be evaluated against your existing viewer-rating framework using:

* member age;
* parent-selected permissions;
* result rating;
* video and audio classification;
* caption and description;
* content warnings;
* linked destination;
* group age requirement;
* event age restriction;
* business audience classification.

The child profile should only receive material authorized for that profile. Mature results should be excluded entirely—not shown blurred with a tempting “unlock” button.

## Suggested result labels

Each item should identify what it is and where it came from:

* **Verified Information**
* **Public Resource**
* **Mapping With Melanin™ Community**
* **Community Video**
* **Group**
* **Event**
* **Business or Organization**
* **Kinfolk Summary**
* **Your Saved Interests**

For health, legal, financial, safety, or similarly consequential topics, users should also see:

* the source;
* publication or review date;
* whether the information is general education;
* whether professional guidance is appropriate;
* whether a claim is community-reported rather than independently verified.

## Kinfolk’s role

Kinfolk should act as the conductor.

It should not pretend that every answer comes from its own knowledge. It should understand the search, identify the appropriate types of results, apply the member’s permissions, and explain what was found.

A strong Kinfolk introduction might be:

> **Here’s what I found across trusted sources and your community. I separated verified information from community experiences so you can decide what is most useful.**

That supports your Circle of Trust principle without making the interface feel technical.

## Recommended tabs

The current tabs could be simplified to:

* **For You**
* **Following**
* **Community**
* **Resources**

“Community Hubs” may remain as a feature, but it should not carry the full burden of discovery. “Happening Now” sounds temporary and event-focused, while the system is much broader.

A search result can then be filtered by:

* All
* Information
* Community
* Groups
* Videos
* Events
* Businesses
* Resources
