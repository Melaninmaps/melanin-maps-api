KINFOLK’S ROLE IN THE ECOSYSTEM — NON-NEGOTIABLE

Kinfolk is not a standalone chatbot and is not merely one feature inside
Mapping With Melanin™.

Kinfolk is intended to become the permission-aware intelligence layer
that helps the ecosystem understand, organize, connect, and responsibly
surface community knowledge.

Kinfolk has several connected roles:

1. PERSONAL GUIDE

Kinfolk learns from the individual member’s stated preferences,
permissions, history, saved interests, location choices, travel plans,
safety needs, and membership context.

It helps the member decide:

- where to go
- what to support
- what to explore
- what events or resources may be relevant
- how to prepare for a trip, relocation, meetup, or local experience
- what community context should be considered before acting

It must explain why a suggestion is relevant and allow the member to
control, correct, or disable personalization.

2. COMMUNITY LIBRARY AND RESOURCE GUIDE

Kinfolk is the primary conversational interface to the platform’s
community library and resource ecosystem.

The library may include:

- member-contributed knowledge
- verified articles and resources
- cultural sites and history
- scholarships
- grants and business resources
- food, housing, employment, and community assistance
- travel and relocation information
- safety context
- community discussions
- events
- creator, Ambassador, and business contributions

Kinfolk must distinguish among:

- verified facts
- official resources
- business-provided information
- Ambassador-curated information
- member experiences
- community consensus
- disputed claims
- unverified submissions
- paid or sponsored information

It must never flatten all of these into one undifferentiated answer.

3. COMMUNITY INTELLIGENCE INTERPRETER

Community feedback does not merely create ratings.

Kinfolk should help interpret patterns across:

- member reviews
- safety reports
- nominations
- business responses
- repeated recommendations
- cultural context
- time and location
- member type and stated experience
- verification status
- Ambassador activity

The system should identify meaningful patterns without exposing private
members, overgeneralizing from limited feedback, or treating one report
as proven fact.

Replit must explain how raw feedback becomes safe, explainable,
permission-aware community intelligence.

Do not disclose proprietary formulas or weights. Provide the conceptual
decision flow, safeguards, data classifications, confidence rules, and
human-review requirements.

4. BUSINESS TRUST AND GROWTH LOOP

Kinfolk should connect community feedback to the business experience.

Replit must explain how the proposed design handles:

- member nominations
- business claims
- verification
- reviews and lived experiences
- safety signals
- owner responses
- “make it right” opportunities
- changing trust over time
- positive community recognition
- Hidden Gem status
- sponsored or promoted placement
- removal, suspension, or appeal
- protection against coordinated manipulation

The audit must determine whether businesses are represented as static
listings or as evolving community relationships.

Kinfolk must never secretly alter ratings or present paid promotion as
organic trust.

5. COMMUNITY AMBASSADOR LOOP

Community Ambassadors are trusted contributors, connectors, curators, and
community stewards. They are not simply influencers.

Replit must define:

- what Ambassadors may contribute
- what they may verify or recommend
- what additional weight, if any, their contributions receive
- how conflicts of interest are disclosed
- how sponsored relationships are handled
- how Ambassador activity is reviewed
- how status is earned, maintained, suspended, and appealed
- how abuse or favoritism is prevented
- how Kinfolk uses Ambassador input without allowing one person to define
  community truth

Kinfolk should help members understand the source and context of
Ambassador guidance.

6. JOURNEYS

Journeys are guided, contextual experiences through the ecosystem. They
should not be reduced to itineraries or static lists.

A Journey may connect:

- member goals
- location
- businesses
- cultural sites
- events
- community stories
- safety information
- resources
- Ambassadors
- Circles
- reflection, saving, sharing, or contribution

Examples may include:

- discovering community in a member’s own city
- preparing for relocation
- exploring Black history in a destination
- supporting businesses along a route
- navigating an event weekend
- finding culturally relevant family activities
- building a safety-aware travel plan
- following an Ambassador-curated experience

Kinfolk should help create, adapt, explain, and guide Journeys while
respecting membership permissions and data boundaries.

Replit must clarify:

- who can create a Journey
- whether Journeys are private, shared, curated, or public to members
- how member feedback improves a Journey
- how outdated or unsafe stops are handled
- how paid placements are disclosed
- how recommendations change when safety or trust signals change
- how Journeys work during outages or incomplete data
- how members report errors

7. ECOSYSTEM FEEDBACK LOOP

The future-state design must show how information moves through the
ecosystem:

Member contribution
→ classification and moderation
→ verification or confidence assessment
→ business, resource, event, site, or Journey context
→ permission-aware retrieval
→ Kinfolk guidance
→ member action or experience
→ new feedback
→ correction, trust update, or community learning

This loop must include:

- consent
- attribution
- privacy controls
- moderation
- dispute handling
- correction
- confidence levels
- expiration or freshness
- audit history
- rollback
- prevention of feedback manipulation

8. KINFOLK MUST NOT BECOME A SINGLE POINT OF FAILURE

Although Kinfolk powers the ecosystem, core platform functions must still
work when Kinfolk or the AI provider is unavailable.

Members must still be able to:

- access saved items
- browse permitted community information
- view businesses and events
- access safety information
- use Circles
- review resources
- report concerns
- manage their account

Replit must propose graceful degradation, cached safe responses,
provider failover where justified, cost controls, monitoring, and feature
flags.

9. REQUIRED ARCHITECTURE OUTPUT

Replit must provide a high-level ecosystem diagram showing Kinfolk at the
center and its permission-controlled connections to:

- member profile and preferences
- community feed
- library/resources
- businesses
- reviews and trust signals
- safety systems
- Ambassadors
- Journeys
- events
- Circles
- cultural sites
- relocation and travel tools

The diagram must also show which systems remain independently usable when
