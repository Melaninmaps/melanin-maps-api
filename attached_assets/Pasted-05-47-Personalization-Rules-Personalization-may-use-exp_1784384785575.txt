05.47 Personalization Rules
Personalization may use:
explicit preferences;
saved entities;
current context;
prior user actions;
followed topics;
authorized accessibility preferences;
travel style;
budget;
categories;
city interests.
Personalization may not use:
inferred protected identity;
private messages unrelated to discovery;
sensitive reports for advertising;
emergency behavior for marketing;
undisclosed third-party profiling;
precise location without permission;
family or minor data outside authorized use.
Personalization must be reversible.
05.48 Negative Preference Rules
Users may exclude:
categories;
price levels;
distance ranges;
event types;
unavailable times;
accessibility-incompatible options;
previously dismissed entities;
unsafe or unwanted areas;
nonverified ownership when verification is required.
The Engine must not infer discriminatory exclusions or facilitate unlawful discriminatory filtering.
05.49 Anti-Gaming Signals
Potential discovery manipulation includes:
keyword stuffing;
false categories;
false location claims;
fake operating hours;
review rings;
mass saves;
artificial click activity;
paid traffic intended to influence organic rank;
duplicate listings;
false ownership claims;
repeated profile edits to trigger freshness;
fabricated community impact;
hidden sponsorship;
fake event availability;
expired opportunities kept active;
coordinated search manipulation;
location spoofing;
impersonation;
false accessibility claims;
misleading resource capacity.
05.50 Anti-Gaming Responses
Level	Response
1	Signal monitoring
2	Evidence discount
3	Ranking adjustment
4	Temporary visibility limit
5	Verification request
6	Promotion hold
7	Search hold
8	Human review
9	Governance escalation
10	Removal or block
No punitive action should rely on a single weak signal.
05.51 Fairness Protections
The Engine must test for whether ranking systematically disadvantages:
new businesses;
small businesses;
low-review entities;
rural communities;
under-documented neighborhoods;
non-English-speaking communities;
emerging Cultural Ambassadors;
historically overlooked institutions;
community resources without marketing budgets;
entities outside high-traffic areas;
communities with limited platform adoption.
Fairness does not require equal ranking regardless of relevance.
It requires that structural visibility advantages do not silently become quality assumptions.
05.52 Geographic Equity
The Engine must prevent all discovery from concentrating in already-visible cities or neighborhoods.
Approved mechanisms may include:
city-normalized visibility;
neighborhood rotation;
regional exploration;
scarcity-aware ranking;
local relevance weighting;
underrepresentation monitoring;
Hidden Gem pathways;
community-submitted discovery.
Geographic equity may not invent quality or ignore safety.
05.53 Emerging Entity Protection
Emerging entities may receive:
search visibility;
emerging labels;
limited exploration placement;
evidence collection prompts;
community verification opportunities;
Hidden Gem review;
temporary freshness consideration.
They may not receive:
fabricated confidence;
automatic promotion;
verified labels without evidence;
immunity from safety or trust review.
05.54 Presentation Rules
The user-facing result may display:
entity name;
category;
location;
distance;
open status;
verification status;
ownership status;
relevant community signals;
accessibility information;
safety information where approved;
reason surfaced;
confidence qualifier;
sponsored label;
emerging label;
Hidden Gem status if formally awarded.
The Engine must not expose confidential numeric scores or weights.
05.55 Public Explanation Rules
Approved examples:
“Shown because it closely matches your search and location.”
“Recommended for your relocation search based on community, resource, and trust information.”
“This is a newer listing with promising community feedback.”
“Surfaced as an overlooked local option with strong repeat support.”
“This result matches your request for verified melanated diaspora-owned businesses.”
“Availability could not be fully confirmed.”
“Sponsored result — eligibility requirements were still applied.”
“Shown for exploration because it offers a different nearby option.”
Prohibited examples:
“This business paid to rank first.”
“The algorithm suppressed competitors.”
“This neighborhood scored poorly because it lacks enough Black users.”
“You are likely to prefer this because of your race.”
“The Hidden Gem formula gave this business 86.”
“The platform secretly reduced this entity’s visibility.”
“This result is objectively the best.”
