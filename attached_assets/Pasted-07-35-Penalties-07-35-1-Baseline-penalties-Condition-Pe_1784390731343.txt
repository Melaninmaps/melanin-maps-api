07.35 Penalties
07.35.1 Baseline penalties
Condition	Penalty
Undisclosed paid relationship	−10 to −30
False cultural authority	−15 to −40
Cultural misrepresentation	−10 to −35
Creator or community work used without attribution	−15 to −40
Fabricated impact outcome	−20 to −50
Purchased engagement	−10 to −30
Community extraction without return	−15 to −40
Retaliation against criticism	−20 to −45
False partnership claim	−15 to −35
Repeated refusal to correct material error	−10 to −30
Severe concerns may trigger Trust or Governance action rather than only a score penalty.
07.36 Corrective Action
An entity may improve current Cultural Influence treatment through verified corrective action.
Examples:
adding appropriate attribution;
correcting historical inaccuracies;
compensating contributors;
disclosing sponsorship;
removing fabricated engagement;
apologizing and repairing harm;
changing partnership practice;
obtaining consent;
creating community benefit;
establishing transparent review;
restoring removed credit.
Corrective action must not erase the original record.
07.37 Conflict Resolution Rules
Rule 1 — Cultural grounding over superficial reach
Strong cultural grounding may outweigh limited audience size.
Rule 2 — Integrity over visibility
High visibility may not compensate for deception.
Rule 3 — Community outcome over engagement volume
Verified benefit outweighs large numbers of passive reactions.
Rule 4 — Reciprocity over extraction
Influence that repeatedly extracts without returning value must be limited.
Rule 5 — Lived connection over marketing claims
Verified community relationship ordinarily outweighs self-description.
Rule 6 — Accurate correction may reduce current concern
A meaningful correction may improve treatment without deleting history.
Rule 7 — Different communities may evaluate influence differently
The Engine may preserve materially different experiences.
Rule 8 — Cultural disagreement does not automatically equal harm
Good-faith disagreement must not be treated as manipulation or misconduct.
Rule 9 — Commercial success does not establish cultural authenticity
Revenue and fame remain separate from cultural grounding.
Rule 10 — Safety and Governance controls remain superior
Cultural influence may never override a valid safety or governance restriction.
07.38 Cultural Diversity and Non-Monolithic Treatment
The Engine must not treat:
Black culture;
African cultures;
Caribbean cultures;
Afro-Latin cultures;
Indigenous cultures;
Asian diaspora cultures;
Middle Eastern or North African cultures;
mixed or multiracial communities;
regional communities;
generational groups;
as internally uniform.
Cultural influence must be evaluated at the most relevant level of specificity supported by evidence.
07.39 Anti-Gaming Signals
Potential manipulation includes:
purchased followers;
purchased views;
engagement pods;
coordinated comments;
copied testimonials;
fabricated impact claims;
hidden sponsorship;
false community affiliation;
fake business referrals;
false event attendance;
duplicate nominee campaigns;
artificial link traffic;
self-created “independent” reviews;
undisclosed employee promotion;
manipulated attribution;
false historical claims;
impersonation;
repeated content recycling presented as new impact;
audience-location spoofing;
coordinated suppression of criticism.
07.40 Anti-Gaming Responses
Level	Response
1	Monitor
2	Discount suspect signal
3	Reduce confidence
4	Freeze score change
5	Require disclosure or verification
6	Hold partnership
7	Hold promotion or recognition
8	Human investigation
9	Governance escalation
10	Removal or disqualification
A single unusual engagement pattern must not automatically establish fraud.
