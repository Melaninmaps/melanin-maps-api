05.30 Diversity and Exploration Re-ranking
The Engine must prevent result sets from becoming unnecessarily repetitive.
Diversity may be applied across:
ownership status;
business category;
neighborhood;
price range;
experience type;
institution size;
established versus emerging;
local versus regional;
indoor versus outdoor;
event format;
resource type;
Cultural Ambassador perspective;
route location.
Diversity must be relevant to the query.
It must not be imposed in a way that weakens explicit requirements.
05.30.1 Re-ranking rule
Displayed Rank Score =
  Base Discovery Score
+ Exploration Adjustment
+ Diversity Adjustment
+ User-Specific Relevance Adjustment
- Saturation Penalty
- Redundancy Penalty
All adjustments must remain within approved limits.
05.30.2 Maximum re-ranking adjustments
Adjustment	Maximum
Exploration boost	+8
Hidden Gem boost	+10
Diversity boost	+6
User-saved relevance	+5
Route convenience	+5
Redundancy penalty	−8
Saturation penalty	−10
Low-confidence penalty	−15
No adjustment may override a hard filter or safety block.
05.31 Popularity Limitation Rule
Popularity signals may include:
views;
saves;
visits;
reviews;
shares;
followers;
clicks;
bookings;
transactions;
event attendance.
Combined popularity signals may contribute no more than 12% of the Discovery Engine’s internal relevance calculation.
Popularity must not:
establish trust;
establish safety;
establish ownership;
establish cultural authenticity;
establish community impact;
erase negative evidence;
permanently dominate visibility.
05.32 Saturation Control
Saturation control prevents already-visible entities from capturing disproportionate exposure.
Potential saturation indicators:
repeated top placement;
high share of category impressions;
high paid and organic overlap;
frequent repeat exposure to the same user;
dominance across nearby locations;
excessive category concentration;
high visibility unsupported by current relevance.
The Engine may apply a temporary saturation penalty when adequate alternatives exist.
Saturation control must not suppress exact-match searches.
05.33 Result Rotation
Eligible candidates with similar scores may rotate to preserve opportunity and reduce deterministic concentration.
Rotation is permitted when:
scores are within an approved tolerance;
all candidates meet relevant thresholds;
no safety-sensitive context requires strict ordering;
the user has not requested a specific ranking basis;
rotation does not misrepresent quality.
Default rotation tolerance:
Candidates within 5 Discovery Score points
and within the same eligibility tier
may rotate.
Rotation decisions must be logged.
05.34 Hidden Gem Candidate Detection
The Discovery Engine™ may identify a Hidden Gem candidate but does not award final Hidden Gem status.
A candidate may enter Hidden Gem review when it shows:
strong community evidence;
strong trust evidence;
high context relevance;
limited existing visibility;
sustained positive lived experience;
low marketing reach relative to quality;
no active safety or governance concern;
current operating status;
meaningful local or cultural value;
sufficient evidence independence.
05.34.1 Hidden Gem Potential Score
Hidden Gem Potential =
  Quality Strength
+ Community Strength
+ Trust Strength
+ Cultural or Local Distinctiveness
+ Underexposure
+ Continuity
- Popularity Saturation
- Risk Penalties
Normalized to 0–100.
05.34.2 Baseline Hidden Gem weights
Component	Weight
Quality Strength	20%
Community Strength	20%
Trust Strength	15%
Cultural or Local Distinctiveness	15%
Underexposure	15%
Continuity	10%
Opportunity Relevance	5%
Risk penalties and disqualifiers apply after weighting.
