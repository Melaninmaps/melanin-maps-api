This is useful, but the cause is **still not proven**. The controlled tests show that two and four simultaneous requests complete normally, so an ordinary one-time startup burst does not exhaust the pool.

The strongest new clue is that connections were checked out between Replit’s tests. However, that does not yet prove `useActivityAlerts` is responsible. It could also be another user, web traffic, an admin request, a background job, or a request that happened at the exact moment pool stats were sampled.

## What this report establishes

* Individual routes release their connections.
* Two simultaneous requests pass.
* Four simultaneous requests pass.
* The five-connection pool can handle the tested concurrency.
* The recurring failure is likely associated with **repeated or sustained traffic**, not one normal screen load.
* `maxLifetimeSeconds: 1800` matching 30 minutes is probably coincidental because idle connections are closed after 30 seconds.
* `useActivityAlerts` polling every 30 seconds deserves investigation, but has not been proven as the cause.

One poll every 30 seconds that completes quickly would not exhaust a five-connection pool. The concern becomes credible only if:

* the alert query is unusually slow in production,
* many app instances are polling simultaneously,
* duplicate intervals are being created,
* polling continues after leaving the map,
* or failed requests retry and overlap.

## A likely design improvement—but not yet approved

Even if the alert poll is not the full cause, polling every 30 seconds may be unnecessarily aggressive for a travel/community app. A safer eventual design may include:

* polling only while the Map screen is focused,
* stopping while the app is backgrounded,
* increasing the interval,
* preventing overlapping requests,
* aborting the previous request before starting another,
* and using one shared request rather than one interval per mounted component.
