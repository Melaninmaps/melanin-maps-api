I think this is one of the most important product questions we've discussed, because it affects the integrity of your entire marketplace.

My answer is **yes**—I think **every business** should eventually have these attributes, whether or not they've claimed their profile. But I would handle the source of that information differently depending on the business's status.

## Three profile states

Instead of thinking of "joined" versus "not joined," I'd define three states.

### 1. Community Profile (Unclaimed)

A community member adds a business.

The community can safely provide objective information such as:

* Business name
* Address
* Phone
* Website (if known)
* Category
* Location on the map

I would also allow:

* Estimated audience:

  * All Ages
  * Family Friendly
  * 18+
  * 21+

**But only if it's obvious.**

For example:

* A brewery → likely 21+
* A playground → All Ages
* A children's museum → Family Friendly

If someone is unsure, they should be able to select:

> **Unknown**

The community should **not** be filling in detailed operational information that only the business would know.

---

### 2. Verified Business (Claimed)

Once the owner claims the profile, they become the authoritative source for things like:

* Audience
* Age restrictions
* Accessibility
* Amenities
* Environment
* Hours
* Services
* Policies

This is where they complete the Trust Profile.

---

### 3. Verified by Community

There will be cases where a business never joins but the community consistently confirms the same facts over time.

For example:

* Family Friendly
* Outdoor Seating
* Wheelchair Accessible

Rather than immediately trusting a single submission, I'd use a confidence model:

* Community Verified (many consistent reports)
* Business Verified (owner confirmed)

Those are different, and users should be able to see which one they're looking at.

---

## I would not ask safety reporters

If someone is reporting:

* discrimination,
* harassment,
* an unsafe incident,
* another safety concern,

they're already doing something important.

Don't ask them to complete audience classifications or business metadata at that point.

Keep the safety flow focused.

---

## When should audience information be collected?

I would collect it during:

* business submissions,
* business edits,
* community business additions (limited fields),
* owner profile completion.

Not during reviews or safety reports.

---

# Your bigger question

> **Do we show and promote businesses that have not joined?**

This is where I think Mapping With Melanin™ becomes different.

My recommendation is:

## YES — show them.

Otherwise users won't discover many great places.

But...

## NO — don't promote them the same way.

I would create three visibility levels.

### Level 1 — Community Listed

Added by the community.

Visible in search.

Visible on the map.

No promotional placement.

No featured status.

No analytics.

No marketing emails beyond an invitation to claim.

---

### Level 2 — Claimed Business

Owner joins.

Now they can:

* edit information,
* respond to reviews,
* add photos,
* create events,
* access analytics,
* update hours.

Still not automatically promoted.

---

### Level 3 — Participating Business

Businesses that actively participate in the ecosystem.

For example:

* keep information current,
* engage with the community,
* meet verification requirements,
* follow community standards,
* perhaps subscribe to an eligible business tier.

These become eligible for:

* featured placement,
* recommendation boosts,
* campaign participation,
* launch promotions.

Notice the distinction:

You're not charging businesses to exist.

You're rewarding participation.

That's a much healthier model.

---

## One additional safeguard

Whenever a profile is community-created, I would display a small status such as:

> **Community Listed — This business has not yet claimed its profile. Some information may be community-provided.**

Once claimed:

> **Business Verified — Information confirmed by the owner.**

That gives users context without diminishing either type of listing.

---

## I think this belongs in your Trust Profile

Eventually every business profile should have sections like:

* **Profile Status**

  * Community Listed
  * Business Claimed
  * Business Verified

* **Audience**

  * All Ages
  * Family Friendly
  * Teens Welcome
  * Adults 18+
  * Adults 21+
  * Unknown

* **Verification Source**

  * Owner Confirmed
  * Community Confirmed
  * Pending Verification

That aligns with something you've consistently emphasized throughout Mapping With Melanin™: **trust should be earned, explained, and transparent.** Users can still discover businesses that haven't joined, but they'll also understand how the information was obtained and how much confidence to place in it. I think that approach balances inclusivity, accuracy, and fairness very well.
