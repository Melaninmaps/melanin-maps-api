You’re right. The pins are technically present, but the map still does not communicate clearly.

The purple head, star, heart, house, globe, and orange location symbols are not self-explanatory. A user should never have to guess what a marker means. More importantly, tapping **HBCU** should help you find Howard immediately—not just change a color chip while leaving you to scan the entire region.

## What the map should do

When you tap **HBCU**:

1. Hide all non-HBCU cultural-site pins.
2. Keep only HBCU markers visible.
3. Automatically zoom and center to the HBCUs in the current area.
4. Show a small result panel such as:

   * **HBCUs nearby — 3**
   * Howard University
   * University of the District of Columbia
   * Bowie State University
5. Let you tap **Howard University** from either:

   * its map marker, or
   * the results list.
6. Open a callout or detail card with:

   * Howard University
   * HBCU
   * Washington, DC
   * short historical description
   * address
   * directions
   * website
   * save/share
   * **View Details**

## The marker system needs clearer meaning

I would simplify it.

### Cultural-site markers

Use the same landmark/book-style marker shape, but vary the color by cultural category:

* Purple — HBCU
* Red — Civil Rights
* Gold — Heritage
* Green — Native
* Orange — Latino
* Burgundy — LGBTQ+ history

Do not use a purple person silhouette for an HBCU. That icon looks like a user profile or people marker, not a university.

### Business markers

Use one consistent business-pin shape, with ownership or category shown after tapping it—not many unexplained icon shapes scattered across the map.

### Selected marker

When a marker is selected:

* enlarge it,
* add a white outline,
* show its name above or in a bottom card,
* keep the selected location visible while the map recenters.

## My design recommendation

The category chips should behave like **search tools**, not just colored labels.

For example:

* Tap **HBCU** → only HBCUs appear, map recenters, list opens.
* Tap **Civil Rights** → only civil-rights landmarks appear.
* Tap **Heritage Sites** again → all cultural categories return.
* Tap a location → a named card opens.

That creates context and purpose. The current map shows that the data exists; the next step is making it understandable and navigable.
