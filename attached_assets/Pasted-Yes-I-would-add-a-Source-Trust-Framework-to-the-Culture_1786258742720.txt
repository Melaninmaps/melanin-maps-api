Yes — I would add a **Source & Trust Framework** to the Culture & Roots / Library specification so Replit knows not only *what* to surface, but **where the information is allowed to come from and how trustworthy it must be**.

This is important because Kinfolk and the Library should not treat a tourism board, a medical authority, a local creator, a historical archive, and a breaking-news outlet as if they are interchangeable.

For Africa specifically, the African Union is appropriate for the 55-member-state framework, WHO and Africa CDC for health/public-health information, UNESCO for culture and heritage, and UN Tourism for tourism-sector information. ([African Union][1]) Afrobarometer is also a useful source for public opinion and social/political attitudes because it conducts cross-country survey research across African countries. ([Afrobarometer][2])

A few **source anchors I would explicitly bake into the registry**:

* **Africa geography/governance:** African Union; it maintains the 55-member-state framework. ([African Union][1])
* **Health:** WHO/WHO Africa plus Africa CDC; WHO Africa publishes country and health-topic resources, while Africa CDC maintains continental public-health resources. ([WHO | Regional Office for Africa][3])
* **Culture/history/heritage:** UNESCO and the UNESCO World Heritage Centre. ([UNESCO][4])
* **Tourism:** UN Tourism plus each country's official tourism authority. UN Tourism maintains an Africa program and tourism-sector information. ([UNWTO][5])
* **Social/public opinion:** Afrobarometer is especially valuable because it measures public attitudes across African countries rather than relying on outside assumptions. ([Afrobarometer][2])
* **Economic/development data:** World Bank, UNDP, African Development Bank and national statistical agencies. World Bank provides country/regional development datasets, while UNDP publishes Africa-focused development and governance work. ([World Bank Open Data][6])
* **Languages:** UNESCO is useful because Africa is highly multilingual; its materials emphasize the continent's exceptional linguistic diversity, which is precisely why we should not assign a single cultural/language identity to a country. ([UNESCO][7])

**My recommendation:** the Library should eventually give every country a **living source pack**, rather than one giant global list of approved websites. Ghanaian news, Nigerian entertainment, Haitian history, Jamaican tourism, Ethiopian health resources, Cuban culture, and Puerto Rican community information deserve sources that are actually rooted in those places and communities.

That is how this can become substantially more than an article library: **Culture & Roots becomes a trusted doorway into a place and its people — history, what's happening now, health, culture, politics, entertainment, travel, community and the diaspora — while Kinfolk always knows what kind of source it is relying on.**
