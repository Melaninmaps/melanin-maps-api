# Houston Kids Enrichment Research Report

**Category:** Kids enrichment and family learning in Houston and clearly Houston-metro locations
**Prepared:** 2026-09-19
**Author:** Manus AI

## Result

This research pass produced **22 candidate records** and **8 held leads** for ages 6–12-oriented after-school programs, camps, STEM, arts, library offerings, mentoring, recreation, and family learning. The strongest everyday-discovery options are municipal programs, public library services, museums and cultural venues, university programs, and nonprofit youth resources. Candidate records retain only literal, source-supported identity or organizational designations; all other unverified attributes are `null`.

The candidate set includes a municipal library and park-program pathway, arts and cultural learning, nature and space science, youth golf and club-based enrichment, and two customer-facing STEM businesses. Several programs span a wider range than ages 6–12, but each candidate has an official source explicitly covering at least part of that range or is a useful public cultural/family-learning venue. Examples include HPARD’s ages 6–13 after-school program, Space Center Houston’s ages 6–11 Explorer Camps, and UH’s ages 6–12 arts camp. [2] [6] [10]

## Files and scope

The candidate file is `candidates-kids-enrichment.jsonl`; the held file is `held-kids-enrichment.jsonl`. Both use the requested 23-field order and literal JSON `null` values for unknown data. The work covers Houston and one clearly Houston-metro Bellaire record. It does not make claims about availability, price, enrollment, accessibility, safety, licensure, quality, or current schedules beyond what the cited sources directly state.

This is **research-only**. No production database or API write, publication, map pin, geocode, deployment, native build, auth/user/session/waitlist/payment change was performed.

## Candidate-selection and deduplication checks

Candidates were retained only when an opened official source supplied a current customer-facing destination or a source-supported physical program location with a numbered address. Candidate names were normalized before review by stripping punctuation and program-location suffixes for comparison. The 22 retained records have no duplicated normalized name, street address, telephone number, or same-provider/same-program combination within this category. Closely related HPARD records were retained only where the source identified distinct programs and distinct physical locations.

The eight held records are intentionally not candidates. They lack a specific active program address, have a failed or malformed official destination check, or do not state an age range or program relevant to ages 6–12. Search snippets were not used as substantiation for held or candidate facts.

## Limitations

Some official destinations are organization offices while the verified program is delivered on school or community-partner sites. Young Audiences of Houston is retained as a service directory resource because its official page says after-school programming is arranged with schools and youth centers, but the record notes that delivery model. Camp Adventure and Parent Stars are held because their official pages identify only partner or school campuses rather than an individually listable program address. [20] [22] [23]

The results are a point-in-time discovery set. Camp sessions, waitlists, seasonal registration, venue access, and eligibility can change. Families or testers should use the listed official destination to verify a particular date, age band, cost, space, and location before acting.
