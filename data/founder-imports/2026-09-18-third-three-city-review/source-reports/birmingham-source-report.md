# Birmingham, Alabama Directory Research Wave — Source Report

## Result

This single bounded pass produced **33 retained candidates** and **10 held records** for Birmingham and nearby suburbs. Retained records meet the requested minimum contract: a public source URL, a specific name, locality, evidence-supported category, a numbered street address for physical candidates, and a customer-facing official website or social destination. Two retained records are explicitly online-only and therefore set `address` to `null`. Exact name + city + state + address checks against the existing research JSONL files found no duplicate retained record.

Identity descriptions were only preserved when a source explicitly supplied them. Editorial Black-owned and Hispanic-owned features, a Black Restaurant Week listing, ByBlack’s Black-owned directory context, first-party social pages, and first-party heritage or community descriptions were treated as distinct evidence types. **A chamber or directory listing alone was not used as proof of ownership.** For the ByBlack trade records, the directory context is stated but certification is not inferred. Regulated health/wellness, personal-care, engineering/planning, and contracting/electrical candidates were routed to `regulated_review`.

## Limitations

Source records span different publication dates and pages may change. This pass does not verify current hours, inventory, menus, pricing, availability, quality, ownership beyond the cited designation, language ability, licensing, credentials, accessibility, insurance, services beyond the stated category, or regulatory compliance. Customer-facing pages were used only to establish destinations and, when published there, address/category context. Items with an unavailable or closed official site, unresolved address conflict, a post-office box rather than a street address, or no defensible office address were held rather than promoted.

The City of Birmingham Office of Business Diversity and Opportunity and related chamber/directory sites were inspected as context and discovery sources. They were not treated as blanket evidence that a listed business is minority-owned. The official City page confirms that it supports minority and women entrepreneurs, but it did not yield a public candidate-level certification directory in the inspected content. [9]
