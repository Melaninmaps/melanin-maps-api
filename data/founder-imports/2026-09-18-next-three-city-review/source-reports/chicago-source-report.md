# Chicago, IL Directory Research Wave — Source Report

## Scope and Method

This single bounded pass covered **Chicago, Illinois and nearby suburbs**. Research prioritized public Black/African American, Hispanic/Latino, and diaspora-focused directories, then corroborated address, category, or customer-facing destination details on first-party websites or official social accounts where publicly accessible. Exact duplicates were prevented using the existing research data only, with the normalized key **name + city + state + address**. No latitude/longitude, hours, ownership beyond the cited designation, language ability, licensing, accessibility, availability, service quality, or other unsupported attributes were added.

## Factual Limitations

This is a time-bounded discovery wave, not an exhaustive directory or business verification. Directory statements and linked official destinations were treated as evidence only to the extent stated. The directory identity labels are preserved in `ownershipDesignations` and described in `ownershipEvidence`; they are not independently adjudicated legal ownership determinations. A chamber or directory listing alone was not treated as proof of ownership unless the source itself explicitly made the ownership designation, and no chamber membership was used as ownership proof.

Some public pages supplied a street number but not a postal code; those records retain the source-supported numbered street address and city/state/country without fabricating a postal code. Soul Veg City is a documented address reconciliation: the Black directory showed 201 E 75th Street, while its official website showed 203 E 75th Street; the first-party address was retained and the conflict is recorded in notes. Two physical/mobility candidates and two other candidates with required information gaps are in `held-candidates.jsonl`, rather than promoted. No online-only candidate was promoted because this pass did not establish a candidate as online-only to the required standard.
