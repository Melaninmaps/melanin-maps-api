# Los Angeles County Dining & Drinks Directory Research

**Category researched:** Dining & Drinks — upscale restaurants, chef-led dining, wine experiences, cocktail bars, special-occasion dining, and LGBTQ+ nightlife.
**Geographic scope:** Los Angeles County, California.
**Research date:** September 19, 2026.
**Author:** Manus AI

## Result

This research produced **12 candidate records** and **6 held leads**. All candidate records are physical businesses with a source-supported numbered address and an opened official customer-facing destination. Los Angeles, Inglewood, and West Hollywood were retained because the County of Los Angeles city directory lists each as an LA County city.[1]

The candidate set is intentionally narrow in one respect: it records a Black, Latino, diaspora, or LGBTQ+ designation only where an opened source expressly attributes it. It does not infer ownership, community affiliation, quality, luxury level, audience, safety, eligibility, or personal characteristics from a name, cuisine, neighborhood, venue type, or customer profile.

## What the records support

The records describe concrete, source-backed offerings rather than a subjective ranking. The official 1010 Wine site identifies it as an upscale Black- and women-owned wine bar and restaurant, with dinner, a wine list featuring Black-owned wines, events, and weekend music programming.[2] Alta Adams’ official site names Chef Keith Corbin, describes dinner and a wine shop, and offers group and private dining.[3] Meals by Genet’s official story identifies chef-founder Genet Agonafer’s Ethiopian background and says she opened the restaurant to present Ethiopian cuisine in an elevated yet familial setting.[4] Harold & Belle’s official history identifies its multigenerational family operation and its private dining and Peacock Lounge event space.[5] Bouka’s official site explicitly calls the restaurant Black-owned and advertises event hosting and catering.[6]

For Latino attribution, the Los Angeles Times article is expressly titled **“14 Latino-owned restaurants from the 101 Best Restaurants guide.”** It is the attribution source for Damian, Holbox, and Moo’s Craft Barbecue.[7] Opened official destinations then verified each candidate’s current customer-facing destination and numbered address. Damian identifies Chef Founder Enrique Olvera, a Mexican-rooted contemporary menu, an artisanal-spirit program, reservations, and private events.[8] Holbox’s official site verifies its Mercado La Paloma address and contact details; the Times describes its twice-weekly tasting menu, which should be checked for current availability.[7] Moo’s official site verifies its Lincoln Heights location and operating days, while the Times identifies Andrew and Michelle Muñoz and describes its Mexican-influenced craft-barbecue and draft-beer program.[7] [9]

For LGBTQ+ venue designation, the Eater LA directory is explicitly titled **“The Best Queer Bars and Nightclubs in Los Angeles.”** It supports the venue-level designation for Micky’s, Akbar, and Honey’s; Roosterfish’s own history calls its original location a gay bar and a safe haven for the LGBT community.[10] [11] The official sites then verified current customer-facing destinations, addresses, and, where provided, programming: Micky’s lists drag shows, drag brunches, Latin programs, dance parties, and happy hour; Akbar lists a DJ and event calendar; Honey’s provides late-night hours and its official Instagram contact.[12] [13] [14]

## Duplicate checks

Within the **Dining & Drinks** category, records were deduplicated by normalized business name, numbered street address, official domain, and venue type. There are **no exact duplicate candidate records**. Source overlap was retained only as corroboration, not as additional records: Discover Los Angeles and Black Owned Food LA both informed Black-attributed lead discovery; Eater and an official site both informed LGBTQ+ venue verification. Mercado La Paloma was not separately included because this category records the distinct restaurant Holbox rather than its host market. No alternate branch, pop-up, food truck, or event was added as a duplicate of a fixed-location candidate.

## Held leads and limitations

The held file contains six leads that were not promoted to candidates because they did not meet the required verification threshold. Alto’s official site displayed “COMING SOON,” creating a current-status conflict with the Los Angeles Times listing. Casa Gish Bac and Macheen had valid editorial evidence but no verified official customer-facing destination. Eagle LA was source-supported but its official site was blocked by a security page. Hi Tops Los Feliz had a source-supported address but its official customer page produced no extractable content. The Ruby Fruit’s official site says it is closed forever.

This is a targeted directory-research pass, not an exhaustive inventory of every restaurant, bar, or event venue in Los Angeles County. Websites, menus, event schedules, operating hours, reservation availability, alcohol-service terms, and business status can change. The records do not assert that a venue is “luxury” unless an opened source uses related wording; “special-occasion,” “chef-led,” “wine,” “cocktail,” and “event” search terms were selected from documented services or programming. Candidate inclusion is not an endorsement or suitability judgment.

## Attribution and scope rules applied

The terms **Black-owned**, **women-owned**, **Latino-owned**, **LGBTQ+ venue**, **gay bar**, **LGBTQIA bar**, and diaspora-origin descriptors were recorded only where an opened official site, explicit Black-owned directory, explicit Latino-owned directory, or explicit LGBTQ+ directory supported the specific wording. In particular, a cuisine, chef background, venue neighborhood, customer audience, or social-media presence was not treated as evidence of ownership or identity. “LGBTQ+ venue” is a source-attributed description of the business or its history, not an inference about its owners, employees, visitors, or any requester.

All candidate cities are within Los Angeles County according to the County’s city directory.[1] No coordinates were collected, generated, inferred, or stored.

## Operational boundary

This work is **research-only**. There was **no production database/API write, publication, map pin, geocode, deployment, native build, auth/user/session/waitlist/payment change**.
