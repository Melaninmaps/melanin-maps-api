# Mapping with Melanin — Allentown / Lehigh Valley: Beauty, Spa & Social Discovery

**Research date:** September 19, 2026
**Prepared by:** Manus AI

## Result

This research-only wave produced **32 candidate records** and **14 held records** for the Allentown/Lehigh Valley beauty, spa, wellness, food-and-drink, music, arts, and date-night/social discovery category. Candidate records are confined to Allentown, Bethlehem, Easton, or clearly Lehigh Valley locations, with a source-supported numbered address. Each candidate has an opened customer-facing official destination, including a business website, booking site, or public business social destination. No coordinates were added.

The highest-yield options are the **AABLC-verified Black-owned** salons, barbershops, natural-hair/loc businesses, food businesses, workshops, and cultural collectives. The local visitor directory adds public cultural and social anchors such as SteelStacks, Allentown Art Museum, and The Dime without implying ownership where the source does not state it.

## Evidence and source method

The **African American Business Leaders Council (AABLC)** describes its directory as a verified, maintained directory of Black-owned Lehigh Valley businesses. Its directory explains that listings must clear a seven-point check including verified Black ownership, active operation, location in the Greater Lehigh Valley, non-duplication, and a working clickable destination. The use of “Black-owned” in `ownershipDesignations` appears only where AABLC or the Chamber’s Black-owned list explicitly supports it. A public cultural or visitor destination is retained without an ownership designation unless an explicit designation was available.

The **Greater Lehigh Valley Chamber’s Black-Owned Business List** was opened as an additional source for addresses, category labels, phone numbers, and linked customer destinations. The Chamber describes that page as a **working list**, so it is not treated as an independent verification of activity. **Discover Lehigh Valley** directory pages were used for visitor-facing dining and cultural places. Official customer destinations were then opened for every candidate and used to limit `servicesSearchTerms` to literal, neutral terms supported by the destination.

## Dedupe and integrity checks

A deterministic within-category dedupe check was run on normalized **name + address** pairs for both JSONL files. The candidate set has no duplicate name/address pair. The two Board & Brush records are retained because their source-supported physical addresses are distinct. Wrap’d Tight is represented once at its verified Easton location; its Bethlehem location was not added as a second record. Every candidate has a numbered physical address, except none; therefore no coordinate or approximate-location substitution was needed. `sourceRow` is consecutive from 1 in each file, and all values use the required 23 fields in the specified order.

## Held leads and limitations

The held file preserves **14 leads** rather than converting weak evidence into candidate claims. Reasons include parked/expired or generic official destinations, a customer page labelled unofficial, a non-live location notice conflicting with a current directory listing, absence of a numbered address, an unconfirmed recent relocation, or a social-only destination not opened in this pass. The availability, hours, pricing, accessibility, licenses, quality, and safety of all candidates remain unverified unless explicitly surfaced in the cited destination; they are not inferred by these records.

Ownership evidence is source-attributed and deliberately narrow. “Black-owned” appears only where the AABLC directory or Chamber’s Black-owned list says so. The AABLC’s verification description is source context, not an inference about any entity outside that directory. The Allentown Art Museum and The Dime have null ownership fields. SteelStacks’ nonprofit-operated designation is attributed only to its official statement about ArtsQuest.
