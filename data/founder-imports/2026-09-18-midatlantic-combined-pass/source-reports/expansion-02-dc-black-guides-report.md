# Washington, DC Black Business Guides — Evidence Report

## Scope and method
This pass reviewed credible public guides and official local-organization pages for Washington, DC Black-owned businesses and support resources, prioritizing everyday services and retail beyond restaurants. The primary commercial source was [Destination DC’s Black-Owned Businesses to Support in Washington, DC](https://washington.org/visit-dc/black-owned-business), a customer-facing guide that supplies business descriptions, ownership framing, street addresses for listed physical locations, and official websites. The organizational source was the [DC Department of Small and Local Business Development (DSLBD) National Black Business Month page](https://dslbd.dc.gov/nationalblackbusinessmonth), an official DC government page. GWU’s [Black-Owned Businesses in the DMV Area](https://diversity.gwu.edu/black-owned-businesses-dmv-area) was used as a discovery cross-check; it links to guides and directories but generally does not provide enough listing-level address and first-party destination evidence for direct imports.

## Results
The candidate file contains **12 records**: **11 physical commercial candidates** and **1 community resource**. Categories include florist (2), retail (5), tailoring/clothing (1), fitness (2), beauty/spa (1, classified `regulated_review` because the guide explicitly mentions facials and massage), culinary education (1), and government small-business support (1). Restaurants were intentionally not the focus, though the source guide also contains restaurant entries that were excluded from this pass.

All commercial records have a published street address in Washington, DC and a customer-facing official website. Phone numbers and social URLs were left null where the reviewed source pages did not publish or verify them. No ownership, services, licensing, hours, accessibility, or availability was inferred beyond the cited guide wording.

## Source and evidence notes
Destination DC explicitly frames the article as Black-owned businesses and provides business-level descriptions. Specific evidence was retained in each JSONL record, including statements about Lee’s Flower Shop being Black-owned and family-run, Anika Hobbs founding Nubian Hueman, Angel Anderson’s Spice Suite, Fia Thomas’ consignment shop, Chris Perrin’s Cut Seven, Angela Jennings and Reggie Smith’s Boombox Boxing Club, and Meka Mathis’ Skin Beauty Bar. For entries where the guide gives less biographical detail, the article-level Black-owned framing is recorded rather than adding unsupported identity claims.

DSLBD’s page is an official government resource, not a customer directory. It lists support programs and organizations including WACIF, Anacostia Arts Center, BlackBone Project, Greater Washington Black Chamber of Commerce, and Black Business Association of Washington, D.C. The page provides DSLBD’s own street address and phone, so DSLBD is included as a community resource; the other organizations were not imported because this pass did not verify a complete street address plus customer-facing destination for each.

## Exclusions and gaps
The GWU page links to Supporting Black Owned, Black-Owned Business Network DC, DC Shop Small, and other resources, but those pages are directory/guide-level discovery sources rather than sufficient evidence for every individual listing. DC Shop Small’s page contains a long list of Black-owned brands and official links, but generally lacks DC street addresses; those online-only or location-ambiguous entries were not converted into map pins. Destination DC entries without a DC street address (for example, Bailiwick Clothing, Black Pepper Paperie Co., and Black Broadway on U) were excluded from the physical candidate file. MahoganyBooks and ZAAF Collection were excluded because the cited locations are in Oxon Hill, Maryland, not Washington, DC.

Accessibility limitations: the sources do not systematically state wheelchair access, sensory conditions, parking, transit access, language availability, hours, appointment requirements, or current operating status. These fields remain unclaimed. The Destination DC page uses map links for several addresses and may reflect editorial publication timing; current status should be rechecked before publication.
