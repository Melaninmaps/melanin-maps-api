## Evidence and limitations

The primary identity evidence came from first-party pages where an organization expressly described itself (for example, Black woman-owned, African American law firm, Ethiopian/Tanzanian/Latin-focused) and from public editorial/municipal lists explicitly framed as Black-owned or Latino-owned business guides. The latter evidence is preserved in each record’s `ownershipEvidence`; **chamber membership was not used as proof of ownership.** First-party business pages were used for official customer-facing destinations, physical street addresses, public contact details, and plainly stated categories.

This is a bounded, time-limited pass rather than a comprehensive census. Records were not retained where the reviewed source lacked a numbered physical address for an evidently physical business, where a purported official domain was parked, or where the required community/identity designation was unsupported. The held file documents those cases. Search snippets, customer reviews, and unverified third-party claims were not used to invent ownership, language ability, licensing, accessibility, quality, hours, inventory, availability, or services. Where a site separately publishes a P.O. Box, that was not treated as a physical address.

**No production database/API was modified or called, no account was used, and no production publication occurred.**
