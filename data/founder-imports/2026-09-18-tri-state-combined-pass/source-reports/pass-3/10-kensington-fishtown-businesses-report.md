# Philadelphia Deep-Dive Pass 3: Kensington and Fishtown Businesses

## Scope and method

This pass was limited to Philadelphia-area neighborhood and local business guides covering Kensington, Fishtown, Port Richmond, Olde Kensington, and nearby North Philadelphia/River Wards corridors. The primary ownership evidence came from Visit Philadelphia's curated Black-owned, Latino-owned, and Latino-owned restaurant/shop guides. The New Kensington Community Development Corporation (NKCDC) Business/Community Directory was used as a neighborhood discovery source. A Philadelphia Neighborhoods article about Port Richmond was reviewed for local context and addresses.

Candidates were retained only when a guide supplied a business-specific customer destination and the published material supported a Black, Latino, Puerto Rican, Dominican, Colombian, Ecuadorian, or related diaspora designation. Where a source supplied a precise address and official destination, the record is importable as a physical business. One NKCDC feature is marked `manual_review` because the accessible directory excerpt supplied a customer website but not an address or ownership evidence.

## Evidence notes

Visit Philadelphia explicitly identifies Bean2Bean as a multigenerational Puerto Rican family operation, gives the Olde Kensington cafe address, and links the official brand website. Its Latino-owned shops guide identifies Louis Quiles and his Puerto Rico-meets-Philly heritage for Encanto Kicks & Coffee, identifies Ecuador-born founder Mike Andino for Estilo Dance Studio, and names Radhi and Mónica Fernández as FAIYA founders. The same guide describes Taller Puertorriqueño's Puerto Rican culture and Latino arts mission and the Julia de Burgos bilingual bookstore at 2600 N. 5th Street; this is classified as `cultural_place` rather than an ordinary business.

Visit Philadelphia's Black-owned shops guide identifies Harriett's Bookshop as the Fishtown bookstore founded and owned by Jeannine A. Cook. It lists American Grammar as a Fishtown shop in the Black-owned guide and provides the business Instagram as its customer destination. Because the accessible guide does not provide a separate domain for American Grammar, the Instagram URL is retained as the official customer-facing destination without inventing additional ownership detail.

Visit Philadelphia's Latino-owned restaurants guide identifies Colombian chef-partners Giselle Poveda and Daniela Garcia for Café Tinto and gives the Fishtown location at 1701 N. Front Street. It identifies Dominican chef Jose Luis for Alta Cocina and lists the 2460 N. 5th Street location. It also identifies chef Frankie Ramirez and his Mexican restaurant Amá at 101 W. Oxford Street; the record preserves the guide's classification while avoiding an unsupported claim about legal ownership structure.

## Source accessibility and limitations

The Visit Philadelphia pages were accessible and supplied detailed narrative evidence, addresses, and business-specific websites or social destinations. Some location links embedded in the guides were Google Maps links; these were used only as the guide's exact listing evidence, not as the candidate's customer destination. Customer destinations in the JSONL are official business domains or guide-linked business social accounts.

The NKCDC directory was accessible and confirms that its coverage area includes Kensington, Port Richmond, and Fishtown. Its directory page contains many listings, but most entries do not publish ownership identity. The featured Lua Vietnamese Restaurant entry includes an official website but the accessible excerpt does not expose a physical address or ownership evidence; it is therefore retained only as `manual_review`, with no map-ready address. NKCDC directory social links belong to NKCDC and were not used as business customer destinations.

The Philadelphia Neighborhoods Port Richmond article was accessible and supplied addresses for Super Delicatessen, Lure Salon, Mercer Café, Tony's Pizzeria, and The Thrifty Irishman. The article describes Polish and Irish community context but does not establish Black, Latino, or diaspora ownership for the requested inclusion standard. Those businesses were excluded rather than inferred into the candidate file.

## Exclusions

The following categories were excluded: NKCDC listings that had no explicit Black/Latino/diaspora ownership evidence; businesses whose only available URL was a generic directory, chamber profile, Google page, or directory-owned social account; physical businesses lacking both a street address and a business-specific customer destination; and Port Richmond businesses from the Philadelphia Neighborhoods article where the article documented Polish or Irish community context but not qualifying ownership evidence. No ownership, language, services, hours, accessibility, licensure, insurance, or availability was inferred beyond the cited published text.
