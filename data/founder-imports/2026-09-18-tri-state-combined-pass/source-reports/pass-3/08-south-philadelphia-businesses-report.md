# South Philadelphia Businesses: Evidence Report

## Scope and conclusion

This pass researched South Philadelphia Black, Latino, and diaspora consumer businesses and service sources, with emphasis on South Street and the South 9th Street/Italian Market corridor. Ten records were retained in the companion JSONL file. Seven are physical businesses from the South Street Black-owned-business guide, two are South 9th Street Latino restaurants from Visit Philadelphia's South Philly Barbacoa/Casa México guide, and one is a community resource representing the Association of Mexican Business Owners of Philadelphia (AEM / AMBOP).

Ownership designations are reproduced only where a source explicitly states them. The South Street guide identifies YOWIE, Moore Vintage Archive, Black and Nobel, and Atomic City Comics as Black-owned. It identifies South Street Art Mart as queer-owned and artist-run. Marsha's is described as an all-women's sports bar, and Loomen Labs is described through its founder and services without a racial or diaspora ownership claim. The report therefore does not infer Black, Latino, or diaspora ownership for those two businesses. Casa México and South Philly Barbacoa are designated Latino-owned based on Visit Philadelphia's Latino-focused coverage and its published connection to chef Cristina Martinez; no additional identity attributes are inferred.

## Source findings

The principal source was [YOWIE's South Street guide][1], which provides exact street addresses and descriptions for seven customer-facing businesses. For inclusion, each physical record also has a business-specific customer destination rather than a generic map, directory, or chamber page. Official customer destinations were used where available: YOWIE Shop, Moore Vintage Archive, South Street Art Mart, Black and Nobel, Atomic City Comics, Marsha's, and Loomen Labs. The guide itself supplies the exact listing context and addresses; business pages and social profiles were used only to preserve customer destinations and corroborate details such as phone numbers.

Visit Philadelphia's guide to [South Philly Barbacoa and Casa México][2] supplied the South 9th Street restaurant records, including Casa México's 1134 S. 9th Street address and phone. South Philly Barbacoa's 1140 S. 9th Street address and phone were corroborated from the chef's customer-facing site and menu PDF. The broader [Latino Philadelphia page][3] places these restaurants within Philadelphia's Latin culinary scene and identifies South 9th Street's Italian Market as a Latino cultural hub. The records are classified as ordinary businesses rather than regulated services.

The [Sabores of South Philly campaign][4] is a useful corridor source because it states that AEM / AMBOP created the campaign to amplify Latino-owned businesses along the 9th Street corridor and surrounding streets. Text extraction exposed the campaign context and social links but returned “0 businesses” for its dynamic directory. No participant names, addresses, or ownership claims were invented from that incomplete rendering. AEM / AMBOP is retained as a community resource, not as a physical commercial candidate.

## Accessibility limits and exclusions

The campaign directory was partially inaccessible to text extraction because its participant cards and map content are dynamically rendered. This is documented rather than treated as evidence for individual listings. The Impacto Media article on Fiesta on 9th Street [5] confirms the corridor's Latino-commerce context and event geography, but it does not name individual merchants with business-specific customer destinations, so it was not used to fabricate additional candidate rows.

The pass did not use generic Google pages, directory home pages, chamber accounts, personal LinkedIn profiles, or shared directory social accounts as business destinations. No houses of worship were identified in the reviewed sources. No health, legal, finance, childcare, or similarly regulated-service candidates were retained. No claims were made about hours, language, accessibility, licensing, insurance, availability, or services beyond published evidence.

## Candidate inventory

The JSONL file contains ten sequential records. Physical businesses have a street address and a business-specific website or social URL. The AEM / AMBOP record is a community resource with no fabricated map pin; its address is null because the campaign source does not publish a physical customer address for the association.
