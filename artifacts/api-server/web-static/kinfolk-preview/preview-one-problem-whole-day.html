A photo. A warning.
A safer next question.

The visitor taps a dashboard photo, asks a human question, and sees Kinfolk do the most important thing first: distinguish what it can explain from what deserves a careful, professional response.

The preview explicitly avoids diagnosing the car or telling someone it is safe to drive. A flashing warning is treated as a reason to pause and consider roadside help.

A car question becomes
a money question.

Kinfolk retains the warning-light context but does not pretend it knows the repair bill. It makes the estimate legible: get the diagnostic finding, ask what is essential, and know what to confirm before work begins.

Costs are intentionally not fabricated. The demonstration shows a responsible estimate-explainer until sourced local pricing or provider data is available.
