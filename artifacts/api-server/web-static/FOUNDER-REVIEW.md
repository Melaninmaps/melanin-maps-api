# Founder Review — Cinematic Website Direction

## The decision this prototype makes

The public-facing website should not explain Mapping With Melanin as a series of tabs, features, or a map next to a chatbot. It should let someone feel a connected world through a slow, reader-controlled visual story.

> **Mapping With Melanin is the place where community, culture, business, knowledge, and care can meet. Kinfolk is the companion that helps a person find their way through it.**

This is deliberately different from future mobile onboarding. The website has room to create belief; the app should later get a new member moving quickly.

## What MWM and Kinfolk are

| Product | Core role | Avoid framing it as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mapping With Melanin | A living ecosystem for belonging, everyday life, culture, community knowledge, business relationships, and shared care. | A map app, directory, tourism product, or feature bundle. |
| KinfolkAI | A thoughtful companion that helps someone name what they need, understand the relevant context, and find a meaningful next step. | A generic chatbot, search bar, or a bolt-on assistant. |

## Cinematic screen sequence

| Scene | Visitor feeling | Story being told |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Opening** | Recognition | “Map your life. Connect deeper.” The site begins with a life, not a feature. |
| **Everyday life** | Relief | A person has a real need: care, work, belonging, home, or a place to gather. |
| **Kinfolk** | Being understood | Kinfolk helps make the need smaller and more actionable without stripping away context. |
| **Kinfolk practical journey** | Immediate usefulness | A member chooses whether Kinfolk remembers a private trip note, asks “I’m heading to Phoenix for business—what should I know?”, then opens a context-rich (intentionally blurred) business-profile preview. |
| **Community knowledge** | Trust | A useful recommendation carries lived experience, memory, and the people behind it. |
| **Business belongs in the story** | Reciprocity | An owner is not buying a pin; they are becoming visible inside a trusted community relationship. |
| **The owner’s side** | Dignity | An owner can be known through story, identity, access needs, community voice, and growth goals—not only what they sell. |
| **Owner practical journey** | Agency | An owner moves through a Kinfolk-assisted proposal concept, a promotion concept, and a verified-owner Broadcasts review moment before anything travels. |
| **Shared care** | Grounded safety | Safety is familiarity, relationships, context, and collective care—not a fear-driven feature. |
| **Knowledge carried forward** | Possibility | Lessons and relationships return to the community so the next person does not begin alone. |
| **Invitation** | Belonging | The visitor sees a world they may want to join. |

## Business-owner tier answer

The source includes both business membership mapping (`biz_free → Explorer`, `growth_business → Community Builder`, and `premium_business` / `founding_business → Legacy Member`) and a mobile `business-guide` implementation with differentiated tier labels and feature language. It does **not** establish that this matrix has been finalized or founder-approved for public marketing, so this preview does not publish it as a pricing, entitlement, or outcome promise.

The source supports an owner experience that includes profile and identity management, links and hours, governed community feedback and reviews, events, messaging and participation surfaces, content and category-aware discovery, selected analytics and click information, and Kinfolk-assisted improvement planning. Analytics access has membership gates in the implementation, but that is not enough to make a public tier promise. This website therefore tells the owner story without publishing a made-up benefit table, price, or outcome.

### Required accuracy boundaries

| Topic | Public-preview rule |
| --- | --- |
| Claiming a business | A claim grants ownership control; it does not verify an ownership designation or issue a verified badge. |
| Community experience | Feedback must remain governed and context-aware; it is not proof of verification. |
| Owner analytics | Avoid invented numbers or eligibility promises. Access and membership rules must be finalized before marketing them. |
| Kinfolk business support | Show a direction toward practical, contextual help; do not guarantee grants, providers, revenue, or results. |
| Safety | Do not claim emergency alerts, automated incident notifications, rerouting, trusted contacts, or release-pending functions are active. |

## Visual and interaction direction

Five original cinematic assets support distinct moments: everyday life, Kinfolk, community knowledge, business ownership, and shared care. The page uses full-viewport scenes, large editorial type, muted amber and sage illumination, a minimal story rail, and a progress line.

The interaction is the reader’s scroll. Chapters activate as they enter view, copy and photography settle slowly, and chapter anchors let visitors revisit a moment. The site never auto-advances. Reduced-motion preferences disable the transitions and parallax.

Three source-informed “app in motion” screens now make the product tangible inside the story: a Kinfolk business search, a contextual Community post, and a business-admin owner flow. They use proportionate portrait devices and staged, scroll-triggered reveals so the visitor sees a person searching, receiving contextual guidance, reading community context, preparing a response, and being prompted to create a business update.

Two larger three-screen sequences now carry the immediate-use case. The **member journey** is “remember → ask → find”: a private continuity decision, a business-trip question, and an intentionally blurred profile that exposes the kind of context a member can use. The **owner journey** is “plan → promote → review”: a proposed Kinfolk proposal helper, a proposed promotion helper, and a current-source Broadcasts-style review state. These stage buttons are intentional reader controls; each new screen reveals in place rather than auto-advancing the page.

### Current-app visual distinction

These are **not exported screenshots of the current native app**. They are editable website mockups grounded in inspected mobile/API source patterns. The current sources support Kinfolk’s public-listing recommendation treatment, Community post cards, a moderated business-response form that collects a response statement/corrective actions/trust plan, and a verified-owner Broadcasts compose flow. The Kinfolk draft helper inside the response screen is expressly marked as a **proposed direction**, not a released feature. Every screen remains illustrative rather than a live account, data feed, listing, entitlement, or Safety action.

## One problem, whole day — Kinfolk demonstration

The additional standalone preview proves the same product idea in a single relatable morning: **“My check-engine light came on, and I still need to get to work.”** The viewer actively progresses through five connected app states rather than watching a feature reel:

1. **Understand:** a dashboard-photo review and a safety-oriented flashing-light follow-up.
2. **Evaluate money:** a diagnostic / estimate explainer and questions to ask before authorizing work.
3. **Find support:** preference-aware mechanic discovery, with a clearly illustrative directory card and map.
4. **Keep the workday moving:** a repair-stop-to-work route-comparison handoff, visibly labelled as an integration preview.
5. **Carry on:** a local-only employer-message draft and nearby-breakfast continuation that retain the full morning context.

### Scenes 3–4: approved-memory revision

The mechanic screen now carries **clearly fictional, member-approved preview data**—Home and Work as saved places, a Black-owned-business preference, and a 2018 Honda Civic—without asking the member to repeat it. The two mechanic cards are equally explicit fictional directory records: their ownership-verification and Honda-service fields demonstrate what verified live data would look like rather than claiming a real business, verification, service, availability, or appointment.

After a demo mechanic is selected, the transportation screen proactively carries that selected shop forward. It uses a clearly fictional authorized 9:00 AM calendar call and saved Work location to frame the next decision. Bus/rail and rideshare are shown as the next comparison surfaces, not as live results; the arrival warning makes the non-negotiable behavior explicit: if real routing cannot reach Work before 9:00 AM, Kinfolk says so and offers alternatives instead of promising an impossible arrival.

The preview includes a tappable dashboard-photo review, flashing-light response, five reader-controlled progression states, a local-only draft-copy acknowledgement, and a restart. It never diagnoses a vehicle, invents a business/verification/availability, returns a live route or fare, or sends a message. The [guided demo and capability report](ONE-PROBLEM-GUIDED-DEMO.md) documents the 75-second presentation sequence, product/simulation distinctions, and integrations needed for a live experience.

### Safety & Peace of Mind

The standalone Safety story has five reader-controlled phone states: fictional meetup context; a public alternative with no invented rating; a member-chosen details-only or temporary-location check-in; a report draft that makes privacy and moderation boundaries visible; and a job-exploration handoff that separates employer facts from individual experiences. It also defines the separate consent boundary for serious credible threats, routine police/ICE notifications, and missed check-ins.

All places, contacts, reports, alerts, employers, and experiences are fictional preview states. No safety action is sent, published, or activated. The [suite capability-status report](KINFOLK-PREVIEW-SUITE-STATUS.md) records the featured capability, live-service, and consent boundaries for every story.

### Universal “hold the phone” interaction update

The suite now uses the same interaction principle in every story: the reader controls the editorial scroll **and** can push the visible next-step controls inside the phone. The existing Everyday Life car journey and Safety story already used this pattern; it is now extended to Travel and Business Owners.

The Atlanta story adds two distinct appointment moments without implying a live transaction: a member selects a fictional hair-planning option **before departure**, then, after arrival, selects a fictional emergency nail option when the original appointment falls through. Each choice leads into the next app state, but no business service, hours, cost, availability, booking, hold, payment, or confirmation is claimed.

The owner story now lets a viewer move from the existing dashboard through a selectable planning priority, a creator post nudge, a community-loop menu idea, moderated response preview, member profile, and restart—inside the device rather than from scroll controls alone. These actions remain local simulation states; they do not create a plan, upload video, publish a caption or promotion, create a menu, submit a response, or modify an owner profile.

### Travel cards and the owner creator loop

The Atlanta story replaces abstract hair and nail slots with tap-to-open business cards. Before departure, the member asks for **braids** and sees Crown & Coil Braiding Studio and Rooted Braid Bar. After landing, the member says **“I broke my nail when I got to Atlanta. I need to get it fixed,”** and Kinfolk identifies Peach City Nail Room near the hotel, with an alternate card. Each card carries a **Verified Minority-Owned** treatment and opens a compact in-app profile pop-out—not a website. Its information is expressly a demonstration only: it does not assert a live business, verification, hotel distance, service, availability, price, or booking.

The owner’s post sequence now starts with **“Your supporters are online.”** It presents a video-preview surface and three selectable captions, then moves to the community loop: **“Community members have been searching for happy hour near you. Would you like to create a menu for them?”** The menu-draft acknowledgement is a local interaction only. No upload, caption, community signal, menu, promotion, analytics claim, response, or publication is created.

### Explore Your Way and suite navigation

The fifth story makes a different product point: Kinfolk should not be a gatekeeper for ordinary use. A miniature, direct-use Mapping With Melanin app now exposes clickable **Map**, **Discover**, **Events**, **Community**, and **Resources** routes, each with a detail surface and in-app back arrow. It demonstrates six short everyday needs—nearby food, a vibe search, weekend events, community conversation, housing, and healthcare—using complete preview pages rather than generic placeholder labels.

**Mapping With Melanin** is the default profile in Map and Discover. Its profile carries the supplied concept signals **“342 community members said it’s a vibe”** and **“657 said worth the wait,”** plus interactive About, Community, and Updates tabs. Those signals are deliberately documented as supplied preview content—not live analytics, a rating, verification, or an independently measured claim. The Events route is now filterable by **This Weekend, Culture, Community, Networking, Family,** and **Food & Drink**; each category produces different event cards, an event-detail page, and a local-only RSVP acknowledgement.

Every suite experience now carries a persistent top experience bar: **← All Experiences**, five clickable numbered titles, and a highlighted current story. The combined Travel/Business page keeps both internal story anchors. This makes the suite navigable as a set, while each app still has its own contextual in-phone back behavior.

### Mapping With Melanin brand-aligned front page

The Explore app shell now carries the established Mapping With Melanin visual hierarchy: **espresso `#3A1F0E`** for primary action and high-emphasis brand treatment, **cream `#FAF6EF`** for the app canvas, **gold `#CA922B`** for selected/progress states, and **community green `#2D7A4F`** for supportive utility. Kinfolk’s smaller map and contextual accents remain deliberately varied; the app is no longer visually led by a generic Kinfolk-only palette.

The current native source does include the actual Mapping With Melanin logo assets. The suite, Explore surface, and Kinfolk app moments now use those assets directly—never a fabricated letter mark or replacement wordmark. The responsive review route `preview-mwm-brand-front-page.html` places the revised app shell inside a desktop/mobile-ready front page. It is a local review route only—nothing is published or deployed.
