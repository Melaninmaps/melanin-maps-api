01 · The opportunity

A new job can feel
like a new possibility.

Kinfolk starts with the real question—not a rating: what is known, what is not, and what choices would help the member decide how they want to meet.

The location, nearby places, and community context are fictional preview inputs. The design never invents an incident, a safety score, or a live alert.
02 · Plan the meetup

A better setting
is a practical choice.

Instead of treating safety as a binary verdict, Kinfolk helps someone choose a public alternative with the conditions they care about: people, staffing, timing, and a viable way home.

Each place is a fictional record. Live hours, transit, and public-location details must come from supported sources; none is inferred here.
03 · Choose trusted support

Your safety plan
still belongs to you.

A trusted contact is not an automatic surveillance feature. The member chooses whom to notify, what to share, whether location is included, when it ends, and what permissions govern a missed check-in.

This is a local-only consent demonstration. No contact, location, scheduled check-in, or escalation is created or sent.
04 · Tell the community

Privacy does not mean
hiding the choices.

Kinfolk can help someone prepare a report while keeping the line visible: a member reviews what may be shared, and a moderation workflow—not a frictionless auto-post—comes next.

The preview makes no absolute anonymity promise and sends no report. Privacy notice, technical safeguards, and moderation are live-product requirements.
05 · Keep opportunity moving

The experience is not
the end of the story.

A freelance meeting can lead naturally to a larger work question. Kinfolk moves toward employer transparency and job exploration without blurring verified employer facts into individual community reports.

Serious-threat contact preferences, routine local notifications, and missed-check-in escalation are distinct consent workflows—never one automatic action.
